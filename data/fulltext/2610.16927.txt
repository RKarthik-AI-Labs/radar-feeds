Abstract.

Short-video search needs compact query rewriters that recover users’ personalized intent from ambiguous queries under strict latency constraints. Existing LLM-based methods have improved rewriting by using synthetic reasoning and subsequent consumption-behavioral feedback. However, overemphasizing subsequent consumption can favor engaging content over the user’s immediate need, causing intent drift even when rewritten queries are grounded in behavioral history.
We propose WeFair,

A

gnment from Human Feedback for Pe

WeFair uses human feedback according to how directly it reflects personalized intent, assigning feedback fidelity to a progression from demonstration to preference to verification. We first mine actual queries and user reformulations from search sessions, and then screen them for contextual support. In the demonstration stage, a supervised fine-tuning curriculum progresses from large-scale context-to-query learning to reasoning and direct prediction on human reformulation and preservation. In the preference stage, direct preference optimization learns relative preferences from subsequent consumption feedback. In the verification stage, group relative policy optimization rewards exact agreement with reformulation or preservation targets to constrain intent drift. Further analysis shows that broad supervision benefits the full curriculum supervised training, while strict verification achieves better performance than the tested auxiliary rewards. In offline experiments, the compact WeFair-2B model outperforms strong rewriting baselines in overall accuracy and intent preservation. Furthermore, a 10-day online A/B test shows relative increases of 2.951% in click-through rate and 2.593% in effective video-view rate, alongside a 2.350% relative reduction in query reformulation rate. WeFair-2B has been deployed in the WeChat short-video search system, which serves over 100 million queries per day.

1. 
Introduction

Personalized query rewriting is essential when an ambiguous search query does not fully express the user’s immediate information need. In short-video search, the intended meaning may depend on recent searches, viewed content, or other context available when the query is issued. Rewriting can expose this information to retrieval by producing a more informative expression, but it must also satisfy strict latency constraints 
(
Gong et al., 2026
; 
Chu et al., 2026
)
. Therefore, the practical objective is training a compact rewrite model that can resolve ambiguity without replacing the user’s current intent and needs. Furthermore, the rewrite pipeline also needs to accurately decide when to rewrite or preserve the original query.

Although general large-size large language models (LLMs) exhibit strong reasoning capabilities, they fail to accurately understand diverse user intents and incur excessive per-inference latency, which is unacceptable for real-time search.
To address these limitations, some approaches use synthetic Chain-of-Thought(CoT) traces to distill reasoning capabilities from large teacher LLMs into smaller rewrite models, and then use large-scale subsequent consumption feedback to align them with platform-specific user preferences.

However, consumption-oriented alignment does not necessarily reflect the user’s immediate information need. When insufficiently constrained by direct evidence of user intent, a rewriter may favor queries that retrieve engaging content while redirecting the original search intent, resulting in intent drift. Figure 
1
 shows an example of intent drift.
To better capture personalized intent, recent methods incorporate actual user reformulations, which provide more direct evidence of how users clarify their information needs. PicQue
(
Chu et al., 2026
)
 uses historical reformulations as context for generating training examples that subsequently undergo manual verification, while WeWrite
(
Cheng et al., 2026
)
 directly uses mined reformulations for supervised fine-tuning and reward construction in GRPO. While these advances demonstrate the value of real user reformulations, the subsequent consumption feedback differs in how directly they reflect personalized intent, and its fine-grained use across training stages remains underexplored.
To bridge the gap, we propose WeFair, a fidelity-aware alignment framework for training a compact personalized query rewriter model in WeChat short-video search. Its central idea is to match supervision fidelity to the requirements of each training stage, improving how human feedback guides a small model toward faithful and efficient rewriting. This progression can enable a compact model to acquire personalized query rewriting capabilities while mitigating intent drift.

WeFair combines automated data construction with three complementary training stages. We mine actual reformulations from search sessions and apply LLM screening and contextual verification to retain demonstrations grounded in user intent. Training then proceeds through three stages, each with a distinct purpose. In the demonstration stage, a supervised fine-tuning (SFT) curriculum progresses from context-to-query prediction to context-conditioned reasoning and finally direct query rewriting, building solid intent understanding capability. The broad coverage of human demonstrations prepares the compact policy model to generate useful candidates for later optimization. In the preference stage, direct preference optimization (DPO) learns from candidate pairs distinguished by subsequent consumption outcomes, increasing the probability of rewrites associated with better personalized user intent. Finally, group relative policy optimization (GRPO) applies a strict verification reward based on exact match with actual human reformulations or preservation. This final stage anchors policy updates to observed human expressions without adding graded similarity, retrieval, or other judge rewards. At inference time, the rewrite model directly outputs a short rewrite or a preservation decision, avoiding additional CoT generation latency. Figure 
2
 shows the overview of the proposed WeFair.

Our further analysis suggests that the three training stages differ in their tolerance for supervision with varying intent fidelity. SFT can tolerate lower-fidelity supervision to acquire broad rewriting capabilities, while DPO can incorporate preferences inferred from subsequent consumption to refine candidate selection. In contrast, final GRPO optimization requires strict fidelity to the intended target. Auxiliary rewards may involve more fine-grained supervision but reduce rewriting accuracy. Offline and online experiments demonstrate WeFair’s effectiveness in personalized rewriting. WeFair-2B rewrite model has been
deployed in the online WeChat short-video search system, serving over 100 million queries per day.
Our main contributions can be summarized as follows:

(1)

We examine the role of supervision fidelity in personalized rewriting post-training. Demonstrate that extensive broad lower-fidelity supervision benefits curriculum SFT, while strict verification better supports on-policy alignment.

(2)

We propose WeFair, a fidelity-aware framework that matches human feedback to demonstration, preference, and verification stages, enabling compact models to capture personalized needs while mitigating intent drift.

(3)

Offline and online experiments demonstrate that WeFair achieves strong rewriting performance with compact models and significant improvements in online WeChat short-video search.

2. 
Related Work

2.1. 
Personalized Query Rewriting

LLM-based query rewriting improves retrieval by enriching query representations and adapting to search domains. Query2doc generates pseudo-documents, while CardRewriter incorporates platform knowledge through knowledge cards 
(
Wang et al., 2023
; 
Gong et al., 2026
)
. Domain adaptation further aligns query expressions with retrieval requirements. BEQUE combines supervised rewriting with retrieval feedback
(
Peng et al., 2024
)
, and negation-aware rewriting preserves explicit constraints 
(
Guo et al., 2025b
)
. MAAQR and QP-OneModel extend this direction through adaptive refinement and unified query understanding 
(
Zheng et al., 2025
; 
Huang et al., 2026
)
. Beyond representation enrichment, IterQR and Think-Then-Rewrite incorporate reasoning into retrieval-oriented training
(
Chen et al., 2025
; 
Li et al., 2026a
)
. MiniELM combines lightweight distillation with simulated feedback 
(
Nguyen et al., 2025
)
. For personalized search, user context and observed behavior provide evidence of the user’s current information need, helping resolve ambiguity and determine when to rewrite. PicQue combines hybrid reasoning supervision with GRPO to address over-rewriting 
(
Chu et al., 2026
)
, while Think-to-Personalize uses purchase history to support personalized retrieval 
(
Jiang et al., 2026
)
. WeWrite mines actual user reformulations and trains with SFT and GRPO using lexical similarity and conditional query-volume rewards 
(
Cheng et al., 2026
)
.

Furthermore, OneBar emphasizes content-grounded recommendations, while short-video and conversational query suggestion address consumption utility and intent coverage 
(
Tang et al., 2026
; 
Tian et al., 2026
; 
Liu et al., 2026
)
. DVCQR aligns separate rewrites with sparse and dense retrieval 
(
Li et al., 2026b
)
. These studies demonstrate the value of contextual and behavioral signals for query rewriting, while highlighting distinct objectives such as retrieval relevance, consumption utility, and personalized intent.

2.2. 
LLM Post-Training and Feedback Alignment

Post-training methods provide different mechanisms for learning from demonstrations, preference pairs, and verified rewards. SFT learns demonstrated responses, whereas DPO optimizes chosen–rejected preferences without separately training a reward model, and GRPO estimates advantages within sampled groups 
(
Guo et al., 2025a
; 
Ren et al., 2026
; 
Rafailov et al., 2023
; 
Ouyang et al., 2022
)
. Beyond algorithm selection, recent work examines which supervision is useful at different learning stages. Stage-specific data selection distinguishes capability acquisition from reinforcement 
(
He et al., 2026
)
. SmartSearch uses a process-reward-guided curriculum 
(
Wen et al., 2026a
)
. Hindsight self-distillation further derives corrections and preferences from follow-up user interactions 
(
Kleine Buening et al., 2026
)
. This work motivates matching supervision to both the policy’s capabilities and the evidence available from users.
In search applications, AWHCP combines SFT, DPO, and GRPO with quality, format, and recall rewards 
(
Ou et al., 2026
)
, while CoRe uses deployed relevance feedback 
(
Wen et al., 2026b
)
. Such signals align generation with search outcomes, but provide different evidence about intended reformulations. Comparisons under compute constraints further show that on-policy rewriting need not outperform prompted alternatives 
(
Xu et al., 2026
)
, motivating examination of the optimization signals alongside the algorithms.

On-policy optimization consequently requires considering the reliability of candidate-level feedback. TTRL shows that noisy pseudo-labels can still yield reliable rewards 
(
Zuo et al., 2025
)
. This motivates prioritizing reward purity in on-policy preference alignment.

Framework Overview.

WeFair matches supervision fidelity to the requirements of each training stage. We utilize context-to-query records, user reformulations, and subsequent consumption feedback from search sessions. These signals capture a broad range of user preferences but differ in how directly they reflect personalization and immediate intent. Accordingly, curriculum SFT builds rewriting capabilities from broad demonstrations, DPO learns relative preferences from consumption-grounded pairs, and GRPO anchors final refinement through strict agreement with screened reformulation and preservation targets.

We mine and screen search-session records to construct three supervision pools: a context-to-query pool

Experiment

4.1. 
Experimental Setup

Offline evaluation.

We trained our method on four weeks of search logs and evaluated all methods on the test set from the following three days. Let 
 and 
 denote the numbers of rewrite-required and preservation-required examples in the test set. During the evaluation, all the evaluated rewriters use the original input query and query-time context. We use DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash as the LLM judge, which additionally considers preceding and subsequent user behavior to assess whether a generated query is consistent with the information need expressed by the user’s actual subsequent reformulation. To test the effectiveness of the judge, we randomly sampled 500 responses of Gemini-3.1-Pro rewriter from the test set and evaluated each response using both the LLM judge and human review
(
Chu et al., 2026
)
. The LLM judge showed 96.6% agreement with human judgments, with Cohen’s Kappa = 0.919.

Baselines and implementation.
 For general LLM baselines, we evaluate DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash, Gemini-3.1-Pro, and Qwen3.8-27B. Furthermore, we reproduce two other adaptive rewrite methods, WeWrite and AWHCP, using the same training set. We reproduced WeWrite following the complete procedure described in its original paper 
(

4.2. 
Overall Offline Performance

We present the offline performances in Table 
1
. WeFair achieves the best overall performance. The WeFair-4B model reaches 73.48% overall accuracy, exceeding the strongest large-size baselines Gemini3.1 and DeepSeek4.1. WeFair-2B achieves 72.81% and the highest consistent Recall of 83.95%. Its EM also exceeded the other two adaptive rewrite baselines. Gemini 3.1 retained the highest EM and the lowest Hallucination and Intent Drift Rates, while achieving much lower rewrite recall. This suggests that large-size models with CoT can substantially reduce hallucination and intent drift, but yield more incorrect preservation. WeFair’s advantage lies in the balance of successful rewriting and preservation. Notably, our small-size WeFair-2B achieves significantly lower hallucination and intent drift rates than other comparable rewriting methods (with its intent drift rate reduced to about one-third), validating the effectiveness of our fidelity-aware training approach. 

Intent fidelity.

WeFair achieves lower hallucination and intent drift rates than WeWrite and AWHCP, while more reliably preserving queries that did not require rewriting. Both baselines tended to favor rewriting, particularly AWHCP. Even the smallest WeFair-0.8B surpasses AWHCP-8B in preservation. The large-size LLMs generally had lower intent drift rates than WeFair but also lower successful-rewrite recall. These trends support the value of fidelity-aware alignment with human feedback in mitigating intent drift while maintaining effective personalized rewriting.

Gains from model scaling.

WeFair’s overall accuracy increased by 5.91 percentage points from 0.8B to 2B, but only 0.67 points from 2B to 4B, while consistent recall remained nearly unchanged. These results indicate diminishing gains over the backbone sizes, with most of the observed gain already attained at 2B. 

Inference mode.

Direct WeFair exceeds WeFair-CoT in overall accuracy at both 2B and 4B, and also achieves lower intent drift. CoT variants yield higher EM and PD Recall, but these gains did not translate into better overall performance, indicating explicit reasoning is unnecessary for the strongest results in these comparisons. For general large-size LLMs, we observe that Qwen3.8-27B with CoT achieves much better performance than direct prediction. These results suggest that our fidelity-aware training enables the rewriter to perform more effective implicit reasoning than explicit CoT. We further analyze the corresponding decode tokens and inference latency in Section 
4.5
.

Ablation Study

4.3.1. 
Ablation Study of Curriculum SFT

To examine the role of curriculum learning in the SFT stage, we ablate the C2Q and CoT phases before direct human-target adaptation (HR). All four variants use the same number of human reformulation examples, but C2Q adds context-to-query samples in 
. The results in Table 
3
 show that this broader, lower-fidelity demonstration supervision is most beneficial when combined with the subsequent human-target learning phases. Specifically, C2Q
HR improves consistent recall over HR Only but reduces preservation recall, producing little overall gain. CoT
HR achieves higher preservation recall and decision accuracy than both variants, supporting the contribution of reasoning supervision to rewrite-or-preserve decisions. Full Curriculum further improves rewrite consistency and decision accuracy, achieving the highest overall accuracy. These findings suggest that the broad and lower-fidelity C2Q and CoT supervision can aid capability acquisition in our curriculum SFT.

4.3.2. 
Ablation Study of Progressive Alignment.

To assess the contribution of each stage in our progressive alignment framework, we compared four training configurations on the 2B model: SFT Only, SFT
DPO, SFT
GRPO, and the full training sequence (WeFair). All configurations use the same total amount of training data. Results are shown in Table 
3
.

Demonstration builds the rewriting foundation.

Our curriculum SFT already surpasses all evaluated adaptive baseline variants in consistent recall. This supports the effectiveness of demonstration-based learning in establishing rewriting capabilities in compact models. However, its low PD recall reveals a strong tendency to rewrite queries even when preservation is appropriate. Demonstration learning provides a capable starting point but remains insufficient for reliable rewrite-or-preserve decisions.

Consumption preference alignment further improves rewriting.

The subsequent consumption-guided DPO yields modest improvements in consistent recall and precision, further strengthening successful rewriting. However, PD Recall decreases slightly, also indicating an increased tendency to rewrite preservation-required queries. This imbalance limits the improvement in overall accuracy, suggesting that consumption-based preference alignment refines rewriting capabilities but cannot reduce over-writing.

Verification delivers the largest benefits.

Adding strict-verification GRPO produces the largest improvement in overall accuracy, substantially improving preservation while maintaining high rewrite-decision recall. Importantly, the full sequence achieves higher consistent recall and precision than SFT
GRPO. These gaps even exceed the corresponding gains from adding DPO to SFT alone, suggesting that the value of preference alignment extends beyond its immediate improvements. Although SFT
GRPO achieves slightly higher PD recall, the full sequence delivers the best overall performance by combining successful rewriting with effective preservation.
All these ablation results support the complementary roles of demonstration, preference, and verification. Their progressive combination achieves the strongest overall performance.

On-policy Reward Purity Analysis

To examine whether auxiliary rewards improve on-policy alignment beyond the strict target verification, we compare five GRPO reward configurations initialized from the same startpoint. 
. All configurations use the same total amount of training data. Strict Verification(SV) uses only the reward in Eq. (

The consumption signal follows the criteria used for DPO preference construction. We further evaluate vote-aggregated RM feedback (RM-Augmented Verification 4 Vote), where four independent decisions are obtained from four rollouts of the same RM, and a response is accepted only if all four decisions accept it. Strict verification provides more reliable positive reinforcement.

SV reward pattern achieves the highest positive and overall advantage purity, together with the strongest overall accuracy among the evaluated configurations. Adding RM feedback reduces positive purity and substantially weakens preservation, suggesting that auxiliary reward models provide less reliable positive reinforcement than strict target agreement. SV has lower negative purity than RM-Augmented Verification, consistent with exact matching discouraging some acceptable alternative reformulations. These results support prioritizing high-fidelity verification in final on-policy alignment without implying perfect semantic supervision. Four Vote setting over repeated judgments from the same RM therefore does not automatically make the induced advantage signals more reliable. This discrepancy also shows that advantage purity is a diagnostic of training-signal reliability.

4.5. 
Offline Efficiency Analysis

To examine the effects of model size and inference mode, we measure the mean number of decoded tokens and per-sample inference latency with the same computing resource. The DeepSeek and Gemini baselines use the API call and only report the mean number of decoded tokens. The other methods are deployed on an Nvidia H20 GPU with the same settings. Decoded tokens include reasoning when CoT is enabled. All the results are reported in Table 
5
. The details of the implementation are described in Appendix 
A
.

General-purpose LLMs achieve competitive rewriting performance without task-specific fine-tuning, but their full-CoT configurations incur substantial decoding overhead. The CoT–Direct comparison for Qwen3.8-27B illustrates this trade-off. Explicit reasoning improves rewriting quality but sharply increases inference time, making full-CoT inference impractical in real-time search systems.
In contrast, WeFair achieves low latency while retaining strong rewrite quality. WeFair-2B required only 7.60 decoded tokens and 0.316 s per sample, compared with 26.65 tokens and 0.795 s for WeFair-CoT-2B. At 4B size, direct WeFair used 72.66% fewer tokens and achieved a 
 speedup over its CoT variant. Notably, both direct variants also attained higher offline overall accuracy than CoT variants(See Table 
1
). Relative to WeWrite at matched sizes, WeFair delivered higher overall accuracy and slightly lower mean latency.
For the three direct WeFair variants, WeFair-2B retained nearly the latency of WeFair-0.8B while improving overall accuracy. Increasing the size to 4B significantly raises inference time with only a slight accuracy gain. Accordingly, we select WeFair-2B for both online A/B testing and production deployment.

4.6. 
Online Evaluation

To evaluate WeFair’s online performance, we conducted a 10-day online A/B test in WeChat short-video search, deploying WeFair-2B on 16 NVIDIA L20 GPUs. The production control method is WeWrite
(
Cheng et al., 2026
)
. In production, WeFair generates rewrites for 42.26% of processed queries and outputs preservation markers for 57.74%. We measured click-through rate (CTR), effective video-view rate (VV>10s), and query reformulation rate (QRR). We also measured P95 model inference latency (81 ms) and the end-to-end search latency increase (+16 ms).
As shown in Table 
7
, WeFair with cache + vector retrieval strategy increases CTR and VV>10s by 2.951% and 2.593%, respectively, while reducing QRR by 2.350%. WeFair with only cache retrieval also achieves improvement against the control method. Additional lightweight vector retrieval further reduces the query reformulation rate. All improvements were statistically significant (P<0.01).
Higher engagement and fewer reformulations validate the practical value of WeFair for meeting users’ immediate information needs. 

Online cases analysis.

Table 
6
 presents four cases from our online search system. The first illustrates context-guided spelling correction and the next two illustrate intent-aware expansion: adding explanatory and recruitment details brings retrieval results closer to the users’ current needs. In the degraded case, adding “Education Bureau” provides relevant information, but the rewrite omits “XX City”, which is needed to identify the intended account. The rewrite query therefore remains incomplete despite being topically relevant. Together with the online gains, these examples support WeFair’s effectiveness in translating contextual clues into rewrites that better serve personalized information needs.

5. 
Conclusion

We presented WeFair, a fidelity-aware alignment framework for training compact personalized query rewriters that mitigate intent drift. The framework matches human feedback to three complementary roles: demonstration through curriculum SFT, preference learning through DPO, and verification through GRPO. Offline experiments showed that WeFair achieves the best overall accuracy and reduced intent drift while maintaining low inference latency. A 10-day online A/B test further showed significant gains in engagement and fewer query reformulations. Furthermore, our analyses revealed that broad, lower-fidelity context-to-query supervision benefited the complete SFT curriculum, while consumption-based preference learning complemented subsequent verification. In GRPO, strict agreement with human targets yielded higher overall accuracy than the tested auxiliary rewards. These findings support matching supervision fidelity to the role of each training stage. Our method has been deployed in WeChat short-video search, demonstrating the practical value of proposed fidelity-aware alignment for effective personalization under production latency constraints.

A.1. 
Adaptation of AWHCP

Our local adaptation retains AWHCP’s Problem–Intention–Fix–Rewrite (PIFR) structure and SFT–DPO–GRPO sequence 
(
Ou et al., 2026
)

For the DPO training, we continue from the SFT policy using preference pairs constructed from user reformulations and subsequent consumption feedback. Each pair shares the same original query and query-time context. For rewrite-required examples, human reformulations or consumption-supported candidates serve as preferred responses. The candidates whose observed retrieval results contain none of the relevant consumed items serve as negative responses. Preservation examples favor the preservation marker over generated rewrites. Missing feedback is excluded, and the subsequent consumption-based behavior feedback is used only to construct supervision. DPO thus learns relative preferences before final GRPO refinement returns to strict human-target verification.
