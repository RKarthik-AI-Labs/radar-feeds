Abstract

Adaptive reasoning must decide whether to answer now or spend more compute.
Predicting that the current answer is wrong does not establish whether
further reasoning will improve it. We introduce the Full-Ledger
Counterfactual Value Audit (
FLCVA
), which compares an immediate answer
with four fixed-length continuations from the same frozen prefix under one
answer procedure and accounts for the cost of every signal used by a policy.
On GSM8K and MATH-500, a random 256-token continuation recovers a wrong answer
in 26.6% of Qwen3-8B cases and 18.9% of DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B cases,
while damaging a correct answer in 1.4% and 1.9%. Correctness labels from independently sampled
continuations predict a separate continuation’s outcome in both primary
models. In offline replay, using these labels to allocate compute improves
performance on DeepSeek; the Qwen3 gain remains inconclusive. Under full
cost accounting, the tested lightweight, myopic controllers mainly recognize
current errors and add little continuation-specific value beyond that
shortcut. A Llama-8B replication and action/cost ablations examine the
robustness of these findings. 
FLCVA
 separates
measuring local continuation value, predicting it from observable signals,
and using those predictions to allocate computation.

Introduction

Longer reasoning can improve language-model accuracy, but it is not
automatically the best use of a limited inference budget. Chain-of-thought
and zero-shot reasoning prompts elicit explicit reasoning, while
self-consistency, repeated sampling, verifier reranking, and search offer
several ways to spend additional compute
 
(
Wei et al., 2022
; 
Kojima et al., 2022
; 
Wang et al., 2023b
; 
Brown et al., 2024
; 
Cobbe et al., 2021
; 
Yao et al., 2023
)
.
Revision can improve an answer 
(
Madaan et al., 2023
)
, but without
reliable feedback it can also reduce accuracy 
(
Huang et al., 2024
)
.
Test-time scaling must therefore decide not only whether more computation can
help, but 
where
 and 
when
 to spend it
 
(
Snell et al., 2024
; 
Muennighoff et al., 2025
)
.

Adaptive methods use learned halting policies or signals such as confidence,
agreement, entropy, and predicted difficulty to halt, sample, branch, or route
computation
 
(
Graves, 2016
; 
Banino et al., 2021
; 
Schuster et al., 2022
; 
Aggarwal et al., 2023
; 
Damani et al., 2025
; 
Pan et al., 2025
; 
Scalena et al., 2025
; 
Yang et al., 2025b
)
.
These signals can identify an answer that is likely wrong
 
(
Kadavath et al., 2022
; 
Kuhn et al., 2023
; 
Xiong et al., 2024
; 
Lightman et al., 2024
)
,
but that is not the same as identifying whether further reasoning will repair
it. Likewise, a stable answer may be correct and finished, wrong but
recoverable, or correct and vulnerable to revision. The decision-relevant
object is a checkpoint-level counterfactual: from an identical frozen prefix,
what changes if the model answers now rather than receiving one more
predeclared block of reasoning?

We separate three questions. 
Measurement:
 how much does a specified
continuation change correctness per unit cost? 
Prediction:
 do
observable signals distinguish recoverable errors and vulnerable correct
answers? 
Allocation:
 do these predictions improve accuracy under a
shared compute budget after paying for the signals? For allocation,
comparison with a current-wrongness baseline tests the additional value of
predicting which errors can be repaired.

Figure 1: 
FLCVA
 separates measurement from control. From one frozen
prefix, the same answer procedure evaluates answering now, continuing along
the stored trajectory for another 256 tokens, and taking three separately
sampled continuations of the same length. Correctness change divided by
charged cost defines signed local value. Continuation correctness labels
are available only offline; replay policies use inference-time signals and
pay for every observation they require.

Our contributions are:

•

A paired audit of continuation value.
 We compare answering
now with fixed-length continuations from the same prefix under a fixed
answer procedure, measuring signed correctness change per expected logical
token.

•

A full-ledger proxy audit.
 We evaluate observable signals
against this target and charge every observation required by a policy,
including the cost of obtaining its signals.

•

A measurement-to-control decomposition.
 We distinguish
offline diagnostics using future correctness labels from deployable
policies. Scoring with independently sampled branches avoids leakage from
the evaluated continuation. Comparison with a current-wrongness baseline
tests allocation gains beyond error recognition.

•

Cross-model evidence and intervention robustness.
 Across
two primary models and a Llama-8B replication, continuation can repair errors
and damage correct answers. Persistence provides information beyond
current correctness, but the tested lightweight, myopic controllers add
little beyond error recognition. We examine sensitivity to continuation
length, answer cues, parsers, and logical and physical cost accounting.

Related Work

Adaptive inference and value of computation.

Metareasoning and anytime algorithms frame computation as an action whose
value depends on the current decision state
 
(
Horvitz et al., 1989
; 
Zilberstein, 1996
; 
Hay et al., 2012
)
.
Modern test-time reasoning instantiates this idea through adaptive sampling,
learned routing, branch allocation, confidence-based stopping, and budget
forcing
 
(
Aggarwal et al., 2023
; 
Damani et al., 2025
; 
Pan et al., 2025
; 
Scalena et al., 2025
; 
Muennighoff et al., 2025
)
.

Wu et al. (2025)
 compare model sizes and inference strategies
under compute budgets, showing how the inference procedure shapes the
accuracy–cost trade-off.

FLCVA
 instantiates value of computation as paired measurement of local
stop–continue value for a realized language-model prefix, complementing work
whose primary contribution is a controller.

Confidence and process verification.

Self-evaluation, semantic uncertainty, outcome verifiers, PRMs, and
process-advantage verifiers estimate answer reliability or intermediate
progress
 
(
Kadavath et al., 2022
; 
Kuhn et al., 2023
; 
Xiong et al., 2024
; 
Uesato et al., 2022
; 
Lightman et al., 2024
; 
Wang et al., 2023a
; 
Zhang et al., 2025
; 
Setlur et al., 2024
)
.
SCoRe uses multi-turn reinforcement learning to improve a model’s ability to
correct its own errors 
(
Kumar et al., 2025
)
.
We test how well these allocation signals predict the correctness change
from a specified continuation. The public PRM serves as an observable
proxy, and external endpoint grading defines the audit target.

Closest adaptive-reasoning systems.

Studies of overthinking identify redundant reasoning that adds cost with
little accuracy benefit 
(
Chen et al., 2025
)
.
TRACE combines temporal consistency and confidence; UAB allocates sampling
from question uncertainty; PoLR and Prefix Consistency use truncated prefixes
or regenerated continuations; ThinkBooster unifies scaling strategies and
cost views; and PUMA combines semantic redundancy with answer verification
 
(
Li et al., 2026
; 
Nguyen et al., 2026
; 
Jindal et al., 2026
; 
Iwase et al., 2026
; 
Smirnov et al., 2026
; 
Min et al., 2026
)
.
These works primarily propose controllers, reliability signals, or evaluation
frameworks. 
FLCVA
 instead makes the paired stop–continue contrast the
estimand and jointly audits proxy information and charged cost.

3.1 
Signed continuation value

At each checkpoint, we keep the prompt and existing reasoning tokens fixed.
We obtain an answer immediately and another answer after each of four
continuations. All answers use the same generation, parsing, and grading
procedure, called the 
answer operator
. We subtract immediate-answer
correctness from average continuation correctness and divide by average
continuation cost, including the answer tokens. The resulting value is
positive for observed repair, negative for observed damage, and zero when
none of the sampled continuations changes correctness.

3.2 
Protocol and evaluation split

We evaluate Qwen3-8B and DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B
 
(
Yang et al., 2025a
; 
DeepSeek-AI, 2025
)
 on all 1,319 GSM8K test problems and
all 500 MATH-500 problems 
(
Cobbe et al., 2021
; 
Hendrycks et al., 2021
)
. Each
model–problem pair has four independently sampled reasoning trajectories,
called 
spines
, capped at 4,096 tokens and frozen every 256 tokens.
At every nonterminal checkpoint we apply answer-now and four 256-token
continuations. Branch 1 reuses the next stored trajectory segment (the

on-spine
 branch); branches 2–4 are sampled separately from the same
prefix (
off-spine
 branches). The same greedy answer operator grades
all endpoints.

We deterministically partition problem IDs, assigning 80% of problems to
analysis and 20% to confirmation. We use analysis problems to choose each
proxy’s scoring direction, calibrate predictions, and fit controller
parameters. Confirmation continuation outcomes are used only for held-out evaluation.
Unless stated otherwise, intervals use 2,000 bootstrap draws of complete
problems, retaining their models, spines, checkpoints, and branches. The
primary study contains 14,552 spines and 218,280 eligible checkpoints.
Details of sampling, parsing, missing data, and bootstrapping are in

Appendices
 
A
 and 
C

3.3 
Identification and uncertainty

The paired comparison holds the prompt and frozen reasoning tokens
identical across the answer-now arm and all continuations. The arms differ
in the action taken after checkpoint 
. Branch 1 reuses the stored spine
segment while retaining its full reasoning-plus-probe cost in 
.
This saves physical generation while preserving the specified intervention
and its logical cost. Later checkpoints follow the original spine,
independently of the outcomes of off-spine samples.

We estimate uncertainty by resampling benchmark problems. Each problem
contributes multiple models, spines, depths, and correlated outcomes, so each
bootstrap draw retains all records nested within the selected problem IDs.
Point estimates weight eligible checkpoints in the reported population;
intervals preserve within-problem dependence and quantify uncertainty across
problems conditional on the stored trajectories.

3.4 
Proxies and cost

We audit recent-answer persistence, answer confidence and its negative-log
form (ANLL), four-spine modal agreement, minimum step-level process reward
model (PRM) score, and top-20 approximate entropy. Persistence is the fraction
of up to four recent stop answers matching the current answer. Confidence
and ANLL satisfy 
, so they encode
the same signal with reversed order; we summarize them as one signal family.
The controllers use both input features.

The primary prediction metric is AUROC for detecting any recovery among
currently wrong checkpoints and any damage among currently correct ones.
These labels indicate whether at least one of four branches changes
correctness; they differ from the single-draw probabilities above. Average
precision with prevalence baselines and calibration assess rare events and
probability quality. Matched and conditional diagnostics test information
beyond model, dataset, depth, difficulty, and current correctness; they are
grouped in 
Appendix
 
C
. Difficulty is a label-aware diagnostic
stratifier and is never exposed to a deployable policy.

The primary ledger counts all logical tokens needed to expose a prefix,
obtain an answer, advance a spine, or score a policy-specific proxy,
including the observations required by PRM and parallel-agreement policies.
Logical tokens quantify intervention cost. To examine hardware-related
costs, we also replay policy orderings under estimated FLOPs and calibrated
H20 seconds and report measured GPU time (
Section
 
E.2
).

From Measurement to Allocation

A 
frontier
 is a problem’s current checkpoint during compute
allocation. Replay begins with one frontier per confirmation problem and a
shared budget. Each action advances one problem by the next stored 256-token
segment and reveals its next answer. The four spine seeds define four
repetitions, with one spine per problem in each repetition and accuracy
measured over problems. All policies are compared up to the same reachable
budget endpoint. We measure budget-averaged accuracy using normalized area
under the accuracy–ledger curve (AULC). For each full-system bootstrap
replicate, we resample problems, rerun allocation, and recompute the budget
endpoints.

Effects of signal cost and scheduling.

Two controlled comparisons examine signal costs and scheduling. First, we
fix the action order based on agreement and charge the extra trajectories
needed to obtain that signal. DeepSeek normalized AULC falls from 0.819 to
0.672 (
Table
 
15
), isolating the effect of including
signal costs.

Second, we compare greedy allocation by true next-step correctness change
per token with exact multi-step allocation on the same 64 non-overlapping
four-problem subsets per model. Both schedules use future outcomes.
Normalized AULC is 0.820 versus 0.902 for Qwen3 and 0.791 versus 0.854 for
DeepSeek (
Table
 
16
). This hindsight comparison shows the
benefit of planning beyond immediate value within these subsets.

Together, these controls motivate charging every signal and considering the
value of subsequent states. The relative contributions of signal quality,
calibration, and planning horizon to the observed controller gap remain an
open question.

6 
Discussion

A signal can predict current answer quality, predict continuation effects,
or improve a sequence of compute-allocation decisions. Our results show
why each claim needs its own evaluation.

Test continuation value beyond error recognition.

The correctness shortcut captures much of the observable allocation gain,
making it an informative baseline alongside round-robin. Persistence
retains conditional information about recovery and damage, and highly
persistent correct answers are less vulnerable to harmful revision. These
findings support using stability as a predictive feature and evaluating its
allocation value through gains over the correctness shortcut.

Charge signals and value later states.

The fixed-order agreement comparison shows that observation costs can erase
a signal’s apparent allocation benefit. The hindsight comparison on matched
subsets shows that greedy scheduling can still underperform multi-step
optimization even with exact knowledge of immediate value. The same full
ledger provides a basis for testing stronger sequential controllers using
inference-time features.

Model and dataset differences.

Ridge-

 over the shortcut on DeepSeek; the Qwen3
increment is inconclusive, and transfer to Llama without refitting gives a
negative gain. Exploratory replay by dataset shows a DeepSeek gain on
MATH-500 and a loss on GSM8K (
Appendix
 
F
). On the paired
200-problem subset, changing cost weights while fixing action order barely
changes the gain difference between models. The effects of calibrating
predictions separately for each dataset remain uncertain
(
Section
 
F.2
). The Llama result concerns transfer
of predictors fitted on the two primary models. Explaining these
differences calls for targeted analysis of recoverable error types, reasoning
changes associated with repair, and predictor transfer. The audit provides
a common protocol for testing these explanations and comparing stronger
controllers.

7 
Conclusion

FLCVA
 measures the externally graded value of one more reasoning block
from the same prefix, while accounting for its cost. Across the evaluated
models, continuation can repair incorrect answers and damage correct ones.
The tested lightweight, myopic controllers add little beyond identifying
likely errors. This measurement-to-control gap motivates policies that use
inference-time signals to value both immediate outcomes and future reasoning
states. Their benefit can be tested through accuracy–cost gains over the
correctness shortcut.

8 
Limitations

The study evaluates three open-weight models on mathematics tasks, with
continuations of 128, 256, and 512 tokens. Generalization to other tasks,
models, and reasoning actions requires further evaluation. Continuation
value depends on sampling and on how answers are generated, parsed, and
graded. Exact-match grading may miss equivalent answers. Bootstrap
intervals quantify variation across problems while keeping the generated
trajectories fixed.

The controller results cover lightweight policies that score only the next
reasoning step. In the Llama replication, these policies are transferred
without refitting. Correctness labels from separately sampled branches
support offline diagnostics. Stored multi-step transitions are available
only along the main trajectories. Exact dynamic programming uses these
transitions and known future outcomes to provide an upper bound on sampled
groups of four problems. The same protocol supports evaluating richer
controllers.

9 
Ethical considerations

The study uses established mathematics benchmarks and open-weight models,
performs no human-subject intervention, and trains lightweight diagnostic
predictors. Better allocation could reduce the computation needed for a
given answer quality. The audit itself requires generation of multiple
spines and continuations, answer probes, and auxiliary scoring. We report
measured GPU time and alternative cost ledgers in

Section
 
E.2
. Energy consumption, emissions, and net
environmental savings fall outside these measurements.

Premature stopping can preserve consequential errors in applications such as
medicine and law, while continuing can damage an initially correct answer.
Applying adaptive stopping in these domains requires task-specific
validation of both recovery and damage, together with safeguards
appropriate to the consequences of errors.

Cost coordinates

C.2 
Matched and conditional information

These diagnostics examine variation in continuation outcomes among
checkpoints with similar observed signals. Collision coverage is the
fraction of checkpoints in matched cells containing both a feasible nonzero
event and a zero-value control. Bin sensitivity, exact matching, and
nearest-neighbor comparisons assess this outcome heterogeneity at different
matching resolutions. Conditional AUROC and held-out log-loss reduction
measure predictive information beyond the matching covariates.

At ten-bin resolution, 81.9% and 94.5% of Qwen3 and DeepSeek checkpoints
lie in at least one ambiguous matched cell; exact persistence matching gives
77.3% and 87.7%. Conditional AUROC is 0.632–0.692, and held-out log-loss
reduction is 0.021–0.053 bits. Thus persistence provides predictive
information even though checkpoints with matching signals can have different
continuation outcomes.
