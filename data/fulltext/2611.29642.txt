Abstract

Building NLP applications for education is constrained by data scarcity due to student privacy concerns and data regulations. To address this, we propose a style-aware approach to rewriting student forum posts that retains analytical value while reducing identifiability. Our method isolates content, structure, and style as separate conditioning channels and aggregates over student-disjoint exemplar partitions within style clusters, extending Differentially Private In-context Learning (DP-ICL) with cluster-restricted retrieval. Across three educational NLP datasets, our approach achieves high style fidelity, preserves downstream task performance, and reduces authorship attribution risk relative to the original student-written texts, including against a dedicated authorship-attribution model.

Introduction

NLP applications for education face significant 
reproducibility challenges
: real student data is subject to strict data protection requirements under privacy regulations and institutional policies 
pardo2014ethical
; 
young2014educational
; 
neshaei2025bridging
; 
Lohse et al. (2019)
. We note that simply redacting sensitive information is insufficient, as Institutional Review Boards (IRBs) often prohibit the release of student-authored content; therefore, even student texts without any personally-identifiable information (PII) cannot be released. Given these constraints, researchers have investigated general-purpose LLMs for student simulation

; 
Li et al. (2026)
;

 and data synthesis

; 
Kieser et al. (2023)
.

A central challenge for current simulation and synthesis efforts is the 
tradeoff between privacy and utility
: methods that strongly protect identity tend to degrade the linguistic and pedagogical signals that make the data useful. Our paper addresses this tradeoff in the context of 
discussion forum posts
.

Discussion forums are the de facto interactive medium for both MOOCs and university courses, where learners ask questions, discuss ideas, and reflect on course material 
onyema2019online
. Unlike formal academic writing, forum posts embody naturalistic student language, reflecting diverse cognitive and communicative tendencies as evidenced by their varying lengths, tones, and rhetorical structures. These posts are rich with pedagogically meaningful signals, including urgency, confusion, and emotional states 
Khodeir (2021)
; 
Agrawal et al. (2015)
, making them a valuable, rich resource

Cho et al. (2024)
. More recently, increasingly capable language models have spurred the deployment of virtual teaching assistants in discussion forums 
Goel and Polepeddi (2018)
; 
shah2024students
; 
Kweon et al. (2025)
,
which require training and evaluation data that reflects the full distribution of student writing styles.

In the context of student simulation, general-purpose models often fail to capture the diversity in personalities, cognitive capabilities, and stylistic idiosyncrasies 
Martynova et al. (2025)
; 
zheng2025teaching
. This failure could lead to high-stakes consequences; a virtual teaching assistant trained on distributionally flat synthetic student data may fail to produce the pedagogically optimal response in deployment.
Addressing this requires synthetic data that captures the underlying stylistic distribution of real student populations.

We propose 
rewriting
 each real student post into a less-identifiable version that preserves the stylistic and semantic properties needed for downstream NLP; unlike fully synthetic generation, each output corresponds to exactly one source post. Prior work on synthetic data 
Li et al. (2023)
; 
Nadă˙ (2025)
 and differentially private language modeling 
yu2021differentially
; 
shi2022just
; 
Flemings et al. (2024)
 pays little attention to style-preserving synthesis in education. The core challenge is to 
preserve cluster-level style for utility while suppressing individual stylistic fingerprints
 that enable re-identification. We address this by decomposing student texts into content, structure, and style, then aggregating over student-disjoint exemplar partitions to limit any one author’s impact. We implement this via Style-conditioned Ensemble Aggregation with Multi-channel Synthesis (
SEAMS
). SEAMS extends DP-ICL (
wu2024privacy
, more details in Section 
3.5
) by incorporating stylistically similar exemplars while restricting influence of individual authors. Across three datasets, SEAMS preserves style, maintains downstream task performance, and empirically reduces authorship attribution risk relative to the originals.

3 
SEAMS

SEAMS isolates three conditioning channels from student texts: content, structure, and style. It then aggregates over candidate generations from student-disjoint exemplar partitions within style clusters. The rest of this section describes each component. 
SEAMS does not include PII redaction
; therefore, any institution interested in using SEAMS should perform redactions independently.

3.1 
Preprocessing

Each post is converted into two SEAMS input channels: bullet summaries for content and academic-content-removed templates for structure. Together with retrieved cluster exemplars for style, these form the three conditioning channels.

Bullet summaries:
 We summarize each post with 
Qwen-2.5-7B-Instruct
 
qwen2024qwen2
 into bullet points that retain content while removing style signals, using a GEPA-optimized 
Agrawal et al. (2025)
 prompt. We use this model for cost considerations, and a 7B model is sufficient for this task. The optimization uses Stack Exchange posts, maximizing semantic similarity, stylistic 
dissimilarity
, reconstruction fidelity, and succinctness. Details are in Appendix 
C
.

Academic-content-removed templates:
 We also use GPT-5-mini (
permitted by our IRB
) to convert each post into a template where academic content is replaced by bracketed slots, e.g., 
[concept or knowledge]
, while preserving connectives and rhetorical structure. We chose GPT-5-mini because templating requires reliable span-level identification of domain-specific content; in pilot comparisons, GPT-5-mini produced templates that matched expert annotations more closely than 
Qwen3-30B-A3B-Instruct-2507
. The template was originally designed by education collaborators for clustering, but we find it suitable for SEAMS’ structural channel. We GEPA-optimize the templating prompt against expert annotations on proprietary student posts, maximizing the per-character agreement rate between model predictions and human reference (Appendix 
D
).

3.2 
Embedding Model Selection

Well-formed, meaningful style clustering requires an embedding model that distinguishes posts by writing style rather than topic. We compare 
StyleDistance
 
patel2025styledistance
, 
wegmann2022same
, Coh-Metrix 
Graesser et al. (2004)
, and 
all-Mini-LM-v6
 
reimers2019sentence
. 
StyleDistance
 is selected because it separates within-student and between-student post pairs the most in terms of embedding cosine similarities, yielding the highest t-test statistics (Appendix 
B
).

3.3 
Per-Seed Style Clustering

HDBSCAN (with UMAP reduction first) is selected for its ability to identify clusters of varying density without requiring a fixed cluster count.
We sweep a combination of HDBSCAN settings, with minimum cluster size 
 and minimum samples 
. The best HDBSCAN setting for the dataset is selected and fixed across seeds based on the average validation cluster membership similarity. Intuitively, high cluster membership similarity indicates better similarity between the unseen posts and existing clusters, leading to higher-quality retrieval.

3.4 
Cross-Seed Meta-Cluster Formation

Per-seed HDBSCAN clusters can be unstable across random seeds. To identify a shared meta-cluster mapping across seeds for easy comparison (and to enable comparison against other cluster-based approaches), we perform average-linkage agglomerative hierarchical clustering to connect clusters from the previous step to form meta-clusters. As all data points are discussion forum posts, they occupy a similar style embedding space, potentially flattening the cosine similarity between cluster centers and reducing modularity in the final meta-cluster hierarchy. Therefore, we remove dominant shared directions using All-but-the-Top (ABTT, 
Mu et al. (2017)
) on the embeddings. The final meta-cluster count aims to maximize cross-seed cluster representation, such that most meta-clusters aggregate clusters from multiple seeds rather than a single seed, enhancing generalizability.

3.5 
Privacy-Motivated Generation

Once the clusters are finalized, the generation step consists of three components: student-disjoint partition, candidate generation, and noisy aggregation. We adopt the DP-ICL framework 
wu2024privacy
 as the structural basis for this step. DP-ICL obtains differentially private responses from a noisy consensus over LLM outputs conditioned on disjoint exemplar subsets: noisy voting over predicted labels for classification, and noisy aggregation in an embedding or keyword space for generation. SEAMS follows the generation setting and aggregates in embedding space. We extend it with cluster-restricted retrieval and student-disjoint partitioning to match our specific threat model. Unlike DP-ICL’s Embedding Space Aggregation (ESA), which returns a 
public
 zero-shot candidate 
(
wu2024privacy
)
, we select among the privately conditioned candidates themselves and retrieve exemplars by style similarity rather than random subsampling. We therefore 
do not claim any formal differential privacy guarantees
; our framework is privacy-motivated, drawing structural principles from DP-ICL. We include ESA as a baseline. Algorithm 
1
 describes this procedure.

Student-disjoint Partitioning:
 Retrieved exemplars are divided into disjoint sets, and if a student authors multiple posts among the exemplars, they are all placed in the same partition. This is to ensure that any single student’s individual style influences appear in only one of the 
 candidates, as one candidate is produced per partition. We use cluster-restricted retrieval (retrieving within the same cluster only), which further pulls candidates toward the 
cluster
’s typical register.

Candidate Generation:
 Given the partitions, one candidate rewrite is created by an LLM per partition; the content and structural channels are shared across candidates so that candidate differences stem from stylistic conditioning alone. The rewriter prompt is selected from alternatives proposed by the MIPROv2 prompt optimizer 
opsahl2024optimizing
 under a simplified setting rather than refinement (selection details in Appendix 
E
; the final prompt is given in Appendix 
P
), as iterative refinement may steer 
all
 generations toward the typical structure of forum posts to maximize average metric value, leading to low-diversity generations.

Noisy Aggregation:
 Following DP-ICL, candidate embeddings from 
StyleDistance
 
patel2025styledistance
 are 
-clipped
 to bound the influence of any single candidate, 
averaged
 to find a consensus, and 
perturbed
 with Gaussian noise to reduce the determinism in the selection. The chosen rewrite candidate is the one whose clipped embedding lies closest to the mean. Together, these operations limit the per-candidate influence on the output.

Component ablations (Appendix 
L
) show that selecting the candidate most similar to mean embeddings filters low-quality tail outputs, while cluster restriction and noisy aggregation preserve fidelity and serve structural roles.

Datasets

We validate SEAMS across three complementary, English-language datasets spanning different educational contexts and downstream classification tasks (Table 
1
).

4.1 
Proprietary Discussion Forum Dataset

Through an existing institutional partnership with a large public university in the United States, we retrieve student discussion forum posts in their deployed learning management system, covering 15 academic terms from Fall 2019 through Winter 2024. The data access is approved by the Institutional Review Board.

The full corpus comprises over four million posts across nearly two thousand courses. Most of these are non-question posts, featuring instructor-issued task prompts with limited stylistic variation; therefore, we restrict our analysis to student-originated academic questions, rather than more logistics-focused queries and announcements, to better capture diverse writing styles.

To identify these questions at scale, we use a two-stage filtering pipeline. We first retain only posts containing a question mark. From these, we randomly sample about 25,000 posts and prompt GPT-4o-mini to classify each as academic or not, yielding 10,074 academic questions. The filtered data includes 7000+ unique students, more than 50 departments, and 700+ courses.

We generate the cognitive engagement level of forum posts based on Bloom’s taxonomy 
Krathwohl (2002)
 using Qwen3-30B-A3B-Instruct-2507 
yang2025qwen3
, following guidance from learning science co-authors and motivated by prior evidence of its applicability to related tasks 
wang2026memorization
; 
Badry et al. (2026)
. In a random sample of 100 LLM-generated silver labels, 88% match domain expert judgments. We formulate this as a multi-class classification task, assigning each post to one of six cognitive complexity levels. This tests whether synthesized posts preserve the cognitive and linguistic properties that distinguish higher-order thinking (e.g., analysis, evaluation) from lower-order thinking (e.g., memorization).

5.1 
Baselines

Naive Zero-shot Generation:
 We examine whether the content and the structural channels alone are sufficient to reconstruct discussion forum posts. We study two baselines: 
Zero-shot-Content (
ZS-C
)

Zero-shot-Content-Structure (

Cluster-Summary-Centric (
CS

 Another natural way to utilize our cluster structures from SEAMS, but with enhanced privacy, is to condense clusters to their summaries. Summaries are generated by repeatedly sampling from the cluster and producing a series of summary candidates; the final summary is the one closest in the semantic embedding space to the averaged candidate embedding. This is similar to our aggregation mechanism. We then employ contrastive summaries to prevent all cluster summaries from becoming too similar, thereby preserving cluster-level signals (Appendix 
F

The naive and summary-based baselines all leverage variants of the SEAMS prompt to ensure fair comparison. Instead of referring to the style context, these baseline prompts instruct the LLM to follow the tone and register typical of a student discussion forum post.

5.3 
Reconstruction Quality

To improve robustness, we repeat all experiments across five seeds. We sample 1,000 validation posts and 1,000 test posts, stratified by post-content categories (course department or discussion topic). We evaluate generation quality along two dimensions: semantic preservation and style fidelity. Semantic preservation is measured using sentence embedding similarity from 
all-mpnet-base-v2
; style fidelity is measured using style embedding similarity under both 
StyleDistance
 and 
wegmann2022same
, which is used to ensure evaluation robustness by providing an embedding separate from the SEAMS mechanisms.

Results are reported in Figure 
2

7

SEAMS preserves stylistic similarity to the original student data across domains

SEAMS-1 performs comparably to SEAMS on fidelity across all three datasets, and slightly better on Stanford MOOC, indicating that 
fidelity gains

For the naive approaches, ZS-CS often outperforms ZS-C, suggesting that our structural templates are useful for generation. ESA, which selects among ZS-CS candidates, still falls below SEAMS because it selects from stylistically weak exemplars, and selection cannot compensate for missing style. Finally, most reconstruction methods achieve reasonable semantic similarity, suggesting that content and structural channels are generally sufficient to preserve meaning. In contrast,

5.5 
Human Evaluation

We strengthen our results with a style-similarity judgment study on Prolific. Each one of 180 sampled items shows an original post from the proprietary dataset, paired with two randomly-ordered rewrites of that same post, one from SEAMS and one from a baseline (ZS-CS, CS, or DP-Prompt). Annotators choose which rewrite “sounds more like it was written by the same person who wrote the original,” focusing on wording and style rather than content, or judge them equally similar (Appendix 
Q

5.6 
Authorship Attacks

Prior work on authorship obfuscation 
(
Brennan et al., 2012
; 
potthast2016author
)
 often targets stylometric attackers,
4
4

4

              
              
              
            An 
authorship attacker
 is a model that, given a text and writing samples from a set of candidate authors, ranks the candidates by how likely each is to have written it. 
Stylometric
 attackers rely on hand-crafted style features such as function-word frequencies, character 
-grams, punctuation, and sentence length 
(
Burrows, 2002
)
; 
embedding-based
 attackers compare learned neural representations.
 while we evaluate both stylometric and embedding-based ones. We evaluate on all three datasets; because Stack Exchange authors are public users, results there measure how much individual style survives rewriting rather than re-identification risk. Our central claim is that style-cluster conditioning pulls reconstructions toward the cluster mean, thereby reducing the individual authorship signal. An attacker holding other posts by every student in the training data tries to identify the author of a released rewrite, ranking all students; Appendix 
J
 additionally restricts candidates to the author’s style cluster.

We use three attackers. Two are retrieval-based and rank candidates by the maximum cosine similarity between the input and a candidate’s posts: 
wegmann2022same

(
uar-emnlp2021
)
, an authorship representation trained to identify authors across documents. The third is stylometric (Stylo-clf; 
Burrows, 2002
), a logistic regression classifier using the feature 
difference
 between the rewrite and a candidate post to predict same- vs. different-author. We report the 
MRR ratio
: the true author’s mean reciprocal rank divided by its chance level over the same posts. A ratio of 
 indicates random attribution; higher values indicate more exploitable authorship signal.

SEAMS reduces MRR ratios relative to real posts for all three attackers on all three datasets (LUAR in Figure 
4
a; others in Appendix 
J
). The reduction is largest for the strongest attacker, LUAR (paired

SEAMS empirically reduces attribution risk
 relative to the original data.
SEAMS is also below SEAMS-1 on six of nine attacker-dataset pairs, suggesting that the partition-then-aggregate approach contributes modestly to author protection.
Consistent with the well-known privacy-utility tradeoff, ZS-CS, ESA, and DP-Prompt achieve stronger MRR ratio reduction on the proprietary dataset, but sacrifice style fidelity (Figure

6 
Discussion and Conclusion

Style as the Bottleneck
 Across our experiments, reconstruction methods reliably preserve content but struggle to maintain an authentic student register. Even with high-level stylistic instructions, their rewrites often show limited linguistic diversity. The example generations (Figure 
3
) suggest that Qwen-2.5-72B has a weak prior understanding of student discussion style. The stronger style preservation of exemplar-conditioned methods across datasets indicates that for style-driven generation in non-mainstream registers, in-context exemplars convey information that instructions alone do not. This motivates using 
exemplar-based approaches
 to synthesize data from non-mainstream domains. Refreshing the exemplar pool with SEAMS rewrites may further reduce attribution risk without a significant decline in fidelity, but we leave its systematic study to future work.

Balancing Empirical Privacy and Utility
 Prior differentially private text generation methods often incur significant utility costs, as illustrated by DP-Prompt’s low stylistic similarity to the original data. SEAMS balances privacy and utility differently: it forgoes formal guarantees but achieves a moderate empirical reduction in within-cluster authorship attribution risk while maintaining reconstruction fidelity sufficient for downstream training.

Conclusion
 In summary, we present SEAMS, a multi-channel, cluster-centric framework for high-fidelity, privacy-motivated reconstruction of student text. By isolating content, structure, and style into conditioning channels and aggregating across student-disjoint partitions within stylistic clusters, SEAMS 
preserves high style fidelity and downstream utility
 on educationally meaningful tasks while 
empirically reducing authorship attribution risk
, a combination not achieved by prior methods we evaluated.

Limitation

One limitation of our work is that we do not offer formal differential privacy guarantees. Our privacy claim is strictly empirical and is demonstrated through its ability to mitigate author attribution risk in Section 
5.6
. Practitioners facing requirements of formal 
 guarantees should choose the appropriate DP methods.

We report ablations of cluster-restricted retrieval, noisy aggregation, and candidate selection in Appendix 
L
; their attack differences fall within seed variance, so the privacy contribution of individual components remains uncertain. Our SEAMS vs. SEAMS-1 comparison suggests partitioning contributes modestly to attack resistance.

SEAMS is not the most privacy-preserving method, as seen in the Authorship Attack section. While the tradeoff between privacy and utility is well-established, there is still room for improvement, potentially through performing generation with in-context examples generated through SEAMS to further reduce attribution risk, or using more computationally expensive approaches that offer more formal DP guarantees.

Inter-rater agreement in our human evaluation is low (ordinal Krippendorff’s 
; Gwet’s AC1 
), reflecting the subjectivity of style judgments. We were able to attribute the low agreement potentially to participants disagreeing on stylistically similar rewrites of the same original posts, as there are irreducible variabilities in human language.

Finally, we only evaluate on one generator model, as our work aims to compare different methods using the same underlying model. However, it is possible that changing the model would alter the effectiveness of each of the methods studied. We leave testing the effect of varying the generator model to future work.

Ethical Considerations

Our proprietary dataset is collected and analyzed in accordance with the existing IRB protocol. The data has not been anonymized at the source. All third-party models and datasets are used in accordance with their respective licenses.

For institutions planning to use SEAMS to rewrite their data for release, we note that SEAMS does not inherently support redacting personally identifiable information. Therefore, any rewritten data should undergo PII redaction or abstraction prior to release.

peer-oriented interaction

Appendix K 
Ablation: Meta-Clustering Necessity

SEAMS uses meta-clusters formed by cross-seed agglomerative clustering rather than per-seed HDBSCAN clusters directly. We ablate this choice by comparing two variants of each method: 
 variants retrieve same-cluster exemplars (SEAMS) or generate summaries (CS) using only the per-seed clusters from the current seed, while the other versions use the cross-seed meta-clusters.

Table 
5
 reports the results. For SEAMS, per-seed and meta-cluster variants are statistically indistinguishable on all three metrics, indicating that the meta-clustering step is not critical for high-fidelity exemplar-based generation. For CS, however, meta-clustering substantially improves style fidelity (0.688 vs. 0.608), at a small cost to semantic similarity and ROUGE-1.

This asymmetry is consistent with the failure mode of the CS pipeline. Per-seed clusters are smaller and more variable across seeds, so per-seed cluster summaries are noisier and less stylistically distinctive. Cross-seed aggregation stabilizes the cluster definitions, producing summaries that more consistently characterize the cluster’s register. SEAMS does not depend on summary stability because it conditions on individual exemplars within the cluster.

We therefore retain meta-clustering in the main pipeline: although it is not necessary for SEAMS itself, it is necessary for fair comparison against the CS baseline, which depends on stable cluster-level summaries.

We draw three findings. First, approximately 71% of unconstrained top-

Second, random selection degrades quality primarily in the tail: mean style fidelity drops, and more outputs fall below a style fidelity of 80 (12.7% vs. 10.8%), with a 10th percentile of 74.4 vs. 78.6. Appendix Q 
Annotation Information

Both human evaluations used a Streamlit application. In the style-match evaluation, each screen showed the original post above two rewrites (“Rewrite A” and “Rewrite B”) with the instruction: “Both rewrites cover the same content as the original post. Which rewrite sounds more like it was written by the same person who wrote the original? Focus on wording, tone, and writing style, not on content.” Annotators chose “Rewrite A is more similar,” “Equally similar,” or “Rewrite B is more similar.” Three attention checks were placed at fixed positions: a verbatim copy of the original versus scrambled text, an instruction to select a specific option, and a near-copy of a casual post versus unrelated formal text.
