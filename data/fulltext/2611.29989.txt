Abstract

We tested whether SHAP improves protein-based Horn rules for distinguishing
lung adenocarcinoma from adjacent-normal tissue. CPTAC provided 213 samples
(111 tumour, 102 adjacent-normal) from 111 patients, including 102 matched
pairs. Per-protein effect estimates and tests used the same complete pairs,
with Benjamini–Hochberg correction. Sparse logistic regression used five
stratified patient-grouped outer folds. Filtering, ranking, imputation,
scaling, feature selection and regularisation tuning were training-only.
Feature selection was repeated within inner folds. A separate five-fold
patient-grouped evaluation of first-order inductive logic programming
(FOL/ILP) used training-derived evidence and discretisation.

Of 9,518 proteins passing quality control, 378 differed in abundance
(40 up, 338 down) at FDR 
 and absolute mean paired log-ratio
difference 
. Classifier ROC-AUC, average precision, accuracy and
balanced accuracy were 0.9997, 0.9998, 0.9906 and 0.9906, respectively.
Held-out rule accuracy, balanced accuracy, precision, recall and F1 were
0.9765, 0.9767, 0.9818, 0.9730 and 0.9774, respectively. HSPA12B,
PALM2-AKAP2, STARD13 and PPM1F had non-zero coefficients in all five
classifier folds. On the original rule splits, one clause appeared twice and the others once.

On common folds across 111 patients,
F1 was 0.9600 with SHAP, 0.9910 without SHAP and 0.9909 for L1 logistic
regression. The inclusive SHAP cutoff admitted all 100 ranked proteins per
audited fold. Absolute coefficients reproduced candidate sets, clauses and
held-out predictions in both analyses. A single threshold reproduced
full-rule predictions in the paired-only cohort.

Within-cohort discrimination leaves diagnostic utility, early detection,
prognosis and external generalisability unestablished. Biomarker and clinical
claims require independent validation, rule-stability testing under repeated
resampling and deeper biological annotation.

Introduction

Lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD), a major histological subtype of lung cancer,
has been profiled proteogenomically alongside matched adjacent-normal
tissue, providing a resource for studying tumour biology and protein
alterations linked to molecular and cellular processes

[
3
, 
11
]
. These data support methodological development.
Clinical screening and diagnosis require evaluation in independent
populations.

Machine learning can prioritise proteins for classification, and SHAP
provides additive explanations of individual predictions

[
6
, 
8
, 
12
]
. In high-dimensional biomedical data,
biomarker discovery and validation require careful feature selection and
resampling 
[
2
, 
5
, 
10
, 
7
]
.
Inductive logic programming (ILP) expresses combinations of evidence as
symbolic rules 
[
9
, 
13
]
.

Our three-stage explainability workflow (Figures 
1

and 
2
) uses patient-matched differential
proteomics to identify protein abundance changes and their direction.
Sparse logistic regression identifies proteins associated with tissue
discrimination, and ranked linear-model SHAP attributions quantify the
magnitude and direction of each protein’s contribution to individual
predictions. First-order logic and ILP (FOL/ILP) combine paired differential
evidence and SHAP attributions from training folds into compact, inspectable
relational Horn clauses. The stages address distinct questions about
tissue-level abundance, predictive importance and how evidence combines
in rules. Causal effects, biological mechanisms and LUAD biomarkers
require separate validation. We separate full-cohort descriptive summaries
from evaluation on held-out patients and report feature and clause
recurrence across five outer folds to assess stability.

We used matched patient-grouped folds to test whether linear-model SHAP
changes the proteins selected, the Horn clauses learned, or the resulting
held-out predictions. Absolute L1 coefficients reproduced all three in
both CPTAC-LUAD analyses, and a single-protein threshold reproduced the
full rule learner’s predictions in the paired-only cohort. The workflow
records the measured abundance and training-derived evidence behind
individual rule decisions, allowing those decisions to be replayed.

Methodology

Cohort and proteomic data:

Sample annotations and the biospecimen manifest in the supplied CPTAC-LUAD
files identified 213 tissues from 111 patients (111 primary tumours and
102 adjacent solid-tissue normals, forming 102 matched pairs).
Classification distinguished these tissues within the cohort. Pairing
focuses the descriptive contrast on within-patient differences and
partially controls inherited background, exposure history, and other
between-patient variation. Tissue composition, tumour purity, batch effects,
and clinical confounding can persist. Adjacent-normal tissue differs from
tissue from cancer-free individuals. TMT log-ratios were collapsed by gene
median and aggregated by aliquot as specified in the notebook.

Matched comparators and uncertainty:

We retained the original separate splits to document the submitted model
and rule results. Direct comparisons used scikit-learn 1.8.0 and seed 59
for outer and inner grouping. L1 tuning maximised mean inner ROC-AUC over
the seven-value grid, resolving exact ties in favour of the smallest 
.
All arms shared five patient-grouped outer folds within each cohort.
Sensitivity analysis repeated the procedure for the 102 matched patients.

The full learner retained the first 14 of the top 100 proteins ordered by
the inclusive 60th-percentile predicate, descending mean absolute SHAP,
then ascending differential FDR. The absolute-coefficient comparator
replaced 
 with 
 in the predicate and ranking while keeping
the clause search identical. The no-SHAP arm disabled the attribution
predicate, retained the non-zero-coefficient predicate, and selected 14
proteins by paired differential rank. The arm without differential
predicates retained candidate selection and restricted search to
fixed-protein terms. The single-threshold baseline used the full learner’s
candidates and medians to select the eligible fixed-protein term with
highest training F1. A depth-three tree (at least four samples per leaf)
and a 300-tree random forest used the L1 model’s preprocessed top-100
features, balanced class weights, and seed 42. Probabilistic models used
a 0.5 decision threshold. A channel-only control learned majority tissue
labels by TMT channel on the same training folds, using the training
class majority for unseen channels and majority voting across distinct
channels for replicated aliquots (ties favoured tumour).

For all methods, pooled out-of-fold tissue predictions within each cohort
yielded balanced accuracy and F1. Percentile intervals for F1 differences
used 1,000 patient-level bootstrap draws of held-out predictions (seed 42),
keeping paired tissues together. These intervals condition on the fitted
cross-validation predictions and exclude refitting variability. The
CPTAC-LUAD benchmark compared internally implemented baselines and
ablations. Published SHAP and ILP systems served as methodological
context. For the rule learner, we counted fixed-protein and relational
clauses and traced a held-out decision to its threshold and measured
abundance.

, absolute mean paired difference

Rule complexity and held-out trace:

In the matched rerun, each all-patient fold produced one clause with one
search term. Two used fixed proteins (HSPA12B and SHANK3), and three
used an existential protein variable, allowing one term to match multiple
eligible proteins. Two folds used low abundance, negative paired
direction, and a non-zero coefficient. One used the SHAP predicate.
All five paired-only folds yielded fixed-protein thresholds.

In all-patient fold 2, the HSPA12B clause fired at abundance

, the training-derived threshold. One held-out tumour
had measured abundance 
 without imputation, satisfying the
low-state predicate and yielding a tumour prediction. The supplement
provides the fired-rule witnesses used to reconstruct this decision.

4 
Discussion

Patient grouping and training-only preprocessing and selection support
internal evaluation of the paired-proteomics, linear SHAP, and ILP
workflow 
[
6
, 
9
]
. Training-derived evidence
makes each clause auditable. Full-cohort differential results, SHAP
plots, and the high-abundance clause are descriptive. Four proteins
retained non-zero coefficients across all five classifier folds, while
the most recurrent original clause appeared in two rule folds.
Repeated grouped resampling and external evaluation are needed to
assess stability.

The 102 matched pairs reduce between-patient variation, with protein-specific pair counts varying because of missingness. Nine tumour-only patients contribute to classification alone. The paired-only analysis provides a sensitivity check for excluding those nine patients. Tumour purity, tissue composition, stage, and batch can affect abundance. Channel alone nearly matched the proteomic classifiers, so high internal scores cannot establish discrimination independent of channel. Screening asymptomatic individuals, early detection, malignant versus benign discrimination, plasma markers, prognosis, and treatment response remain untested. Clinical utility also requires independent and prospective validation, a clinical operating threshold, and decision analysis.

The recurrent proteins HSPA12B, PALM2-AKAP2, STARD13, and PPM1F and the SHAP rankings generate hypotheses for tissue research. The study lacks pathway enrichment, protein-complex analysis, systematic literature comparison, and experimental corroboration. Follow-up should include targeted mass spectrometry in an independent LUAD cohort, GO/Reactome/KEGG enrichment against an appropriate protein background with multiple-testing correction, and comparison with established CPTAC-LUAD findings 
[
3
]
. Explainable multidimensional plasma proteomics offers a complementary research direction 
[
4
]
. Independent verification should precede evaluation in settings such as benign versus malignant lesions or blood-based testing.

The matched results qualify the role of attribution in the workflow. The zero percentile cutoff made the inclusive predicate constant among all ranked proteins, while ranking still limited the candidate pool. Absolute-coefficient substitution reproduced the clauses and predictions in both analyses. The no-SHAP arm removes the predicate and changes candidate ordering, so its performance difference reflects the combined change. Signed SHAP values can still communicate sample-level contributions to the fitted logit. The study did not assess their value to human readers. The bootstrap intervals describe uncertainty conditional on the fitted out-of-fold predictions and omit variability from refitting. The reported two-of-five exact-clause recurrence is an observation from one realisation of the grouped resampling design. A single split seed per cohort provides no estimate of how recurrence varies across alternative fold assignments.

5 
Conclusion

The analysis supports the feasibility of a three-stage explainability workflow that links patient-matched differential proteomics, sparse logistic regression with linear-model SHAP attribution, and FOL/ILP rule induction under patient-grouped evaluation. The workflow achieves high held-out discrimination for tumour versus adjacent-normal tissue within the CPTAC-LUAD cohort, but the results do not establish a clinically useful biomarker or generalisable diagnostic model. Exact symbolic rules show limited recurrence across the five outer folds. External cohort validation, repeated grouped resampling, biological pathway annotation, and experimental follow-up are necessary next steps.

Absolute L1 coefficients reproduced the SHAP-guided candidate sets, clauses, and held-out predictions in both analyses. Its paired-only comparison produced identical predictions from the full learner and a single-protein threshold. The held-out decision trace demonstrates how the rule representation exposes the threshold and evidence used for a classification.
