Abstract

Visuomotor policies trained through imitation learning are highly sensitive to changes in camera viewpoint.
This is particularly problematic for mobile manipulation (MoMa), where robots rarely execute manipulation from the exact same base pose used during demonstration collection due to navigation uncertainty, localization drift, and environmental constraints.
Recent approaches use novel-view synthesis (NVS) to augment demonstrations with unseen viewpoints, but they assume static scenes and therefore fail to re-render the manipulated object during the post-contact interaction phase, leaving the most critical parts of the demonstration unaugmented.
We present 
MoMaSplat
, a novel-view demonstration augmentation framework for mobile manipulation that synthesizes full manipulation trajectories from unseen robot base poses, including dynamic object interactions.
Our key insight is that standard MoMa data collection naturally provides the multi-view observations needed to reconstruct both the static scene and the manipulated object in 3D.

MoMaSplat
 combines 3D Gaussian Splatting, feed-forward 3D foundation models, and kinematics-based action retargeting to separately reconstruct the scene and manipulated object, render them from novel viewpoints, and generate geometrically consistent synthetic demonstrations paired with transformed actions.
We evaluate 
MoMaSplat
 on five real-world mobile manipulation tasks involving both rigid and articulated objects across three scenes, and validate the fidelity of its synthesized demonstrations against ground truth in simulation. 
MoMaSplat
 substantially improves robustness to unseen base poses, more than doubling the average success rate of the strongest baseline and maintaining non-trivial success at large base displacements where all baselines fail, while reducing the need for large-scale real-world data collection.

I 
INTRODUCTION

Imitation learning has emerged as a powerful paradigm for training visuomotor policies that perform complex manipulation tasks directly from image observations 
[
1
, 
2
]
.
However, these policies are notoriously sensitive to changes in camera viewpoint 
[
3
, 
4
, 
5
]
.
Even small deviations in camera pose can significantly degrade performance, since the policy learns a narrow visual distribution tied to the demonstrations used during training.

This sensitivity is particularly problematic for mobile manipulation (MoMa).
Unlike tabletop manipulation setups, where demonstrations are often collected from a fixed robot camera pose, mobile manipulators rarely execute tasks from the exact same location at deployment time.
Navigation uncertainty, localization drift, obstacle avoidance, and imperfect planning routinely place the robot at different base poses relative to the workspace.
As a result, the robot observes the manipulation scene from viewpoints that may differ substantially from those seen during demonstration collection.
Robust deployment therefore requires visuomotor policies that generalize across a distribution of robot base poses rather than a single viewpoint.

A straightforward solution would be to collect demonstrations from many different robot locations.
However, large-scale real-world teleoperation is expensive and difficult to scale 
[
6
, 
2
, 
7
]
.
Recent work has therefore explored synthesizing additional demonstrations through novel-view synthesis (NVS), generating virtual observations from unseen viewpoints using real-world captures 
[
8
, 
9
, 
10
]
.
While promising, existing approaches assume a static scene and therefore fail during the most important part of manipulation: object interaction.
Since the manipulated object moves throughout the demonstration, prior NVS methods cannot consistently re-render the object from novel viewpoints during the post-contact interaction phase, restricting augmentation primarily to pre-contact motions or introducing geometrically inconsistent generations.

Our key insight is that standard mobile-manipulation data collection naturally provides the multi-view observations needed to reconstruct both the static scene and the manipulated object in 3D.
During navigation, the robot observes the scene from diverse viewpoints that enable scene reconstruction, while the manipulation demonstrations themselves provide object-centric observations that enable reconstructing the manipulated object and its motion.

We evaluate

Our experiments show that

•

A novel-view demonstration augmentation framework for mobile manipulation that synthesizes complete trajectories, including dynamic object interactions.

Substantial improvements in robustness to deployment-time viewpoint variation across multiple real-world mobile manipulation tasks.

III 
MoMaSplat

We aim to augment a mobile-manipulation dataset with synthetic demonstrations from unseen robot base poses. From source demonstrations at a few robot locations, 
MoMaSplat
 synthesizes observations and actions for nearby deployment-time poses never physically demonstrated.

To achieve this, 
MoMaSplat
 decomposes the environment into three components: i) a static scene model, ii) a dynamic object model for the manipulated object, and iii) the robot embodiment itself.
The scene and object models are reconstructed independently and aligned into a shared metric frame.
A key observation is that the navigation trajectories naturally provide the bridge between both reconstructions: during navigation, the manipulated objects remain static, allowing the object-centric and scene-centric trajectories to be consistently registered.

For augmentation, given a sampled robot base pose, 
MoMaSplat
 renders the scene, manipulated object, and robot from the corresponding viewpoint and retargets the demonstrated actions through kinematic transformations. The resulting observations and transformed actions form synthetic demonstrations that augment the dataset for policy training. Fig. 
2
 provides an overview of the full process.

III-A
 
3D Reconstruction

To synthesize demonstrations from unseen robot base poses, 
MoMaSplat
 requires a 3D representation that can be rendered from arbitrary viewpoints. A central challenge is that manipulation involves dynamic objects whose pose changes throughout the demonstration. Unlike prior novel-view synthesis approaches that model the environment as a single static scene, 
MoMaSplat
 reconstructs the static scene and manipulated objects separately, leveraging observations naturally produced during mobile-manipulation data collection. Navigation
trajectories cover the environment while the objects are static, and manipulation trajectories
capture the objects in motion.

3D Scene Model Reconstruction.

As the robot moves through the workspace, its camera naturally captures diverse viewpoints, providing the multi-view coverage needed for 3D reconstruction without external cameras or scanning. We therefore use 
 to build a photorealistic 3D scene model: we recover per-image camera poses with structure-from-motion via COLMAP 
[
46
]
, which also produces a sparse point cloud that initializes a 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) 
[
11

We optimize the Gaussians by minimizing a photometric loss between rendered and observed images. Since the scene is not perfectly static across navigation trajectories, transient content may appear between demonstrations; we adopt the uncertainty-aware formulation of WildGaussians 
[
47
]
, which down-weights inconsistent regions during optimization to yield a robust model 
. We additionally mask out pixels belonging to the manipulated object and the robot during scene reconstruction, preventing the object from being baked into 
 and avoiding a static “ghost” when the independently reconstructed dynamic object is later composited into the scene.

Structure-from-motion reconstructs the scene only up to an unknown global scale, while our framework transforms metric actions and therefore requires a metric reconstruction. We recover the scale using the robot’s onboard depth sensor: for the frames in

V 
Conclusion and Limitations

Conclusion.
 We propose 
MoMaSplat
, a framework for novel-view augmentation of mobile manipulation demonstrations that reconstructs the static scene and manipulated object in 3D, then re-renders full demonstrations, including the post-contact interaction phase, from unseen base poses. Unlike prior NVS augmentation methods that model only the static scene, 
MoMaSplat
 produces geometrically consistent observations across the entire trajectory. Across five real-world tasks in three scenes, this improves policy robustness, outperforming prior augmentation methods and maintaining performance beyond the distances where baselines fail. We will publicly release our code to support further research.

Limitations.
 
MoMaSplat
 assumes mostly static scenes and rigid, visible objects, making deformable objects, heavy occlusions, and texture-poor surfaces challenging. Furthermore, because 
 is reconstructed from pre-interaction navigation views with object masks excluded, regions occluded by the object in its initial state (such as the surface beneath a lifted bottle or the interior of an opened drawer or air-fryer basket) are never observed during scene reconstruction and remain unmodeled when disoccluded. It also depends on consistent open-world segmentation and produces non-physical demonstrations by replaying source object trajectories without modeling contact forces or dynamics. Finally, view quality depends on navigation-data coverage. Addressing dynamic backgrounds, deformable objects, physical contact, and cluttered scenes remains future work.

Baseline Details

In this section, we provide more details about the baselines we are using in our evaluation.

Source Only.
 This baseline trains the same Diffusion Policy 
[
1
]
 as we do, but using only the source demonstrations 
, without any augmentation. This allows us to quantify how well a policy trained only on the original base poses generalizes to unseen poses.

Action Augmentation.
 Following 
[
54
]
, we augment each demonstration by perturbing the proprioceptive state and the action labels with zero-mean Gaussian noise while leaving the image observations unchanged. This exposes the policy to small action variations, but because the images remain the same, this augmentation provides no information about how the scene looks from a different base pose. We use this data to train the same Diffusion Policy and use the same training schedule as we do with all other baselines.

[
