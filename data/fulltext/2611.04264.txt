Abstract

External text can affect a retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) system only after the retriever admits it to the model context. We stress this admission step while varying document content, attacker query information, per-target poison budget, and contamination scope. Across 623 Benchmarking Information Retrieval (BEIR) queries from SciFact and NFCorpus, five query-targeted documents per query reduce classical-retriever nDCG@10 by 40.2–61.5% in isolated-target stress. Full-query anchoring explains most Best Matching 25 (BM25) degradation, whereas appended text also shifts latent semantic analysis (LSA) and Hybrid rankings. A pretrained BGE-small validation reaches 100% target exposure for query-only 
 on both datasets; adding the injection payload changes SciFact nDCG only from 0.269 to 0.271 and lowers NFCorpus exposure slightly to 98.8%, indicating that query anchoring remains the dominant retrieval-side factor for this dense model. Query length materially changes the interpretation of partial-information attacks: on 162 long SciFact queries, two random terms cover 19.9% of eligible terms and still yield 39.5% Hybrid target exposure. Multi-target contamination adds cross-target interference. Overlap-based admission can remove full-query poisons but may sharply reduce clean retrieval utility, and maximal marginal relevance (MMR) leaves exposure unchanged under the tested configuration. The study measures retrieval-layer failures, not downstream instruction following.

Index Terms: 
retrieval-augmented generation, reliability, prompt injection, corpus poisoning, information retrieval, admission control

I 
Introduction

A retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) system selects evidence from an external corpus before passing it to a language model 
[
1
]
. Some indexed documents may be controlled by third parties. In 
corpus poisoning
, an attacker inserts adversarial material into these retrievable sources; a document resembling the user’s query may enter the top-
 context and displace legitimate evidence before any malicious instruction is executed.

For example, an attacker can publish a page repeating terms from a scientific question and append misleading text. Although unable to change the query or retriever, the attacker can influence which evidence is admitted. We measure that retrieval boundary, not downstream instruction following.

Prior work studies knowledge corruption, retrieval jamming, optimized triggers, and indirect prompt injection 
[
2
, 
3
, 
4
, 
5
]
. We isolate retrieval-stage effects that are difficult to distinguish when document content, query knowledge, poison budget, and contamination scope change together.

We organize the evaluation around three research questions:

RQ1.

What makes a poisoned document retrievable?

RQ2.

How does retrieval failure scale with attacker query information and contamination scope?

RQ3.

What does admission control trade for lower exposure?

Public Benchmarking Information Retrieval (BEIR) labels and Benchmark for Indirect Prompt Injection Attacks (BIPIA) payload text support matched content controls, isolated and multi-target contamination, graded query-information conditions, and post-filter measurements.

We measure ranking quality, poison exposure, retained evidence, safe retrieval reliability, and latency. Query-information analysis accounts for effective query length: two or four terms disclose different fractions of short and long queries. The experiments distinguish query anchoring, cross-target contamination, and the evidence cost of admission rules.

II 
Related Work

II-A
 
Indirect Injection

BIPIA benchmarks indirect prompt injection and associated defenses 
[
5
]
; InjecAgent extends indirect injection to tool-integrated agents 
[
6
]
. Those studies evaluate downstream instruction following. Here, BIPIA payloads are used as standardized appended content, and the measured endpoint is retrieval admission.

II-B
 
Retrieval Poisoning and Jamming

PoisonedRAG jointly optimizes retrieval and generation objectives 
[
2
]
, and AgentPoison targets agent memory and RAG knowledge bases with optimized triggers 
[
7
]
. Machine Against the RAG shows that retrieval disruption need not rely on instruction semantics 
[
3
]
. Chang 
et al.
 characterize a retrieval barrier and optimize triggers across modern embedding models 
[
4
]
. Here, query information, per-target budget, and contamination scope are varied directly so their retrieval effects can be measured separately.

II-C
 
Reliability and Admission Defenses

ReliabilityRAG uses document-reliability signals and provides stronger corruption defenses and robustness guarantees 
[
8
]
. We use an interpretable overlap rule to measure the trade-off between lower poison exposure and loss of legitimate evidence; maximal marginal relevance (MMR) 
[
13
]
 provides a diversity-reranking comparison. The measurement distinction is that the closest prior work primarily studies downstream attack effects or optimized retrieval disruption, whereas our evaluation makes query information and contamination scope explicit stress axes and reports poison exposure together with retained evidence and ranking utility.

IV 
Experimental Design

We instantiate the system model using public retrieval benchmarks, matched content controls, and isolated-target and shared-corpus stress. Clean-corpus retrieval statistics stay fixed so the comparisons isolate the effect of added documents.

IV-A
 
Datasets and Retrieval

We use the BEIR test splits of SciFact (5,183 documents, 300 judged queries) and NFCorpus (3,633 documents, 323 judged queries) 
[
9
]
. Retrieval uses Best Matching 25 (BM25) 
[
10
]
, 64-dimensional latent semantic analysis (LSA) 
[
11
]
, and reciprocal-rank fusion (Hybrid) 
[
12
]
. Hybrid uses RRF constant 60, fuses the top 100 BM25 and LSA candidates, and retains 50 candidates before the final top-10 stage. For external validation, we also evaluate the pretrained 33M-parameter 
BAAI/bge-small-en-v1.5
 bi-encoder 
[
14
]
 with normalized embeddings and cosine similarity, without task-specific training.

For isolated-target experiments, LSA is fitted once on the clean corpus and injected documents are transformed into that frozen space. BM25 likewise uses vocabulary, IDF, and average document-length statistics fitted to the clean corpus; poison documents are scored under those frozen statistics. Multi-target causal controls use the same clean-corpus statistics, so differences reflect added documents rather than retraining the representation.

IV-B
 
Content Controls and Stress Regimes

For each target query and 
, query-only poisons contain the target query without appended text; query+payload poisons append a BIPIA payload; fixed-neutral controls append deterministic benign filler of matched length. Payloads are sampled deterministically with replacement from four BIPIA text-attack categories (Base Encoding, Reverse Text, Emoji Substitution, and Language Translation) using seed 2026 and a query-specific hash. A stronger corpus-derived neutral condition uses five deterministic non-relevant clean-corpus snippets per target: each source has zero BM25 query score, is excluded from judged-relevant documents, and is truncated to the payload token length. This control estimates perturbation from appended corpus-like text without claiming perfect lexical, topical, or semantic matching to the payload.

In 
isolated-target
 stress, each query is evaluated against the clean corpus plus only its own 
 poisons. At 
, five targeted documents represent 0.10% of SciFact and 0.14% of NFCorpus. In 
multi-target
 stress, every evaluation query contributes 
 poisons to the same corpus; at 
, this yields 1,500 SciFact poisons (22.45%) and 1,615 NFCorpus poisons (30.77%). For the attacker-information axis at isolated 
, payload-only provides no query terms; two- and four-term conditions use either deterministic random eligible query terms or the highest-IDF eligible terms; full-query stress includes the complete query. Random-term selection uses a fixed query-specific SHA-256-derived seed; all term-budget conditions reuse the same per-query payload construction.

IV-C
 
Admission Controls, Statistics, and Latency

MMR reranks the same 50-candidate pool with 
, chooses the highest-relevance candidate first, and then trades normalized relevance against LSA-vector redundancy. Admission control uses IDF-weighted unique-query-term coverage 
 and rejects candidates with 
, where 
. Filtering scans the ranked 50-candidate list and continues downward until ten candidates pass or the pool is exhausted; shortages are reported explicitly. For clean, four-term, and full-query conditions, each threshold is evaluated on final top-10 nDCG, Recall, evidence availability, safe retrieval reliability, target exposure, and the fraction of queries with fewer than ten admitted candidates. We report the full frontier rather than selecting a test-set-optimal threshold.

Metrics are computed per query. Nonparametric bootstrap 95% confidence intervals use queries as the resampling unit; paired intervals compare query+payload with query-only and with corpus-derived neutral content. Latency uses 500 repeated 
 measurements per retriever/defense pair on an AMD EPYC 9V74 allocation with five visible CPU cores under Python 3.13.5, reporting absolute median and P95 milliseconds.

V 
Results

We first examine content controls for RQ1, then attacker information and contamination scope for RQ2, and finally admission trade-offs for RQ3. Each analysis measures the retrieved candidates rather than a generated answer.

V-D
 
RQ3: What does admission control trade for lower exposure?

Fig. 4: 
Hybrid four-term overlap-admission frontier for exposure and safe retrieval reliability.

Figure 
4
 summarizes the overlap threshold security–utility frontier in final retrieval quality. For full-query Hybrid stress, 
 removes target exposure on both datasets. This is an attack-matched rule: a document that repeats nearly all query terms is expected to have coverage near one, so full-query rejection at a 0.95 threshold is not itself evidence of a broadly effective detector. The informative result is the collateral cost. On SciFact, attack and clean nDCG under that threshold are both 0.440, close to the unfiltered clean 0.465. On NFCorpus, however, attack nDCG is 0.138 and clean nDCG is 0.134, far below the unfiltered clean 0.267; 3.4–4.0% of queries also retain fewer than ten candidates. The four-term condition is more difficult to filter selectively: at 
, target exposure falls to 5.3% on SciFact and 0.3% on NFCorpus, but clean nDCG collapses to 0.082 and 0.065. At 
, clean nDCG recovers to 0.440/0.134 while four-term exposure rises to 78.0%/22.3%. Across the sweep, stronger lexical rejection reduces exposure but also removes legitimate evidence, and the trade-off differs sharply between the two datasets.

MMR closes a different question. Under isolated full-query 
 Hybrid stress, target exposure remains 1.000 on SciFact and 0.910 on NFCorpus while nDCG changes from 0.183 to 0.234 and from 0.160 to 0.164; 
SafeRetrievalReliability
@10 remains 0 and 0.006. MMR therefore changes ranking utility without acting as an admission barrier here, while adding about 1.5 ms to median Hybrid latency on both datasets.

VI 
Discussion

VI-A
 
Document Content

Under full-query stress, BM25 is dominated by the query anchor, whereas LSA and Hybrid also respond to the appended text. The neutral controls show that this semantic-ranking shift is not specific to malicious instructions. The BGE-small replication extends the high-exposure result to a pretrained dense retriever, but its query+payload condition is no more damaging than query-only on SciFact and is less damaging on NFCorpus. This reinforces the distinction between query-relevant retrieval manipulation and downstream malicious-instruction semantics.

VI-B
 
Stress Intensity

Isolated 
 combines a small global poison fraction with five documents written specifically for each evaluated query. Multi-target 
 instead creates a broad saturation condition in which poison written for other targets also enters top-
. Query-information budgets require the same care: on short NFCorpus queries, two or four eligible terms often reveal nearly the whole query, whereas the long SciFact subset shows substantial exposure at 19.9–39.7% mean coverage. A single poison rate or term count therefore hides materially different retrieval conditions.

VI-C
 
Admission Policy

Lower exposure does not necessarily improve final retrieval if the same rule removes judged evidence or leaves too few candidates. The overlap sweep has no stable cross-dataset operating point: thresholds that strongly suppress poison impose large clean-task costs on NFCorpus. Attack-side exposure and clean-task utility need to be evaluated together.

VII 
Limitations

The study ends at retrieval, so exposure is not equivalent to downstream attack success. Query-aware stressors are transparent rather than stealthy or optimized, and isolated low-fraction experiments still grant strong per-target targeting. Corpus-derived neutral snippets improve content matching but do not perfectly match payload term frequency, topic, or semantics; the analysis attributes effects to query anchoring versus appended-content identity rather than to malicious meaning itself. Fixed two/four-term budgets are especially difficult to interpret on short queries, so the long-query analysis is reported separately. High-IDF conditions additionally assume access to clean-corpus statistics. Pretrained validation is limited to one compact BGE model and the three core clean/query-only/query+payload conditions; larger embedding models, alternative dense retrievers, and production vector-search stacks remain outside scope. BEIR qrels are incomplete, so evidence availability and 
SafeRetrievalReliability
@10 inherit judgment incompleteness. Threshold curves are measurement frontiers rather than deployment recommendations.

VIII 
Conclusion

The experiments provide direct answers to the three research questions. 
RQ1:
 Query anchoring explains most BM25 degradation; appended content can also shift LSA and Hybrid rankings without establishing a malicious-instruction-specific retrieval effect. The pretrained BGE-small check reaches 100% target exposure for query-only poisoning on both datasets, with no additional payload-specific penalty in the tested conditions. 
RQ2:
 Two randomly selected terms yield 39.5% Hybrid exposure on 162 long SciFact queries, whereas fixed term budgets can approximate full-query knowledge on short NFCorpus queries; broad contamination additionally causes cross-target interference. 
RQ3:
 Overlap admission suppresses full-query exposure but sacrifices clean ranking utility at dataset-dependent rates, and maximal marginal relevance (MMR) leaves exposure unchanged under the tested configuration. These are retrieval-stage findings, not downstream attack-success estimates.
