Abstract

Web agents require diverse, executable, and resettable
website environments for reproducible training and evaluation.
Human-assisted mirrors demand per-site engineering, while
generative construction can diverge from specified real websites.
Capture-and-replay preserves observations, with interaction
coverage constrained by captured content and replay capabilities.

We present 
WebAnything
, a fully automated framework that
reconstructs source-grounded, reusable Web environments
from real URLs without per-site manual implementation
or repair after initial setup and authorization.
An Explorer–Builder collects browser-observable structures,
resources, and interaction outcomes; an independent verifier
checks frozen candidates and guides local repair or
targeted source recapture.

We construct 244 website artifacts across 14 application
domains and 53 categories, supporting 12,984 executed
and accepted tasks.
Compared with human-assisted WebHarbor, 
WebAnything

achieves higher aggregate scores on three fidelity
metrics, with 12 wins, two ties, and no losses in a
blind audit.
Fine-tuning Qwen3.5-9B on 5,856 interaction
trajectories improves REAL success by 9.9 percentage
points, Odysseys-Easy Rubric Avg and Task Avg by
6.25 and 6.32 percentage points, and WebArena-Verified
Overall success by 13.33 percentage points under the fixed harness.

Web-Anything on GitHub

Introduction

Visual Web agents need stable, controllable environments in which they can explore, change state, recover from errors, and receive execution feedback. WebArena and VisualWebArena demonstrate the value of self-hosted websites for reproducible evaluation 
(
Zhou et al., 2024
; 
Koh et al., 2024
)
; WebGym and WebFactory further study scaling environments and interaction experience 
(
Bai et al., 2026
; 
Fan et al., 2026
)
. The real Web offers diverse structures and workflows, but live execution complicates state initialization, reproducibility, and safe isolation.

We investigate how specified real websites can be
automatically transformed at scale into
source-grounded, executable, and reusable Web agent
environments, without requiring manual
site-by-site reconstruction.

Existing approaches trade off 
automation, source fidelity, and interaction coverage
 (Figure 
1
). Human-assisted mirrors provide controllable, source-corresponding environments but require per-site engineering 
(
WebHarbor Team and Contributors, 2026
)
. Generative methods scale executable sites from specifications and references 
(
Fan et al., 2026
; 
Zhang et al., 2026
)
, but matching a specified source remains a distinct requirement. Capture-and-replay preserves source observations, with interaction coverage bounded by captured content and replay capabilities 
(
Shi et al., 2017
; 
Srivastava et al., 2026
)
; Weblica combines replay with source-grounded synthesis 
(
Kar et al., 2026
)
. Scaling real-website environments requires both source correspondence and flexible, repeatable interaction.

A. Generative
Construction

WebAnything
 combines source-grounded reconstruction, independent verification, and iterative refinement.

Two technical challenges arise. The 
Source-Grounding Gap
 concerns reconstructing structures, resources, and behaviors when private backends and website internals are inaccessible. Screenshots and model priors alone can omit state relationships. Browser observations must instead be organized into evidence that constrains implementation. The 
Verification and Feedback Gap
 concerns detecting and correcting reconstruction discrepancies. Builder self-checks can overlook source-inconsistent defects, and a discrepancy may reflect either faulty implementation or insufficient observation. These cases require different actions: local repair or additional source capture.

We present 
WebAnything
, a framework for 
scalable, source-grounded Web agent environment construction
. Starting from real URLs, a coupled Explorer–Builder captures page structures, resources, and before/after interaction observations, then implements executable local routes and state transitions. An Independent Verifier checks frozen candidates in a separate context against source evidence and site contracts. Evidence-supported implementation errors trigger local repair; unresolved evidence gaps trigger targeted recapture. Updated candidates are frozen and reverified before release within their declared scope. Fixed initial states, reset mechanisms, and task evaluators support repeated interaction and trajectory collection.

Our evaluation covers construction scale, environment quality, and downstream utility. The resource pool contains 
244 website artifacts
 across 14 domains and 53 categories. On five matched WebHarbor sites and 14 historical states, 
WebAnything
 achieves higher automated fidelity metrics and 12 wins, two ties, and no losses in a blind overall-fidelity audit. Separate interaction tests characterize functional coverage. Historical analysis identifies evidence-supported discrepancies in 
39 pairs across 22 sites
 and independently reverified resolution of 18 issue records across six sites. The environments support 
12,984 executed and accepted tasks
; SFT on earlier interaction trajectories improves Qwen3.5-9B on REAL, Odysseys-Easy, and WebArena-Verified Overall.

Our contributions are:

•

Fully automated, source-grounded reconstruction.

We introduce a unified Explorer–Builder that automatically
reconstructs executable Web environments from real URLs,
using browser-observable structures, resources, and behaviors
without per-site manual implementation or debugging.

•

Independent verification and evidence-aware refinement.

An independent verifier checks frozen candidates against
source evidence and site contracts, directing discrepancies
to local repair or targeted recapture, followed by
automated revision and reverification.

•

Scalable environment and task supply.

We construct 244 website artifacts across 14 domains,
supporting 12,984 executed and accepted tasks.
Historical construction records, independent quality
evaluations, and downstream SFT demonstrate automation,
source fidelity, and practical utility.

Methodology

WebAnything
 uses source evidence as the shared basis for construction and independent verification (Figure 
2
). An Explorer–Builder reconstructs executable local pages and states; verification feedback then drives implementation repair or targeted source recapture.

. The source is accessible through browser observations rather than private backend code. Its evidence is

3.2 
Source-Grounded Reconstruction

State-associated evidence.

For each visited state, the Explorer–Builder associates page structures, computed styles, element geometry, screenshots, and resource references. Actions are linked to subsequent visible feedback and state changes. Distinct interaction states at the same URL remain distinct evidence: a closed menu and its expanded state, for example, constrain different implementation requirements.

Evidence-constrained implementation.

Structural observations constrain layout and component relationships; resource observations ground fonts, images, and media; behavioral observations constrain navigation, filtering, and state modification. The Builder organizes these into local components, routes, and shared object relationships. Screenshot references thus complement structural and behavioral evidence. A specific discrepancy can be traced back to its associated page state and action. When available observations do not establish a required outcome, the system seeks additional evidence rather than treating an inferred behavior as observed.

Coverage policies.

Full Coverage
 expands accessible page types, interaction states, and neighboring branches from the entry URL and native navigation. 
Just-in-Time Coverage
 prioritizes dependencies of a given task set and expands them through construction and verification feedback. Both share the reconstruction and acceptance mechanisms, differing in exploration scope and expansion timing. Reported construction experiments use Full Coverage.

3.3 
Independent Verification and Evidence-Aware Refinement

Verification decoupling.

The Builder implements and self-checks a candidate; an Independent Verifier then examines the frozen implementation in a separate context. It obtains rendering and operation results anew and compares them with source evidence and the site contract. Applicable checks cover pages, controls, resources, workflows, persistence, and restoration. The Verifier cannot edit candidate code. Stateful tests operate through local UI/API interfaces and restore the initial state. Candidate freezing binds findings to the inspected implementation and prevents verification-time repair from changing the target.

Evidence-aware refinement.

The model diagnoses each discrepancy using check results, source observations, the contract, and runtime conditions. If existing evidence establishes an expected outcome that the candidate fails to reproduce, 
Local Repair
 returns the affected page or control, executed action, expected and observed outcomes, and supporting evidence to the Builder. If evidence is insufficient to establish the target outcome, 
Targeted Recapture
 forms a concrete observation request and revisits the relevant source region. Newly captured structures, resources, or behaviors constrain the next implementation.

For example, Figma’s authorized source capture established a canvas-anchored comment composer. Independent checks found mismatched placement and appearance, providing a concrete target for local revision. The Builder read the feedback, changed the editor markup and styles, and submitted a new candidate; independent checks then confirmed the composer’s geometry and visibility. Existing source evidence supported repair in this case; an unresolved observation question instead becomes a bounded recapture request (Appendix 
B.8
).

After either path updates the candidate, the controller freezes it again and the Verifier repeats affected checks and applicable regression tests. Verification therefore connects discrepancies to actionable revision, while source evidence constrains both initial reconstruction and subsequent refinement. Candidates passing checks within their declared scope can proceed to release. Implementation details and original prompts appear in Appendix 
B.4
.

3.4 
Environment and Verifiable Task Supply

Released environments package local implementations, resources, initial data, runtime configuration, and reset interfaces. Shared state connects related pages, and applicable creation, modification, and save operations affect later interactions. Fixed initial data and state restoration provide repeatable task starting conditions. The task pipeline generates instructions and matching success conditions for implemented capabilities. Agents execute tasks through a fixed browser-based computer-use agent harness shared across data collection and downstream evaluations; settings and execution budgets are specified in Appendix 
E.1
. Evaluators check final answers or terminal environment states. Accepted trajectories are converted into step-level training examples. Task generation, executed acceptance, trajectory collection, and training export remain separate stages and counting units; Section 
4.4
 reports their outputs.

Experimental Setup

Objects and baselines.

The resource inventory covers 244 unique website artifacts as of October 9, 2026. Full Coverage construction records for 85 sites support automation and expenditure analysis. Quality comparisons use frozen artifacts and an independent evaluation pipeline: five WebHarbor sites with 14 historical reference states, and five VeriEnv artifacts for offline interaction tests. Measurements.

Pixel Similarity, SSIM, and Text F1 measure source-state correspondence, aggregated within sites and then averaged equally across sites. A blind audit of the 14 WebHarbor-matched states uses final labels agreed through discussion by three annotators, with randomized anonymous mirror order. Interaction tests disable external network access and report capability coverage, executed-test success, and all-test pass, keeping their denominators distinct. Explicit adapters restore initial states. Historical verification analysis links self-checks, independent findings, and repair traces; its definitions appear in Appendix

Two disjoint collections contain 6,172 accepted tasks across 74 sites and 6,812 across 229 sites. Multiple trajectories or format variants can share a task ID. The newer 6,812 tasks are not used in this SFT experiment. 5 
Conclusion

We present 
WebAnything
, an end-to-end automated framework
for transforming real websites into source-grounded,
executable, and reusable Web agent environments.
Unlike human-assisted mirroring approaches, 
WebAnything

automates source exploration, evidence collection,
website reconstruction, independent verification,
and iterative refinement, eliminating the need for
per-site manual implementation and debugging in
our audited construction runs.
By grounding reconstruction in browser-observable
structures, resources, and interaction behaviors,
the framework reduces reliance on incomplete visual
references or manually specified workflows.

Structural, resource, and behavioral evidence guides
reconstruction, while independent verification
directs discrepancies to local repair or targeted
recapture.
This separation enables the system to
distinguish implementation defects from insufficient
source evidence and refine reconstructed environments
accordingly.
The framework constructs 244 website artifacts
across 14 application domains and 53 categories,
supporting 12,984 executed and accepted tasks.
Experiments demonstrate source fidelity,
operational validity, and verification-driven
refinement, while also highlighting the importance
of evaluating functional coverage independently
from visual similarity.
Furthermore, supervised fine-tuning on 5,856
interaction trajectories improves Web agent
performance on REAL, Odysseys-Easy, and
WebArena-Verified, demonstrating the downstream
utility of the constructed environments.

Together, these results establish a practical path
toward scaling real-Web agent environments with
minimal site-specific human engineering.
More broadly, 
WebAnything
 connects automated website
reconstruction with reproducible interaction and
training-data collection, providing a foundation
for scalable Web agent development and post-training.

Limitations

Training validation with large-scale models.

Our supervised fine-tuning experiments with Qwen3.5-9B demonstrate the learning value of interaction data from reconstructed environments for downstream Web agent tasks. However, current training experiments primarily concern the 9B parameter scale. We have not systematically evaluated these data with larger foundation models, different post-training methods, or larger training budgets. Future work can examine relationships among environment scale, data diversity, and model capabilities to assess this approach in larger-scale agent training.

Continuous real-Web updates and environment maintenance.

Real websites continually change their page structures, resources, and interaction behaviors. 
WebAnything
 currently reconstructs and verifies source states at particular times, without systematically evaluating the timeliness of local mirrors or long-term maintenance efficiency under continuing source changes. Future research can investigate source-change detection, incremental evidence capture, and local environment updates to reduce maintenance costs while preserving source correspondence.

Copyright, content authorization, and environment distribution.

High-fidelity reconstruction of real websites involves page content, images, fonts, and branding that may be protected by copyright, trademarks, and terms of service. Public accessibility does not necessarily grant permission to reproduce, modify, or redistribute content. Environment construction, research use, and public release must therefore comply with applicable content authorization and compliance requirements. Future releases can prioritize the general construction framework, evaluation protocols, and de-identified research data, while determining the distribution scope of website mirrors and original resources according to their licenses.

Ethical Considerations

Exploring, capturing, and reconstructing real websites raises issues concerning terms of service, intellectual property, privacy, and security. The system should process only websites covered by authorization, public licenses, or research agreements, avoid collecting account credentials, cookies, personal information, and payment content, and run released environments in isolated networks and sandboxes. For websites or tasks that could facilitate abusive automation, reconstruction scope, public content, and model capabilities should be restricted.

Appendix A 
Environment Construction and Quality Evaluation

The appendices provide protocols, implementation details, per-site results, and source inventories for the completed experiments.

A.1 
Historical Construction and Cost Records

Construction reports for the historical 85-site subset record 59 passed sites, 15 source-blocked sites, 6 failed sites, and 5 sites with missing reports. These statuses reflect different versions of acceptance criteria, visual algorithms, and thresholds and serve to trace historical construction processes. After initial setup and authorization, this subset contains no recorded per-site manual implementation, debugging, or repair. Conclusions about human intervention and pricing are restricted to this historical subset and the recovered calls.

This construction experiment used the Full Coverage policy. In the recovered SDK records, the Builder and Verifier primarily used 
gpt-5.6-sol
 with 
xhigh
 reasoning; some Builder calls used 
gpt-6-astra
, so the entire batch did not use a single model.

Recovered model-call and partial cost records cover 84/85 sites. Input tokens, including cached tokens, total 11.207 billion, of which 10.672 billion are cached input tokens; output tokens total 38.4318 million. Recorded, quantifiable model costs total $220.62, with recorded Builder and Independent Verifier costs of approximately $142.52 and $78.11, respectively. Across the 84 sites with records, the mean recorded per-site cost is approximately $2.63 and the median is approximately $1.60. Independently rounded components sum to $220.63, differing from the total by $0.01 due to rounding. Seven sites additionally contain unpriced Astra calls. These costs therefore provide only a lower bound for recovered, priceable model calls. They exclude unpriced calls, browser execution, and other system overhead, are not extrapolated to the total cost of 244 sites, and are not interpreted as the marginal cost of the Verifier under a controlled ablation.

Costs distinguish system development, initial authorization/setup, per-site implementation/debugging/repair, and research auditing. Neither the 244-site artifact pool nor the historical 85-site subset has a complete attempt cohort under uniform acceptance criteria, so we do not compute a unified construction success rate.

Freezing, execution, and denominator rules.

The resource inventory and environment-quality evaluation materials in this section use records frozen on October 9, 2026: resource scale comes from the frozen 244-site inventory, historical construction/cost audits from the 85-site subset, and quality results from two independent artifact comparisons. The construction-stage Verifier drives repair and recapture according to site contracts. The paper’s independent evaluation harness is separate: it neither reads historical verification conclusions nor modifies the artifacts under test, and instead retests each artifact using common metrics. Artifacts, historical references, and runtime configurations are frozen separately. All failures, 404 responses, source-reference defects, blocked tests, and missing references are retained; no composite environment-quality score is constructed.

Historical-state comparisons use the same designated reference, browser, and viewport, recording limitations in the comparability of page states, languages, and JavaScript rendering conditions. The evaluation harness is independent of construction methods and implementation stacks. State recovery uses explicit test adapters rather than requiring external artifacts to adopt 
WebAnything
’s Flask/SQLite architecture. Interaction tests disable external network access, use a fixed planned set, and record reasons for unexecuted and failed tests. A historical 
passed
 status cannot substitute for retesting.

Appendix B 
System Implementation and Verification Feedback

B.1 
Propagation of Evidence, Candidates, and Feedback

The Builder reads existing source materials, candidates, and the latest repair package in the workspace, preserving valid captures and implementations when resuming execution. The independent verification runner reads construction reports and evidence directories and returns verification reports and repair requests. The outer controller records execution statuses and sends failed reports back to the Builder; source blocking and authorization requirements are handled separately. Task generation, interaction collection, and evaluation use frozen environments.

B.2 
Version Association

Sites, source snapshots, candidates, and release packages have separate stable identifiers. Version associations for tasks, trajectories, and evaluation results should identify specific implementations, resources, initial data, and runtime configurations rather than relying solely on site names. Training and evaluation protocols and data-overlap checks appear in Appendix 
E.1
.

B.3 
Site Contracts and Verification-Report Fields

The inspected contracts associate sites, coverage configurations, viewports, page paths, workflows, and acceptance requirements, with optional interaction contracts and state assertions. Specific checks are determined by the corresponding execution reports.

Independent verification reports record conclusions, candidate digests, page and workflow checks, source limitations, and evidence. Repair packages associate issue locations, expected and observed outcomes, corresponding evidence, and repair suggestions. The model determines diagnostic actions using the reports and existing source evidence.

B.4 
Construction-Stage Verifier Implementation and Versions

The construction-stage Verifier uses site contracts, model-executed browser tests, and site-specific assertions. Visual comparison may combine screenshots, DOM, element geometry, and auxiliary scripts. Historical versions did not use a unified SSIM, LPIPS, or L1–L4 composite score. Fidelity v19 introduced normalized RGB pixel similarity, but algorithms and thresholds still differ across sites.

Reset prechecks include programmatic hard checks dependent on 
WebAnything
’s Flask/SQLite architecture; they are not stack-independent external-baseline scorers. Earlier runners did not enforce positive- and negative-case calibration acceptance criteria for evaluators. Task-evaluation calibration therefore requires independent evidence.

Reports preserve concrete check evidence, with top-level conclusions distinguishing passed, failed, and source-blocked outcomes. The outer runner consumes reports rather than recomputing a unified four-layer score. The historical artifact pool contains candidates from different versions with passed, failed, source-blocked, and missing-report statuses. The independent evaluation harness retests frozen artifacts without reading these historical verification conclusions.

B.5 
Resumption Prompt for the Explorer and Builder

The Builder Agent directly receives a real website URL and manages exploration, evidence collection, and local reconstruction within the same context. The historical resumption template requires preserving existing source evidence, candidate implementations, and repair records, reading the latest check feedback, and continuing repairs from failed or unfinished items. It returns to the source for targeted recapture only when existing evidence cannot answer a concrete question. Its final submission comprises the candidate implementation, local resources, and a self-check report containing actual check results and limitations. This template resumes checkpoints after timeouts; it is not the sole initial prompt shared by all construction batches.

The complete historical English template appears in the separate prompt supplement; this description does not replace the original instructions.

The Independent Verifier inspects frozen candidates in a separate context without modifying the Builder’s candidate code. It performs actual browser checks based on source evidence and site contracts, covering applicable pages, controls, resources, state persistence, and recovery, and produces feedback containing concrete failure locations, expected outcomes, observed outcomes, and evidence. Stateful checks modify only local runtime states and restore initial states before finishing. Candidates modified by the Builder must be frozen again and independently reverified.

Appendix C 
Task Supply and Data Statistics

C.1 
Interfaces for Downstream Task Generation, Evaluation, and Collection

Task and training-data construction use released environments. This section describes the implementation interfaces for task specifications, runtime recovery, evaluation, and data binding.

From environment capabilities to tasks.

The task pipeline reads the pages, visible objects, native operations, and seed data of a specific mirror to formulate goals such as retrieval, filtering, detail verification, comparison and aggregation, and state modification. Existing planning materials record whether the required facts reside in lists, detail pages, or other pages, and organize applicable operations into task specifications. Natural-language instructions express user goals, while answers, evidence supporting the operations, and evaluation materials remain hidden. The executable scope of a task is bounded by the capabilities and initial state actually supported by the environment.

Initial states and success conditions.

Public task records contain task identifiers, instructions, sites, starting URLs, and login and reset requirements. Task specifications record capability tags, action and step budgets, and successful and unsuccessful terminal states. Hidden specifications associate required objects, query parameters, source evidence, expected results, and output contracts. Success conditions for information tasks may specify fields, sets, orderings, or comparison results in normalized answers. State-changing tasks check application states after saving, creating, modifying, or restoring objects; tasks admitting multiple solutions use sets of state predicates. When necessary, fictional test records are prepared in private copies of native data structures, while original environment assets remain read-only. Task answers are grounded in the task’s initial data. Task text or a nonempty answer file alone does not establish evaluability: corresponding executable checks must exist.

Evaluator construction and execution-based verification.

During generation, site-specific scripts first check native routes, evidence visibility, and applicable answer, state, and reset conditions. A visual agent then receives instructions and observations in the initial state of the corresponding mirror through the fixed harness and performs browser operations. An independent task evaluator checks the final answer against hidden success conditions and determines goal completion using the terminal DOM, screenshots, and applicable state evidence. Model-based evaluation is used for applicable semantic conditions and is separated from agent execution. Tasks with evaluation materials become end-to-end accepted tasks only after actual interaction and outcome checks, rather than receiving a pass solely because specifications exist.

Recovery, binding, and training entry points.

Task execution packages locate native implementations, immutable seeds, and starting routes through runtime descriptions. When login is required, they restore fictional seed identities while isolating real user sessions. Before task execution, mutable databases are restored from seeds, and site-specific reset and startup checks verify applicable states. Tasks whose runtime prerequisites are not met are recorded as blocked. Task identifiers, source-data versions, and specification digests connect public tasks to hidden evaluation materials. Collection packages record implementations, resources, initial data, runtime configurations, and archive digests to associate the complete environment identity.

The pipeline separately records task generation, evaluator construction, execution-based acceptance, trajectory collection, and training-sample export. Saved successful trajectories are used for training after quality filtering, format conversion, and step-level sample export. The 6,172 accepted tasks in the earlier source collection, the 5,856 trajectories used for SFT, the 5,167 unique training task IDs obtained by deduplicating task IDs in the training manifest, and the 60,517 step-level samples are counted in their respective units. The relationship between the separate batch of 6,812 new tasks across 229 sites and the 74 source sites follows the set definitions in the experimental setup.
