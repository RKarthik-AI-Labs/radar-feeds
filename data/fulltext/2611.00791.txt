Abstract

As consumer smartphone recommerce scales, buyers must quote binding repurchase prices from seller-uploaded multi-view photos and diagnostic screenshots 
before
 devices reach the warehouse.
Accurate remote valuation requires fusing noisy cosmetic evidence with identity, battery, lock, and channel risk from screenshots and staff forms, since an error in any modality changes both grade and payout.
However, existing multimodal resources mainly target general VLM competence, screen reading, or factory defect detection, and largely overlook staff-aligned trade-in fields, dual appearance/staff ground truth, and executable valuation scorecards.
This gap stems from two challenges: (1) the lack of large-scale real trade-in data with audited dual annotations and a compact ranking benchmark kept separate from training labels, and (2) the need to jointly reason over unordered multi-view cosmetics and diagnostic screens under operational deduction rules.
To address these challenges, we propose 
PhoneValue-Bench
, a dual-GT evaluation suite with tier-gated scorecards, together with 
PhoneValue-Corpus
, a matched weakly labeled training set of over twenty thousand orders and hundreds of thousands of captioned images.
We further present 
ValueAgent
, a staged perception–fusion–valuation reference pipeline aligned with production inspection, plus an executable public scorer and submission schema.
Experiments show that ValueAgent reaches a measured 84.1/100 on Bench-Full, with 97.0 appearance fill and 54.1 staff-field fill, establishing a practical dual-GT benchmark for multimodal recommerce valuation.

Introduction

The rapid growth of consumer smartphone recommerce has made remote device valuation a central operational problem for trade-in platforms.
Before a phone arrives at the warehouse, buyers must issue a binding repurchase quote from seller-uploaded multi-view photos and system diagnostic screenshots.
Over-quoting creates inventory loss and reverse logistics cost; under-quoting drives seller churn and marketplace distrust.
Compared with warehouse quality control under controlled lighting, remote valuation must fuse noisy cosmetic evidence with identity, battery, lock, and channel risk that live in screenshots and staff notes.
A missed hairline crack, a misread capacity, or an ignored carrier lock can each change both cosmetic grade and payout.

As a result, assessing multimodal systems for recommerce quoting has emerged as a natural research direction.
Existing evaluation resources, however, have mainly focused on broad vision–language competence, document or screen reading, and industrial defect localization 
(
Liu et al. 2024
; 
Yue et al. 2024
; 
Mathew et al. 2021
;

; 
Bergmann et al. 2019
; 
Zou et al. 2022

.
General suites such as MMBench and MMMU probe open-domain multimodal reasoning but do not score staff-aligned trade-in fields.
Document and screen VQA 
(
Mathew et al. 2021
; 
Hsiao et al. 2025
; 
Singh et al. 2019
)
 and mobile GUI corpora 
(
Deka et al. 2017
; 
Rawles et al. 2023
)
 emphasize reading or controlling interfaces rather than graded resale pricing.
Industrial anomaly and glass-defect datasets 
(
Bergmann et al. 2019
; 
Zou et al. 2022
; 
Höfer et al. 2025
; 
Han et al. 2023
)
 provide factory-captured boxes or masks, and recent tool-use agents combine OCR with multi-step form reasoning, yet these lines still stop short of an executable, auditable valuation protocol for used devices.

Remote recommerce valuation is not equivalent to factory defect detection or screenshot QA.
Valuation quality depends on dual evidence: appearance judgments grounded in multi-view cosmetics, and staff-facing fields that decide quote eligibility, including model, capacity, lock status, and no-quote blockers.
A system may correctly narrate scratches in free text and still fail the operational scorecard if it misses a view, emits the wrong grade taxonomy, or cannot reconcile screenshot identity with visual wear.
This distinction matters in practice: warehouses need per-item fill that can be audited, not a single opaque price regression.

We believe public progress on this task is inherently challenging.
First, there is no compact audited benchmark that couples multi-view trade-in photos and diagnostic screenshots with dual appearance/staff ground truth under tier-gated scoring rules.
Second, scaling requires a large weakly labeled corpus, but training labels must remain strictly separated from ranking gold to prevent leakage.
Furthermore, effective systems must jointly handle unordered seller photos, diagnostic screens, and conflict resolution under production-style deduction logic, which single-turn bag-of-images prompting alone rarely achieves.
Addressing these challenges would enable fair agent and VLM comparison, support detector and fusion research, and give warehouses a transparent protocol for remote quote quality.

In this paper, we propose 
PhoneValue-Bench
, a public multimodal benchmark for consumer smartphone recommerce valuation, together with 
PhoneValue-Corpus
 and the staged 
ValueAgent
 reference pipeline.

 summarizes data packaging; Figure

Our contributions can be summarized as:

•

PhoneValue-Bench.
 We release a dual-GT evaluation suite with tier-gated scorecards, an executable public scorer, and Check/Full splits so that only the audited Full set defines the leaderboard.

•

PhoneValue-Corpus.
 We construct a matched large-scale weakly labeled training set of 21,550 orders and 278,748 English-captioned images, kept separate from ranking gold for detector and VLM pretraining.

•

ValueAgent and evaluation protocol.
 We present a six-stage perception–fusion–valuation reference aligned with production inspection, and show that it reaches a measured 84.1/100 on Bench-Full, establishing a practical baseline for future multimodal recommerce systems.

Stage 1 (Preprocess).

Seller uploads arrive unordered and often poorly framed.
Stage 1 detects the phone region, then crops and rotates each image into six canonical views 
 (front, back, four edges/sides).
Canonicalization directly supports Y01 (view coverage) and makes regional defect scoring (Y04–Y07) well-defined: side scratches are scored on side views rather than being confused with background clutter on a single front photo.

Stage 2 (Object detection).

A pluggable detector (YOLO 
(
Sapkota and Karkee 2025
)
 in reported runs) takes 
 and outputs boxes 
 plus a detector cosmetic grade 
.
These buckets map to scorecard slots and are deliberately distinct from the GT-Y taxonomy in Step 3; Y08 scores agreement after the Appendix A crosswalk.
Stage 2 is the main appearance engine: without it, bag-of-images prompting rarely recovers stable view and regional F1 fills.

Stage 3 (Scene compliance).

Outdoor or street backgrounds are rejected; store-style captures pass.
The gate does not rewrite defects; it suppresses clutter-induced false positives before VLM reasoning and is a precision lever rather than the primary recall driver.

Stages 4–5 (VLM internal recognition).

Stage 4 reads About and settings screenshots to extract identity fields such as model, capacity, and lock status.
Stage 5 reads diagnostic screenshots for battery health and related internal status.
Both stages output structured JSON that fills the corresponding staff scorecard items.
Repair-history text is not handled as a separate vision stage; it is fed into the Expert Knowledge Base so that prior water events or replacements can override overly optimistic visual grades.

Expert Knowledge Base and fusion.

The knowledge base stores identity priors, risk items (battery, foreign parts, locks), and abnormal phrases mined from staff text.
Fusion applies deterministic priority rules over detector outputs, VLM JSON, and metadata: (i) screenshot identity overrides conflicting detector view labels; (ii) cosmetic ceilings follow the worst-scoring body region; (iii) water or activation-lock evidence from any modality propagates to S08/S10 blockers.

5 
Conclusion

We release PhoneValue-Bench, Corpus, a public scorer, and ValueAgent as a staged reference for the valuation of smartphone recommerce. Our core contribution is an auditable dual-GT protocol with Corpus/Bench isolation and tier-gated 
. On Bench-Full, ValueAgent achieves 84.1, with Y-fill 97.0 and S-fill 54.1. This indicates appearance items are largely solved while staff-field reading trails significantly. The wide gap between Y-fill and S-fill suggests that warehouse-style form parsing, rather than cosmetic grading, remains the primary bottleneck in automated device valuation. This substantially outperforms the strongest baseline at 69.9, and ablations confirm the gain comes from staged perception and fusion. By providing a frozen scorer and strict benchmark separation, PhoneValue offers a stable, reproducible foundation for future research on recommerce automation. We invite submissions evaluated exclusively on Bench-Full under the public protocol.

A.2 
Annotation protocol and quality audit

Bench labels follow a fixed warehouse-QC protocol rather than free-form browsing.
Eight trade-in QC annotators use a written guideline covering view names, defect classes, staff-field definitions, and inactivity conditions.
The guideline is treated as part of the benchmark surface: if two annotators disagree, the resolution path is written down in advance rather than negotiated ad hoc after seeing model outputs.
They first mark which of the six canonical views are present and readable, then draw defect boxes only on present views, then fill staff-aligned fields from diagnostic screenshots and intake forms.
Drawing boxes on absent views is forbidden, because it would create phantom GT that later makes Y04–Y07 unfairly harsh on systems that correctly leave those views empty.
When seller free text conflicts with screenshots, screenshots take priority; blank fields are preferred to speculative completion under insufficient evidence.
This ordering matters analytically: free-text seller claims are noisy and sometimes adversarial, whereas About/diagnostic screenshots are closer to the identity evidence warehouses actually trust for payout.
Preferring blanks also protects S09/S03 inactive logic: a guessed channel or IMEI would convert an inactive item into a hard mismatch and distort fill statistics.

A 10% slice is double-labeled independently (35/335 Full; 50/500 Check) before senior adjudication.
On that audit subset, Cohen’s 
 is 0.81 (GT-Y severity), 0.88 (view presence), 0.89 (model), 0.94 (capacity), and 0.86 (lock), supporting dual-GT stability for ranking while retaining exact-string S04.
Capacity agreement is highest because GB/TB tokens are nearly discrete; severity and lock are lower because boundary wear and lock-banner readability are inherently softer.
These 
 values are not claimed as universal human performance ceilings; they are evidence that the released dual GT is stable enough for leaderboard use under the published taxonomy.
About 7.4% of dual-labeled items require adjudication (mainly boundary severity or missing side views); adjudicators may only select published taxonomy buckets, which prevents silent invention of new grade names that would break S04 comparability.
Appearance/staff severity conflicts occur on 12.1% of Full orders, for example, vision GT may record minor wear while staff records a stricter payout grade after intake notes.
Analytically, S items follow staff priority for S04–S10 (payout alignment) while Y items retain vision GT for Y04–Y08 (visual auditability), so the scorecard can expose localization competence and staff-form fidelity without collapsing them into one compromise label.
If the benchmark forced a single severity label, either the detector community or the screenshot-reading community would be silently advantaged.
The dual-GT design is therefore not redundancy: it is the mechanism that lets ValueAgent show 97.0 Y-fill alongside 54.1 S-fill without averaging them into an opaque single score, and that lets future systems improve one modality without claiming victory on the other.

Readers debugging a submission should walk at least one order from each severity band (pristine, moderate wear, heavy damage) against these panels before interpreting aggregate

Stages match the main-paper pipeline and exist to make Det/VLM/Fuse ablations interpretable rather than to invent a proprietary agent brand.
If the pipeline were a single black-box VLM call, removing “perception” would be undefined; with explicit stages, the main paper can report Det/VLM/Fuse as orthogonal factors and show that the largest drop comes from Det, then VLM, then Fuse.

We report nonparametric bootstrap confidence intervals for the measured ValueAgent scorecard on Bench-Full.
Each of

Stratified intervals are computed independently inside each stratum rather than by post-hoc slicing of the all-set resamples, which keeps coverage meaningful for smaller crack/scrap and family subsets.
