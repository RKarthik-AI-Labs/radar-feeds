Abstract

Backpack language models predict with a strictly linear combination of
non-contextual per-word sense vectors, weighted by non-negative
contextualization scores. We show that non-negativity conflates two
properties: the linear readout, which carries the interpretability, and the
conic combination, which merely restricts it. We relax the latter by computing the sense weights with differential
attention, a difference of two softmax maps with a learned per-sense gain,
so context can actively inhibit a sense; we call the result a differential
Backpack. At equal parameters on WikiText-103 this cuts the Backpack’s
perplexity gap to a Transformer by 
, improves over the softmax
Backpack at every sense count, persists with a shrinking margin up to

M parameters on OpenWebText, and is localized at the sense layer by
ablations and controls. The learned inhibition is
directly interpretable (a financial context drives the river sense of

bank
 to 
), and the preserved per-sense attribution predicts
counterfactual removals exactly. We tested a number of combination rules, showing the linear interface is nearly free. Making the readout nonlinear gains only 
 perplexity
points while invalidating two
thirds of the decomposition. The differential Backpack’s senses are also better
representations, improving zero-shot sentence similarity by up to 
.

1 
Introduction

Most interpretability for language models is post hoc. The model is
trained for performance and reverse-engineered afterwards. Backpack language models

(
Hewitt et al., 2023
)
 take the opposite route, building an
interpretable interface into the architecture. Each vocabulary item is
assigned 
 non-contextual sense vectors, and the model’s
prediction at every position is a linear combination of the sense vectors
of the preceding tokens (Eq. 
1
). Because the readout is
linear in the senses, the contribution of any sense of any word to any
prediction is an exact, inspectable quantity, enabling sense-level
control such as debiasing by down-weighting a targeted sense.

Backpack language models have one notable limitation. The contextualization weights that
combine senses are softmax outputs, hence non-negative, so context can
select a sense or ignore it but never oppose one. When 
bank
 appears in a
financial sentence, its river sense does not simply go unused. Left
un-suppressed, it leaks into the prediction. A non-negative combination has
no mechanism to cancel this wrong-sense contribution.

This is the setting differential attention was designed for. The DIFF Transformer 
(
Ye et al., 2025
)

computes attention as the difference of two softmax maps, so that scores
can be negative and common-mode “attention noise” cancels. In a Backpack model,
noise can be seen as the contribution of the wrong word sense, and the
difference of softmaxes is a mechanism for sense inhibition. We adopt it
without giving up the interface, because non-negativity conflates two
distinct properties: the
linear readout, which carries the interpretability, and the
conic (non-negative) combination, which merely restricts it. We keep the first and relax the second.

We call this differential sense contextualization
(Figure 
1
). On WikiText-103
at equal parameters it recovers 
 of the perplexity gap
between the softmax Backpack and an unconstrained Transformer, and the
improvement persists, with a shrinking margin, up to 
M parameters on
OpenWebText. An ablation shows the gain is
specific to the sense-combination layer, exactly where the
non-negativity constraint binds; placing differential attention in the
underlying Transformer instead does not help, and the learned inhibition
is directly interpretable (§
4
).

Our contributions are:

•

We introduce differential sense contextualization: the Backpack’s
non-negative sense weights become the difference of two softmax maps
with a learned per-sense gain, so context can inhibit a sense while the
readout, and hence exact per-sense attribution, stays strictly linear.

•

At equal parameters the differential Backpack outperforms the softmax
Backpack on WikiText-103 at every sense count and at 
M and 
M
on OpenWebText. An ablation localizes the gain at the sense layer, and
three controls that each remove one ingredient recover at most 

of it and an existing signed rule none, so the gain is caused by the
differential form of inhibition.

•

We make the expressivity of negative attention

(
Kong et al., 2025
)
 precise for sense combination,
characterizing which outputs each rule can reach and showing the
constraint binds at the sense layer but not in a Transformer, with a
separation that is exact at one sense head and decays with more, which
we confirm.

•

We examine a number of combination rules that decompose the Backpack’s perplexity
tax. Only

is depth that the sense dictionary’s parameters displace; at matched

sentence representations we measure at

2 
Related Work

Backpack language models.

Backpacks 
(
Hewitt et al., 2023
)
 were introduced as an
architecture coupling Transformer-level performance with a linear,
sense-level interface, and demonstrate sense-based control including
targeted debiasing. Follow-up work extends the architecture to
character-level Chinese 
(
Sun and Hewitt, 2023
)
, uses sense finetuning for
model editing from canonical examples 
(
Hewitt et al., 2024
)
, adapts
senses across languages 
(
Cruz et al., 2026
)
, and intervenes on
senses to debias retrieval 
(
Afzali et al., 2026
)
. All of this work keeps the non-negative contextualization weights; we
modify that mechanism itself, narrowing the perplexity gap to Transformers
while preserving the sense interface these methods rely on. A complementary line
asks whether the interface must be trained in at all, inducing sense
pathways into frozen pretrained models 
(
Cruz and Aji, 2026
)
; we find that senses read off a trained Transformer’s output-value (OV) circuits

(
Elhage et al., 2021
)
 are weaker than learned ones on every lexical
benchmark we test.

Differential attention.

The Differential Transformer (DIFF Transformer, 
Ye et al., 2025
) computes attention
as a difference of two softmax maps to cancel common-mode noise, and has
prompted gated 
(
Lygizou et al., 2025
)
, parameter-shared 
(
Cang et al., 2025
)

and thresholded 
(
Huang et al., 2026
)
 variants. Cog Attention

(
Lv et al., 2024
)
 obtains negative weights by a different rule,

 times a softmax over 
, so a row’s absolute weights sum to one (we test it at the sense layer in
§
4.4
); the Integral Transformer

(
Kobyzev et al., 2025
)
 argues that both routes risk discarding
useful tokens. A subsequent study finds the DIFF Transformer’s
advantage stems from negative-attention expressivity and reduced head
redundancy rather than denoising per se, with modest gains at small scale

(
Kong et al., 2025
)
. Our results agree that differential attention
helps at the sense layer, not in the underlying Transformer. None of this
work places signed attention
at a linear, inspectable readout, where its expressivity can be characterized exactly (§
3.3
).

Word senses as explicit vectors.

Representing a word by several vectors instead of one has a long history:
multi-prototype and non-parametric sense embeddings cluster a word’s
contexts and learn a vector per cluster

(
Reisinger and Mooney, 2010
; 
Neelakantan et al., 2014
)
, a
literature surveyed by 
Camacho-Collados and Pilehvar (2018)
. Those
senses live outside the language model and are selected one at a time by a
similarity rule, whereas a Backpack makes them components of the model’s own prediction.
Unsupervised sense induction still fails to beat a one-cluster-per-lemma
baseline even with modern language models 
(
Mosolova et al., 2026
)
, so an
architecture that carries an explicit inventory is still a live design
choice rather than a legacy of pre-neural lexical semantics. Differential contextualization adds
the operation that selection-based schemes structurally lack: a sense can be scored below zero, not merely passed over.

Interpretability by architecture.

Our work sits with approaches that make a model interpretable by
construction, not by post-hoc probing. The contrast is sharpest
against attention weights, contested as explanations because the map from
weights to outputs is nonlinear and non-unique

(
Jain and Wallace, 2019
; 
Wiegreffe and Pinter, 2019
)
.
Backpack sense weights are not proxies in this sense. They multiply fixed
vectors under a linear readout, so a weight is a contribution. A
second line accepts the trained network as given and recovers an
interpretable basis after the fact, from superposition analyses

(
Elhage et al., 2022
)
 to sparse dictionary learning over
activations 
(
Huben et al., 2024
)
. Our ladder bears on that choice. The cost of a Backpack is its fixed
per-token dictionary, not its linear readout.

Why the constraint binds only in Backpacks.

Non-negativity is a weak constraint in a standard Transformer. Signed
value and output projections let a head realize subtraction downstream, so
negative attention adds little the architecture cannot already route
around. A Backpack is different. Its sense-combination layer is the single
point where context integrates lexical information, over frozen,
non-contextual sense vectors the model cannot reshape, so non-negativity
binds (there is no way to express “not this sense”) and relaxing it adds expressivity that has no substitute. This
predicts that differential attention should help at the sense layer but not
in the underlying Transformer, which our ablation confirms

5 
Conclusion

We keep the Backpack’s linear readout, which carries its
interpretability, and relax the conic combination that merely restricts
it, computing sense weights as a difference of two softmax maps so that
context can inhibit a sense. At equal parameters this cuts the gap
to an unconstrained Transformer by 
 on WikiText-103 and persists to

M on OpenWebText with a margin that shrinks with scale, with an
ablation localizing the gain at the sense-combination layer. Because the
readout stays linear, the attribution predicts counterfactual removals
exactly, the inhibition is directly interpretable, and it doubles as a
control surface. Suppressing a
targeted sense cuts gender bias by up to 
 at negligible cost. The
senses are also better representations, improving sentence similarity by
up to 
 over the softmax Backpack.

A ladder of combination rules then sharpens what the architecture costs:
making the readout nonlinear gains 
 ppl, improves no probe beyond
noise and
invalidates two thirds of the decomposition, while the

that survives is the depth displaced by the sense dictionary’s parameters;
at matched depth the differential Backpack trails a Transformer by 

ppl. Since exact attribution requires only a linear readout over a fixed
inspectable basis, and nothing of how the coefficients are computed, the
natural next step is a global overcomplete dictionary. Non-negativity was never the price of interpretability, and
neither is linearity.

Size and cost of the effect.

The perplexity gains, while consistent, are modest in absolute terms:
differential contextualization reduces the Backpack’s gap to a Transformer

Interpretability evidence.

The sense-level interpretations, though exact by construction, are cleaner

and does not claim every sense is human-labelable, and the inhibition

Scope.

All experiments are on English, and signed weights forgo the “conic
selection” reading of the original Backpack even though exact per-sense
attribution is retained.

Ethics Statement

The debiasing experiment (§
4.9
) measures gender bias as
the gap between the log-probabilities of 
he
 and 
she

after 
 profession nouns in six templates. This binary, English,
pronoun-based measure is a standard probe of one salient bias and not a
measure of fairness: it omits non-binary identities, other languages, and
the many forms bias takes beyond pronoun prediction, and reducing it does
not make a model unbiased. Sense suppression is an inference-time edit
that can remove a targeted association; the same mechanism could be used
to insert one, which is a reason to prefer architectures whose edits are
inspectable, as the linear readout makes ours. All models are trained on
public corpora (WikiText-103 and OpenWebText) that contain the biases of
their sources; we release no new data. Training used a single H200 GPU
for roughly one GPU-week in total.
