Abstract

Influence-function-based methods like MAGIC provide exact first-order scores for trajectory data attribution, yet only retraining indicates whether those scores accurately predict data removal. We evaluate MAGIC on 
M- and 
M-parameter GPT-2-style models learning multistep arithmetic from scratch on filtered web text and four synthetic math families. Full-run attribution at 
M suffers from numerical overflow, and even with metagradient clipping, scores correlate near chance with retraining (linear datamodeling score, LDS, 
–
). In contrast, short checkpoint windows yield stable, accurate predictions at both scales (LDS 
–
 across four 
-step windows at 
M), whereas a single-checkpoint gradient-dot baseline shows near-zero correlation. Rankings of individual training blocks predict retraining only within that single run and do not transfer across seeds or hyperparameters (

–

 score tails, reflecting strong bidirectional local sensitivity distinct from net benefit under multi-epoch family deletion. These results show that trajectory attribution is reliable within short, validated windows and yields family-level insights that generalize across runs.

5 
Discussion

Window attribution measures how strongly individual blocks perturb held-out loss
along a single trajectory, whereas multi-epoch deletion tests macro-scale
reliance when an entire family is withheld. Worked examples and primitive facts
populate both the most helpful and most harmful 
 tails while keeping
near-zero mean scores, reflecting wide block-to-block variance rather than
uniformly positive updates. Because worked examples match the fixed evaluation
format, their sensitivity captures both arithmetic composition and prompt
formatting.

Limitations.

Most window configurations use one seed, restricting cross-run claims to the
family level: per-block rankings are trajectory-specific and do not transfer. In
the 
M grid, batch size and step count co-vary, and web deletion raises the
arithmetic share. We make no cross-scale capability claims: two-phase 
M
runs saw fewer math tokens than 
M runs, and 152M web+math uses a single
parent seed under a different window configuration. Arithmetic accuracy holds
only within the trained numeric range, and TrackStar and SOURCE exceeded our
compute budget (Appendix 
B.7
).

6 
Conclusion

Short-window MAGIC attribution predicts retraining upon removing data during
capability acquisition, but full-run unrolling overflows and clipped full-run
attribution fails validation. Within-run rankings are trajectory-specific and
fail to transfer, but the family-level tail patterns replicate across runs.
These patterns represent local bidirectional sensitivity, which is different
from the multi-epoch net impact. Thus, trajectory attribution is most useful
when used within a validated window and interpreted as a family-level effect.

specification-then-work format the models train on.
Decoding is greedy, and scoring is exact match.
Results are reported both over all items and restricted to items inside each
model’s training specification, because the two differ substantially.

Number-size range evaluation.

Single-operation problems are generated and swept across operand magnitude and
multiplier size to locate where each model’s arithmetic fails.
The sweep covers addition and subtraction from one to seven-digit operands, and
multiplication across multiplicand digits and multipliers from two to
ninety-nine.
