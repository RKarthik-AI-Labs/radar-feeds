Abstract
The correspondence of Heinrich Bullinger (1504–1575), Swiss Reformer and Antistes of Zurich, docu-
ments the political and religious debates of the Reformation, including extensive appeals to the Church
Fathers as authoritative sources. Detecting such references computationally remains largely unexplored.
We investigate dense and sparse retrieval for patristic reference detection, and metadata-informed topic
modelling as a proxy for the topics and ideas discussed. Prior work on Latin intertextuality has fine-tuned
denseretrieversfortheirrespectivetargetdomain,andwetesthowwellthesemodelstransfertoBullinger’s
correspondence. We compare lexical (BM25), dense (bi-encoders), and hybrid methods on explicit and
implicit references. A Latin encoder fine-tuned on biblical reuse performs best among the bi-encoders
(82%Recall@20 onexplicitand16%onimplicitreferences),whileBM25outperformsdenseapproaches
on explicit citations (94%,R@20). Because BM25 and dense retrieval recover disjoint sets of implicit
references, fusing their rankings raises implicitR@20 to 22%, close to cross-encoder reranking of the
same candidates (26%) but without its inference cost. For topic modelling, we extend FASTopic with a
seed-guidedlossfunctionandeditorialabstractembeddings. Measurablecoherencegainsoverthebaseline
remain minimal, but qualitative and temporal analysis shows that some topics track documented historical
events. However, dominanttopicweightsbarelyexceedauniformdistribution, makingtopicsunusableto
inform retrieval. A pilot application of LLM-based concept induction (LLooM) further points towards a
morehuman-interpretablealternative. Together,theseresultspresentafirstapproachtoretrievingpatristic
referencesandmodellingfine-grainedtopicsinBullinger’scorrespondence.

2.2 Topic Modelling in Historical Sources
Topicmodellingaimstoidentifylatentthematicstructureindocumentcollections,surfacingtopics
as ranked word sets or document clusters. A central challenge is interpretability, since without
ground-truthlabelstopicsareonlyusefulinsofarastheyformhuman-coherentgroupings[ 18;19].
Statistical approaches such as Latent Dirichlet Allocation (LDA) [10] remain the field’s
baseline but struggle with multilingual, non-standardised historical sources [13].5 Bag-of-Words
(BoW) models yield usable topic representations for historical (Latin) text but lack coherence
[26; 41]. Neural topic models instead leverage pretrained embeddings. BERTopic [28] provides
coherenceandyieldsusabletopicsonclassicalLatin[ 41]andBullinger-eracorrespondence[ 59],
but approximates document-topic assignments via cluster membership [26] and suffers from
hyperparameter sensitivity and topic collapse [63]. FASTopic [64] addresses this by embedding
documents, topics, and vocabulary words in a shared semantic space, reducing the risk of topic
collapsewhileretaininginterpretabletopic-worddistributions.
To recover fine-grained domain-relevant themes that standard models dilute into dominant,
coarse patterns [36], seed-guided approaches inject small sets of anchor keywords to bias topic
discovery [35; 65], an approach shown to bridge computational output and domain-specific re-
searchquestionsinhistoricalandsocial-sciencecorpora[ 34;55].
Evaluation in the absence of ground truth relies primarily on coherence metrics [19], though
these remain difficult to compare across methods [63] and must ultimately be validated through
expertqualitativereview[ 41].
Morerecently,approacheswithlargelanguagemodels(LLMs)generatehuman-readabletopic
labelsandkeywordseitherdirectlyorfromstatisticallyorneurallyderivedworddistributions,such
as TopicGPT [30]. LLooM [38] is a method that synthesises sampled text passages, generates
concepts based on pipeline generated criteria, applies those concepts to the data, and iteratively
developsbroader,higher-levelabstractionswithLLMs.

4 Methodology
4.1 Dense Retrieval of Patristic References
Following Allenbach et al. [2], we can define patristic text reuse as a textual unit within a letter
in which the author “refers, explicitly or implicitly, through quotation, mention or reminiscence,
to one or more parts” (p. 20) of patristic writings. Within such a unit, the writer’s argument and
thepatristicmaterialcanbeinterwoven,withverbaloverlappotentiallybeingminimalorentirely
absent. Atthesametime,evenasingletermmayconstituteapatristicreferenceandthereforeform
areuseunit[ 33].
When patristic text reuse detection is framed as an IR task [16; 40; 43; 52], we utilise text
segments from the BDC as query documentsq and text segments from sources in the PSC as
candidatedocuments d. TheIRtaskobjectiveisretrievingthemostsemanticallysimilardocuments
foreachquery,rankedbyarelevancescore s(q,d).
For both corpora, we combine sentence-level segments [16; 52] with 3-sentence overlapping
windows to capture references spanning sentence boundaries (cf. Table1). Fixed windows were
rejectedastheydonotalignwithsemanticunits[ 27;40].
We evaluate three bi-encoders, one monolingual and two multilingual, each available as
a base model and as a variant fine-tuned for (Latin) intertextuality. This lets us isolate how
far task-specific fine-tuning transfers to a new domain. The base models areLaBERTa [47];
SPhilBerta [48], a cross-lingual sentence encoder for Latin, Ancient Greek, and English; and
modern multilingual text encodermultilingual-e5-large [61], previously applied to the
Bullinger corpus [59]. Their fine-tuned variants are LaBERTa-W_VULG-S_VL [16] (hereafter
LaBERTa-VL), chosen despite its monolingual scope because it was trained on the related task of
biblicalreuseinpatristictexts,andtheLociSimilesmodels[ 52].
Given the query setQ and candidate databaseD, the three models produce embeddings¯q =
fθ(q) ∈ R768/1024 forq ∈ Q and ¯di = fθ(di) ∈ R768 fordi ∈ D where fθ represents the
respectiveencoder. Cosinesimilarityquantifiesquery–candidaterelevance.
Bi-encodersimilaritysearchusesFAISS; 16 theLociSimilespipelinehandlescandidategener-
ation internally via ChromaDB. For cross-encoder reranking, we use the LociSimiles classifier
PhilBerta-class-latin-intertext-v2 [52], which jointly encodes query-candidate pairs
and classifies each as intertextually related or not, requiring one forward pass per candidate. In
two-stage pipelines, the bi-encoder retrieves the top-100 candidates per query, which the cross-
encoderthenreranks.

For topic modelling, we use FASTopic [64], which derives topic-word and document-topic
distributions from a shared embedding space while retaining an interpretable BoW represen-
tation.17 We restrict BoW input to POS-tagged nouns and proper nouns [59; 64], lemmatised
and lowercased with LatinCy (la_core_web_lg) [14] for Latin, spaCy (de_core_news_lg)

outputs.19 We drop terms appearing in fewer than ten documents, yielding an effective vo-

6 Discussion
6.1 Dense Retrieval
Overall,thedenseretrievalresultsfallbelowtheresultsreportedinotherintertextualitydetection
studies [16; 52]. The performance gap is potentially attributable to several factors. First, the re-
spectivefine-tuningofthemodelsfortheirtargetdomainandtask;second,thetemporallinguistic
distance between early modern Latin used by the Reformers and late/vulgar Latin of the Church
Fathers;andthird,thegenreshiftofepistolaryexchangeandpatristicsourcetexts. Cross-encoder
rerankingoverthestrongestbi-encodertop-100improvesrecallforimplicitreferences,consistent
with prior findings that reranking improves retrieval quality [44; 52]. However, cross-encoder
inference is computationally expensive at full corpus scale. For explicit citations, dense retrieval
falls ≈10-12% points behind BM25, which also recovers implicit paraphrases. Dense retrieval
contributeswherevocabularydivergesasitfindsimplicitreferencesthataresemanticallycloseto
theirsource. FusingbothrankingsviaRRFthereforeoffersacompetitiveandlesscostlyalterna-
tive to cross-encoder reranking, although on our evaluation data the gain amounts to only a few
references.

7 Limitations
Regardingtextreuse,afirstlimitationconcernstheinterpretabilityoftheencoderrepresentations
underlyingtheretrievalpipeline. AsD’Angelo,Taddei,andLenci[ 21]note,computationalmod-
els “cannot distinguish between coincidental similarity, shared literary tradition, and intentional
allusion” (p. 8); passages surfaced by the system require humanistic interpretation before any
grounded claim of patristic influence can be sustained. Following Riemenschneider and Frank
[48], the pipeline is best understood as a first-pass exploration tool. Ground truth evaluation is,
furthertothelimitationsdiscussedinSection 3.3,constrainedbythesizeoftheevaluationdataset,
withn = 100 limiting the statistical robustness of the reported retrieval metrics. The absence of
afalsepositiveevaluation,anassessmentofhowoftenthepipelineretrievespassagesthataresu-
perficiallysimilarbutphilologicallyunrelated, meansthatprecisionmetricscannotbeestimated.
Itisalsoworthnotingthatmultilingualreferencesremainanopenquestion,asonlyoneoutofnine
non-Latin-to-Latinevaluationreferencewasrecoveredbythemultilingualbi-encoder.
Severallimitationsalsobearontheinterpretationofthetopicmodellingresults. Persistentjunk
clusters appear across all four configurations with overlapping vocabulary. The degree to which
seedingandregestaugmentationconstructoraltertopicstructurecannotbecleanlydisentangled.
The regest interpolation weight of 0.5 was set for practical reasons without systematic tuning,
and the direction of the bias it introduces, whether it over- or under-weights scholarly context, is
unknown.
8 Future Work
Several directions present themselves for future work. Fine-tuning the language models used for
retrievalondomain-specificannotationsfollowingCaffagnietal.[ 16];Schelbetal.[ 52]andoth-
ers represents a natural next step for enhancing dense retrieval. Training data for that could be
readily constructed from the numerous references annotated in the HBBW. Further, the role of
named-entityinformationfromthelettersandfurtherintegrationoffeature/metadatadeservesys-
tematic investigation [50] as direct patristic references are frequently mediated through person
mentions. Segmentation strategy equally warrants empirical scrutiny, as does the introduction
of metadata-enriched segmentation [39], such as citation markers or discourse cues, which may
improveretrievalperformanceoverthecurrentsegmentation. Thoughcostly,LLM-assistedrefer-
encedetectionorinterpretation[ 23;60]offersanalternativeorcomplementaryapproach,provided
hallucinationrisksaremanaged.
Asalreadybroughtupinthediscussion,topicmodellingcouldbeexploredfurtheralongsev-
eral axes. One option would be to incorporate the per-letterBullinger Digitalcategory tags as a
signal instead of using them only as topic seeds. The effect of restricting the vocabulary to Latin
onlyversusENHGonlycouldlikewisebeinvestigatedfurther. Adirectionworthexploringisalso
a finer-grained application of LLooM to the corpus. Our pilot applied concept induction at the
letter level, where documents often exceed several hundred words and address multiple topics at
once (reflected in the 177 bullet points generated from only 50 letters). Lam et al. [38]’s original
validation was done on short, self-contained, topically coherent social media posts. Feeding full
letters into the pipeline may therefore understate LLooM’s capacity for specific, idea-level con-
cepts. Since a subset of letters includes scholarly regest annotations in which regest paragraphs
alignwithspecificparagraph-levelspansintheletterbody(markedvia div/@corresp intheTEI
encoding), segmentingalongtheseboundarieswouldyieldshorter,moretopicallycoherentinput
closertoLLooM’sintendedgranularity. Followingthesamelogicbehindusingregestembeddings
inFASTopic,futureworkshouldcompareconceptinductiononregest-segmentedtextagainstthe
same segments augmented with their corresponding regest text, to isolate whether segmentation,
regestaugmentation,ortheircombinationimprovesconceptspecificityandinterpretability.

Moregenerally,across-corpusevaluationontheThBWcouldassessthemethodologicalgener-

9 Conclusion
We investigated the degree to which computational methods can detect patristic references and
modeltopicsasproxiestotheologicalideasinHeinrichBullinger’s16th-centurycorrespondence.
The best-performing domain-adapted Latin encoder,LaBERTa-W_VULG-S_VL , performs better
than other bi-encoders, while BM25 beats all dense approaches on explicit citations. The hy-
brid RRF improves implicit recall over either method alone at minimal additional cost. Topic
modellingoverthe Bullinger DigitalcorpuswithFASTopicrecoversthematicallydistinctand,in
some cases, historically coherent patterns. Neither metadata-informed configuration is reliably
superior to unmodified FASTopic. Gains remain difficult to assess. Although we aimed to use
topic-letter distributions to potentially inform reference retrieval, dominant topic weights barely
exceed a uniform distribution, suggesting that the current topic representations are too diffuse to
informretrieval. Nevertheless,connectingretrievalwithtopicsremainstheintendedroadmapfor
futureworkoncetightertopicmodellingapproachesareestablished. ApilotapplicationofLLooM
suggests a more interpretable, criterion-based alternative to keyword topics at additional compu-
tational cost. Together, these results position patristic reference detection as tractable for explicit
citationbutharderforimplicitreferences,withhybriddenseandsparseretrievalandfuturedomain
fine-tuning as promising paths to narrow that gap. Topic modelling offers a productive but still
coarse-grainedproxyfortheideasdiscussedduringtheReformation. Bothpointtothepotentialof
computational methods to facilitate large-scale exploration of Bullinger’s correspondence, while
highlightingtheneedforfurthermethodologicalrefinement,morerobustevaluationandphilolog-
icalvalidationtoestablishmorereliableconnectionsbetweenpatristicreferencesandtheological
ideas.
