Abstract.

Financial research agents can generate and monitor trading rules faster than a
human can document the resulting selection process. Candidates appear at
data-dependent times, weak rules are abandoned, and promising ones are watched
until they cross a reporting threshold. We introduce 
Promote
, a protocol that
assigns every candidate predictable testing wealth at birth and accumulates a
cost-aware e-process only from subsequent returns. If lifetime allocations sum
to at most one, a weighted Ville argument bounds the probability of ever
promoting a rule whose declared bounded net score has always had nonpositive
conditional mean. The result permits dependent candidates, adaptive births,
and indefinite monitoring. We audit the implementation in two settings. A
retrospective pseudo-out-of-sample replay contains 24 panel–rule
hypotheses, 3,000 shared-sign null paths, injected alternatives, and public
ETF and industry data. Repeated unadjusted tests produce a family crossing in
94.5% of null paths, versus 0.0% for 
Promote
; at a 15-bp aligned
ETF-component perturbation, 
Promote
 has 70.1% target power and median
crossing session 2,327. A separate exact mean-zero experiment lets an
agent select candidate identities and birth times from past scores; the funded
gate has 1.77% false-family crossings across 3,000 programs.
The market replay produces zero promotions after costs. The
certificate controls historical false promotion under the declared null; it is
not a certificate that an earlier edge remains profitable at deployment.

1. 
Introduction

A conventional backtest has a nominal end date.
Research rarely does.
An analyst looks at interim results, changes the universe, adds a seasonal interaction, drops an unpromising rule, and continues when the evidence is close.
An autonomous research agent can perform the same sequence at machine speed.
Treating the final rule as if it came from one fixed test understates the number of opportunities the system had to be lucky.

This failure is familiar in empirical finance.
Data-snooping corrections compare many strategies on a common terminal sample 
(
White, 2000
; 
Hansen, 2005
; 
Romano and Wolf, 2005
)
, while empirical-finance protocols quantify selection and backtest overfitting 
(
Harvey et al., 2016
; 
Bailey et al., 2017
; 
Arnott et al., 2019
)
.
Neither device, by itself, authorizes continuous inspection of a library whose membership is decided from the observed history.
The relevant error event is operational: 
did the research process ever promote a strategy whose declared net score has no positive conditional mean?

Test martingales and e-values are well suited to this question because they remain valid at stopping times 
(
Ville, 1939
; 
Shafer et al., 2011
; 
Vovk and Wang, 2021
; 
Ramdas et al., 2023
)
.
Their use still requires a protocol.
A newly proposed rule must not inherit evidence from before it was specified; adding near-duplicates must consume a finite research budget; transaction costs must be known before the return used to update the test; and trading must begin after, rather than on, the boundary-crossing observation.
Those details are where an apparently safe backtest can leak.

Promote
 turns them into a ledger.
At a predictable birth time, candidate 
 receives initial testing wealth 
.
Subsequent bounded, cost-adjusted payoffs update an e-process with bets chosen from the past.
Promotion occurs at the first crossing of 
.
If the total wealth assigned over the life of the research program is at most one, a weighted Ville argument controls the probability of any false promotion by 
.
The library may be dependent, monitored at different frequencies, and expanded in response to old data.

Contributions.

The statistical ingredients are established; our contribution is to make them
operational for an adaptive financial-research workflow:

•

a pathwise testing-wealth ledger that covers dependent, predictably
registered candidates and continuous monitoring;

•

a cost-aware implementation that separates pre-registration data,
evidence, promotion, and next-session execution; and

•

matched fixed-family and adaptive-birth audits that expose false-family
error, detection delay, and the economic weakness of the historical library.

The protocol tests an executable bounded payoff, not a semantic strategy story
supplied by an agent. Its certificate concerns the complete promotion history,
including rejected ideas; the market replay is a deliberately narrower
pseudo-out-of-sample special case.

3.3. 
Promotion is not same-period trading

The crossing observation belongs to the test, not to post-promotion performance.
If

This one-period separation prevents the return that triggers promotion from being counted as evidence of deployed profitability.
Statistical wealth is also not trading capital:

For a promoted rule, we therefore maintain two ledgers.
The evidence ledger continues to update (
3

Demotion can be governed by a separately registered rule, but optional removal does not retroactively change the false-promotion guarantee.
Production use therefore requires a distinct current-regime or forward
deployment check. A predictable epoch restart can provide one, but it must be
funded as a new test; deleting old factors from the original product would break
anytime validity.

3.4. 
State, corrections, and recovery

Each update reads the committed state at 
, joins one point-in-time outcome,
and writes one state for 
. Its unique key combines the candidate
fingerprint, event time, and data-version fingerprint. Replaying a key after a
crash returns the stored row instead of multiplying an e-factor twice; partial
writes are not evidence.

Event time and ingestion time remain separate. Revised data or code cannot
silently overwrite an old factor: the ledger retains the original row, flags
the certificate, and computes a versioned diagnostic. Any corrected series
used for authorization starts prospectively as a newly funded stream. Thus a
reviewer can reconstruct what the gate actually knew when it acted.

Automated checks enforce idempotent replay, no influence from post-
 inputs,
and cumulative birth debits that never decrease or exceed one. These target
double counting, look-ahead joins, and budget recycling.

4. 
Experimental Design

4.1. 
Retrospective pseudo-out-of-sample library

The implementation crosses three asset panels with eight calendar definitions,
giving 24 panel–rule hypotheses. This is the fixed-family special
case of Theorem 
2
: within the replay, all candidates are frozen
before the 2015 segment is processed and receive equal wealth.
Adaptive births and data-dependent allocations are not exercised by the market
replay; Section 
4.3
 isolates them under an exact mean-zero
score law.
The panels are six equity ETFs (SPY, QQQ, IWM, EFA, EEM, and VNQ), five defensive or diversifying ETFs (IEF, TLT, GLD, DBC, and SHY), and the ten value-weighted Fama–French industries.
The rules are turn of month, first three and last three trading days, Monday, Friday, last three trading days of a quarter, January, and the third Friday of a month.
The common daily sample runs from March 2006 through May 2026.

For rule indicator 
, the candidate takes the approximately calendar-neutral panel exposure

split equally across the assets in its panel.
The activation frequency 
 and direction 
 are fixed on March 2006–December 2010.
The predictable score scale is the larger of 50 bp and the training 99.5th percentile of absolute net return.
The 2011–2014 segment records stability and component-bet diagnostics, but does not choose the test bet or alter the family.
Every test stream uses the predeclared equal mixture in (
4
) and restarts at one in January 2015.
The replay code does not use a post-2014 return to change these choices; the
overall split and library were nevertheless designed retrospectively.

The replay segment covers January 2015 through May 2026.
Candidates divide wealth equally, so each boundary is

All strategies pay five basis points per dollar of one-way risky-asset turnover.
The code solves the proportional-cost self-financing equation against drifted pre-trade weights rather than subtracting cost from stale targets.
At each date it records exposures and costs, observes the return, updates evidence, and schedules any promotion for the following trading day.

4.3. 
Adaptive-birth boundary-null audit

The fixed market replay cannot demonstrate the adaptive-birth part of
Theorem 
2
. We therefore add a separate exact boundary-null
experiment. Each of 3,000 research programs contains 32 independent
Rademacher score streams over 768 sessions. After a 64-session warm-up, an
agent inspects the past only and registers the unregistered stream with the
largest trailing-64 sum whenever that sum is at least 20. Births are separated
by 32 sessions and capped at 12. The selected identity and birth time are thus
data dependent, but its first test score is the next independent Rademacher
increment, so

 exactly after registration.

At each birth the agent predictably spends one half of remaining wealth when
the trailing sum is at least 28 and one quarter otherwise. Consequently the
pathwise lifetime allocation never exceeds one even though candidate identity,
birth time, mass, and final family size vary by path. We compare the funded
boundary with an unfunded per-stream boundary of 20 and with repeated one-sided
Gaussian tests after 32 post-birth observations. The simulation uses seed
20262731 and the same fixed e-mixture as the market study.

4.4. 
Public-market replay

The market study uses a July 31, 2026 frozen snapshot: Yahoo adjusted closes for
the ETFs and the Ken French Data Library’s value-weighted ten-industry file.
Source and derived-file SHA-256 hashes accompany the artifact; the replay itself
performs no network retrieval.
Returns, candidate indicators, costs, and evidence are computed from information available at each decision.
The study reports the entire promotion ledger, including candidates that never cross.
The split and library were designed retrospectively, and trading-day ranks are reconstructed from the realized exchange calendar.
No test return calibrates direction, scale, bet grid, or prior wealth, but one historical path cannot establish the conditional null in (
2
).

For any promoted strategy, exposure begins one interval after crossing and pays exact one-way turnover under the declared cost convention.
Program-level wealth includes cash before activation; the complete daily ledger permits a separate live-only analysis when a crossing occurs.

5.3. 
Market evidence and the finance ledger

The pseudo-out-of-sample market run promotes zero of 24 candidates.
This count should not be read as a rejection rate under a known stochastic null: market returns need not satisfy (
2
), and the experiment supplies only one dependent path.
Its role is to show how the protocol behaves when familiar strategy families compete for the same research budget.

The largest evidence value reached by any candidate is 1.93, versus the declared boundary of 480.
Repeated unadjusted inspection instead promotes the reversed Fama–French industry January rule on 13 January 2016.
Its full 2015–2026 shadow path loses 0.90% per year (Sharpe 
); the fixed 
 sleeve activated by the naive gate ends at 0.992 after costs.
These figures do not prove that the crossing was a statistical false positive, but they show why a nominal 
 crossing is a poor deployment event.

Only two of the 24 full-test shadow paths have a positive arithmetic mean, and the larger is 0.087% annualized; neither comes close to the boundary.
This is a weak library after the retrospective split, not evidence that the gate suppresses profitable rules.
Conversely, any promoted rule has an audit trail identifying the exact observations, score scaling, cost, bet, and boundary responsible for the decision.
This is more informative than reporting only the winning backtest.

5.4. 
What the artifacts establish

The rowwise replay verifies three deterministic invariants: the 24 fixed-family
weights sum to one, every test e-process restarts at one, and no promoted return
is credited before the interval following a crossing. The adaptive simulation
checks variable birth times, identities, masses, and family sizes under an exact
mean-zero law. Seeds 20260731 and 20262731, the complete hypothesis ledger,
summary tables for 15,000 simulated programs or paths, and SHA-256 hashes of
every machine-readable result accompany the artifact.

These checks do not establish a stable positive conditional mean for a market
rule. Power depends on clipping, effect persistence, family size, and bet
fractions. Changing those choices after seeing the replay creates another
research layer that requires new validation data or testing wealth.

6. 
Relation to Existing Work

Backtest overfitting has been studied through data-snooping tests, superior-predictive-ability tests, multiple-testing adjustments, and selection diagnostics 
(

;

.
These methods address closely related selection problems, usually for a collection evaluated at a terminal sample size.
Our target is the event generated by an ongoing research system: a false strategy is promoted at some random time after a data-dependent proposal.

Repeated interaction with a holdout set is adaptive data analysis; the
reusable-holdout framework gives a privacy-based route to valid reuse

(

. Asynchronous online testing handles overlapping
sequential experiments 
(

Online multiple-testing methods allocate error across a sequence of hypotheses,

(

; 
Javanmard and Montanari, 2018
; 
Ramdas et al., 2018
)
.
Our pathwise masses are deliberately simpler: they are a weighted online

That conservative choice makes the audit rule transparent when candidate
identities and birth times are selected from the past.

E-values provide the underlying evidence currency

.
The e-BH procedure extends the framework to false-discovery control under arbitrary dependence

(

;

)
; those applications test forecasts, whereas our unit is a strategy-promotion workflow.
We use the conservative wealth-weighted crossing rule because one false launch

Calendar and technical trading rules provide a demanding case study because their design space is easy to expand and many reported effects are sample-sensitive

7. 
Limitations and Governance

The guarantee is narrow by design. It concerns the clipped, cost-adjusted score
and a null that is uniformly nonpositive after birth; it does not certify that a
formerly positive edge remains profitable. Clipping can also suppress rare
economic losses. It occurs in 128 of 68,832 historical candidate–session
scores (0.186%), so every evidence row is retained beside its unclipped return.

The two simulations answer different questions. Shared independent signs
preserve magnitudes and contemporaneous cross-rule dependence, but remove
serial sign dependence and freeze observed cost fractions; the resulting null
is generally strict. The adaptive audit is exactly mean zero and exercises
selection and staggered births, but its iid Rademacher scores are not a market
model. Neither construction asserts that realized returns satisfy
(
2
).

Validity is only as trustworthy as the filtration. Revised data,
survivorship-biased universes, pretrained-model knowledge of the replay era, or
same-close execution can import future information. A production agent must
therefore retain data and prompt hashes, candidate births, allocations, and
rejected proposals. An idea without a deterministic executable rule receives
no testing wealth.

The economic replay uses adjusted closes and zero cash yield. Five-basis-point
turnover costs are explicit, but financing, borrow fees, bid–ask dynamics,
impact, taxes, and capacity are absent; French industries are not directly
tradable. The design and pseudo-out-of-sample periods were chosen
retrospectively, and realized trading-day ranks can encode rare unscheduled
closures. These facts make Table 
5
 an audit, not a live
performance claim.

Finally, familywise control becomes conservative when an agent proposes many
correlated variants. Hierarchical predictable allocations expose but do not
eliminate that cost; discovery-oriented systems may prefer FDR control. In all
cases, promotion should trigger a separately governed forward evaluation rather
than unrestricted scaling.

In production, a crossing is an authorization request, not an order. A
pre-trade gate still checks data availability, venue and borrow eligibility,
spread and impact, concentration, turnover, and duplicate portfolio exposure.
It may veto or cap a trade without changing the evidence. A predictable risk
kill switch may stop exposure or betting, but cannot reclaim testing mass or
justify a same-day restart.

The ledger should fail closed: missing prices, ambiguous calendars, stale
features, or a code-hash mismatch suspend updates and deployment until an
operator records the resolution and first usable session. The e-process asks
whether the historical null was crossed; operations controls ask whether the
position is safe to trade now.

7.1. 
Certificate lifecycle under failure

A certificate moves through registered, accumulating, crossed, authorized,
suspended, and expired states. Crossing concerns one frozen version;
authorization is separate and time limited. Expiry requires a funded epoch
from Section 
6.1
; corrected history cannot backdate
promotion.

The release manifest binds the fingerprint to its rule package, data vintage,
calendar, costs, bet grid, budget, and expiry. This supports audits of replay,
post-decision inputs, and the first trade. It does not prove profitability; it
prevents implementation changes from voiding the theorem’s premises.

8. 
Conclusion

Adaptive research changes the unit of inference.
The relevant object is the sequence of proposals, inspections, and stopping
decisions, not one terminal backtest. 
Promote
 gives that process a finite
error budget: candidates inherit no evidence and must cross a cost-aware,
wealth-adjusted boundary. The guarantee survives continuous monitoring and an
adaptively expanding library.

The experiments expose the price: repeated inspection finds winners readily,
whereas the valid gate may wait for weak effects. That delay is the cost of an
optional-stopping guarantee, not evidence of current profitability. Frictions,
scale, and forward risk remain deployment questions.
