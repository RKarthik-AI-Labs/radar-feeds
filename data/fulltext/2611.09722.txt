Abstract

Large-language-model decode attention streams the key–value (KV) cache for every generated token.
Packing that cache into 2-bit or 4-bit quantized integers reduces memory traffic but does not automatically translate into latency saving.
Existing 2-bit fused kernels only reach less than

Introduction

Autoregressive language-model decoding reads each active request’s key–value (KV) cache on every decode step.
The cache footprint grows with batch size and context length; storing it consumes high-bandwidth memory (HBM) capacity, and streaming it from HBM often dominates decode-attention latency.
Low-bit KV quantization reduces that traffic directly.
KIVI 
Liu et al. (2024)
, Kitty 
Xia et al. (2025)
, OSCAR 
Zhou et al. (2026)
, KVQuant 
Hooper et al. (2024)
, and QServe 
Lin et al. (2025)
 store each cached value in 2–4 bits with a group-shared scale and offset.

Compressing the cache in memory does not eliminate on-chip operand preparation.
KIVI 
Liu et al. (2024)
, QServe 
Lin et al. (2025)
, TurboAttention 
Kang et al. (2025)
, BitDecoding 
Du et al. (2026)
, SAW-INT4

Zhou et al. (2026)
 all convert or reconstruct low-bit values before each matrix product.
This preparation consumes instruction-issue throughput, registers, and shared-memory bandwidth even when fused into attention without writing dequantized K and V to HBM.

Although bit-packing reduces HBM traffic, per-element operand preparation can still limit the resulting latency speedup.

FOSCAR applies affine folding to the query–key (QK) and probability–value (PV) matrix products, which reduce over channels and tokens.
For token-wise K, the kernel scales each group’s partial QK result and adds the group’s offset times the matching query sum.
For token-wise V, the kernel scales the attention weights before PV and adds a weighted sum of offsets to the output.
Channel-wise K instead scales the query before QK, while channel-wise V scales each group’s partial PV result, and both add the corresponding offset-weighted sum.
These per-group operations replace element-wise scale multiplication and offset addition, while bit manipulation constructs Tensor Core KV operands directly from packed integers.
In exact arithmetic, affine folding produces the same attention output as dequantizing every KV value before the matrix products.

4.2 
Affine folding spans multiple quantization groups

Under acc-fold, an unscaled partial product cannot cross a quantization-group boundary.
When a reduction spans multiple groups, the kernel applies each group’s scale and offset to its partial product before adding the result to the running accumulator (
Eq.
 
4
).
We call this partial product 
group-local
 because it sums only over the values that share one scale and offset.
Operand-fold instead scales the other matrix input before multiplication, so the accumulator can span multiple groups without rescaling; its offset correction still follows 
Eq.
 
3
.

Both the Hopper and Blackwell kernels enforce the same ordering constraints on these corrections, although they assign the work to different warpgroups (
Sec.
 
4.4
).
For operand-fold, the kernel can accumulate offset contributions for the same output separately and add their sum later.
QK corrections must precede softmax, while PV corrections must precede division by the accumulated softmax denominator.

We design separate warp-specialized pipelines for Hopper and Blackwell.
Hopper’s 
wgmma

Related Work

7.1 
KV-cache quantization algorithms

KIVI 
Liu et al. (2024)
, KVQuant 
Hooper et al. (2024)
, and Kitty 
Xia et al. (2025)
 establish low-bit KV formats and motivate channel-wise grouping for K and token-wise grouping for V.
Atom 
Zhao et al. (2024)
 and QServe 
Lin et al. (2025)
 co-design 4-bit quantization formats and serving kernels, while GEAR 
Kang et al. (2024)
 adds low-rank and sparse error corrections.
FOSCAR retains uniform affine quantization and changes how a Tensor Core kernel evaluates the quantized cache.
Nonlinear lookup formats and sparse outlier representations cannot be expressed as a single affine transform and require separate kernel support.

7.2 
Quantized attention kernels

BitDecoding provides low-bit Tensor Core attention kernels across several GPU generations 
Du et al. (2026)
.
On Hopper, it converts stored integer values and applies scales and offsets before the matrix products.
On Blackwell, it uses block-scaled FP4 instructions that restrict scales to the contraction axis, omit offsets, and quantize 
 and each

TensorRT-LLM’s XQA kernel folds a scalar INT8 or FP8 KV scale into the QK and PV accumulators 
NVIDIA Corporation (2026e)
.
QServe fuses low-bit KV dequantization into CUDA-core batched matrix-vector products 
Lin et al. (2025)
.
TurboAttention converts sub-8-bit values into INT8 operands for Tensor Core attention 
Kang et al. (2025)
.
The SageAttention kernels quantize full-precision 
, 
, and

Zhang et al. (2025c)
; 
Zhang et al. (2025a)
; 
Zhang et al. (2025b)

SAW-INT4 
Jia et al. (2026)
 combines token-wise affine INT4 caches with a block-diagonal Hadamard rotation, while OSCAR 
Zhou et al. (2026)
 combines a mixed INT2/BF16 cache with offline spectral rotations; both kernels reconstruct cached values before their matrix products.
FOSCAR instead applies affine folding across independent token-wise or channel-wise K and V caches, folding group scales and non-zero offsets into the 
 or

7.3 
Attention pipelines and kernel reparameterization

FlashAttention-2 introduces work partitioning for tiled attention 
Dao (2024)
, FlashAttention-3 overlaps producer and consumer warpgroups through warp specialization 
Shah et al. (2024)
, FlashInfer provides paged-cache attention kernels for serving engines 
Ye et al. (2025)
, and FlashAttention-4 studies how Blackwell’s higher Tensor Core throughput shifts bottlenecks toward on-chip staging and non-matmul instructions 
Zadouri et al. (2026)
.
RepVGG 
Ding et al. (2021)
 folds training-time convolution branches into inference kernels, CODA 
Guo et al. (2026)

Zhao et al. (2024)
; 
Lin et al. (2025)
 rescale group partial sums or subtract zero-point activation sums in their epilogues.
FOSCAR combines warp-specialized attention pipelining with scale-and-offset folding across both QK and PV under online softmax, where the eliminated per-element work grows with context length.

7.4 
Quantized inference architecture

Oaken combines a KV quantization policy with custom hardware for online and offline compression 
Kim et al. (2025)
.
Weight-only kernels such as Marlin 
Frantar et al. (2025)
; 
Lin (2025)
, FLUTE 
Guo et al. (2024)
, and LUT-GEMM 
Park et al. (2024)

FOSCAR instead targets the QK and PV matrix products of decode attention, which are linked by online softmax, on unmodified Hopper and Blackwell Tensor Cores.

8 
Conclusion

FOSCAR accelerates decode attention over low-bit KV caches through affine folding, which moves each quantization group’s shared scale to a matrix-product input or partial result and applies its offset as an output correction.
Its Hopper and Blackwell kernels construct Tensor Core KV operands from packed quantized values through bit manipulation, while affine folding preserves the attention output of the reconstructed cache in exact arithmetic.
On H100 and B200, FOSCAR INT2 outperforms FlashInfer BF16 and TensorRT-LLM FP8 across all tested batch sizes and context lengths on the main benchmark grid.
Qwen3-8B serving experiments also show throughput gains of up to 
 over baselines in settings with long contexts and high cache hit rates that align with popular agentic workloads.

Appendix A 
KV Packing and Dataflow

Fig.
 
6
 illustrates how FOSCAR packs INT2 K values and constructs Tensor Core operands on Hopper and Blackwell.
The same approach extends to INT2 V and INT4 K and V because cache packing arranges quantized values to match each Tensor Core operand layout, and the decode kernel constructs floating-point operands with shifts and masks.
Quantizing K or V along tokens or channels changes how scales and offsets are grouped and where affine folding applies them (
Sec.
 
4.1
), whereas the integer packing below depends only on the stored bit width and matrix operand layout.

A.1 
Hopper: packing for register operands

Hopper packs K and V according to the register fragments that each consumer thread supplies to 
wgmma
.
For QK, the K operand has token-by-channel coordinates, as shown in 
Fig.
 
6a
.
For PV, the V operand has channel-by-token coordinates, so FOSCAR packs V values according to the transposed V tile.
Both layouts let each consumer thread load contiguous packed words from shared memory and construct its register operands without gathering individual values.

Each 32-bit packed word places the two quantized values for a pair of BF16 operands in fields sixteen bits apart.
An INT2 word holds sixteen values, forming eight pairs; an INT4 word holds eight values, forming four pairs.
A shift aligns both fields with their BF16 mantissas, and a masked merge combines them with a fixed BF16 bit pattern.
The stored bit width sets the field spacing and the number of pairs per word.
K and V differ only in the logical coordinates of those pairs and share the same pairwise register construction.
Affine folding compensates for the resulting operand mapping through 
Eq.
 
5
.

The Hopper dataflow in 
Fig.
 
6b
 therefore also applies to INT2 V and INT4 K and V.
TMA copies the packed tiles from global to shared memory.
Consumer threads construct BF16 operands in registers, and all four warps of a consumer warpgroup collectively issue 
wgmma
.
The QK product consumes K fragments, and the PV product consumes the transposed V fragments.
