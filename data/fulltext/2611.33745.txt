Abstract

World models predict the consequences of actions, but they are trained on factual transitions and represent the future of each action separately. Decisions, however, are comparisons: would another action have done better in the same physical situation? We introduce WhatIf, a counterfactual world model that represents the joint law of all candidate futures of a manipulation scene by passing one abducted physical situation through an action-grounded causal graph. From a factual history, WhatIf abducts hierarchical latent physics and transition noise; each candidate action generates a sparse object–factor graph whose topology and edge strengths specify the physical pathways it activates; and all candidate futures are rolled out under the shared situation. Shared type-conditioned functions instantiate the graph for any number of objects. Counterfactual predictions are anchored on observed outcomes, and the graph confines each action contrast to the objects that the action reaches. We show that factual data determine only the marginal future of each action, and hence the average action effect but neither the individual effect nor the probability that switching actions causes harm, and that anchoring with the average effect incurs an error equal to the conditional variance of the individual effect. A round-trip loss enforces action-invariant abduction without counterfactual labels. On the coupled futures we build Counterfactual Advantage Planning (CAP), which ranks plans by their advantage over an incumbent within shared situations, constrains the probability of harm, and converts each executed transition into hindsight advantages that update the physics posterior, the plan of the next attempt, and an amortized policy without a learned critic. Paired advantages reduce estimator variance by the factor

 is the outcome correlation between branches, and cancel model errors common to both branches. We further derive when the observational transition 
 identifies 
 and where hidden action confounding or insufficient support breaks this equality. Paired simulator interventions evaluate counterfactual accuracy, graph recovery, compositional generalization, and decision quality.

Introduction

World models compress past observations into a state from which an agent predicts future trajectories and plans actions 
[
9
, 
10
]
. Their success has largely been driven by expressive sequence models and predictive objectives. Given a history 
 and the action taken by a behavior policy, a conventional world model learns

(1)

by minimizing error on logged transitions. This objective does not require the model to separate stable physical mechanisms from regularities specific to the training policy, scene distribution, or object arrangement. A predictor may associate a visual configuration with its frequent successor without discovering which object caused which change. Consequently, world models often generalize poorly when masses, friction, object counts, layouts, or contact patterns differ from the training distribution. Scaling the predictor can improve interpolation, but it does not by itself make the learned transition invariant to such mechanism-preserving changes.

This limitation motivates a 
causal world model
: a model that represents the mechanisms by which objects and state factors influence one another, and whose predictions are composed from those mechanisms under intervention 
[
14
, 
17
]
. In robotic manipulation, the distinction is concrete. A push creates a gripper–object contact, propagates force through selected object pairs, and leaves unrelated objects unchanged. If the same contact and force-transfer mechanisms are represented explicitly, they can be reused when the scene contains a different number or arrangement of objects. Causal structure is therefore not only an explanation layer; it is a hypothesis about which parts of the dynamics should remain stable across environments.

Mechanism discovery alone, however, does not answer the decision-time question that matters most: 
what would have happened in the same physical situation had the robot taken another action?
 Logged data reveal only the outcome of the realized action. Predicting each candidate action independently can change latent context or stochastic realization together with the action, confounding action comparison with scenario variation. Counterfactual prediction instead follows abduction–intervention–prediction: infer the latent physical situation and exogenous realization responsible for the factual transition, replace only the action mechanism, and predict the resulting future 
[
14
, 
3
]
. This distinction becomes decisive for control, because decisions are comparisons. Even a perfect conventional world model supplies only the marginal law of each action’s future. These marginals fix the expected advantage of switching actions, but not how often the switch helps or harms in the same situation, nor which failures the action choice could have avoided. A 
counterfactual world model
 must therefore represent the joint law of the futures of all candidate actions, coupled through one shared physical situation. This coupling yields lower-variance action comparisons, cancels model errors common to all candidates, bounds the probability of harm, and turns every executed transition into supervision for many actions that were never executed, without counterfactual 
-value labels.

Causal world models: progress and unresolved problem.

Existing causal world models have pursued several complementary routes. Causal World Models infer object-level latent physical factors and use a structural model to predict outcomes under intervention 
[
12
]
. CausalVAE as a plug-in learns a sparse directed acyclic graph over fixed latent dimensions, enforcing sparsity and acyclicity so that latent dependencies can support counterfactual reasoning 
[
5
]
. DALI infers latent dynamics context from interaction histories to generalize to unseen physical conditions 
[
16
]
. Object-centric models factor dynamics across entities 
[
2
, 
7
]
; FIOC-WM, Dyn-O, and SPARTAN further expose modular interactions, separate dynamics-relevant factors, or infer sparse local graphs 
[
6
, 
19
, 
11
]
. These methods establish that physical context, object factorization, and local structure improve generalization. Nevertheless, they leave a common gap. A fixed latent DAG cannot naturally represent variable object sets or contacts created by a particular action; an unstructured context vector does not specify which edge it modulates; and a learned local graph is not necessarily regenerated under a replacement action while holding the factual physical realization fixed. Thus current causal world models do not yet provide an executable account of how an intervention changes both the topology and strength of a manipulation mechanism.

Counterfactual world models: progress and unresolved problem.

Counterfactually-Guided Policy Search infers exogenous variables from an observed episode and evaluates alternative policies in the same inferred scenario 
[
3
]
. Work on confounded model-based reinforcement learning studies when logged actions are correlated with privileged latent state and develops causal corrections for policy learning 
[
8
]
. FOHL, although developed for heterogeneous treatment effects rather than sequential control, provides a relevant factual-abduction principle: infer latent heterogeneity from factual treatment–outcome data before replacing the treatment 
[
21
]
. Counterfactual Quotient Models directly isolate the part of a future that changes across candidate actions 
[
4
]
. These approaches clarify how alternative actions should be compared, but they either assume a known or sufficiently accurate structural model, focus on static treatment outcomes, or represent action-dependent outcomes without an object-level causal mechanism. They do not explain which contacts appear, which pathways disappear, and how force propagation changes when a manipulation action is replaced. Their counterfactuals may therefore remain brittle under novel object compositions and mechanism shifts.

We address the intersection of these gaps with WhatIf, a counterfactual world model built on an action-grounded causal graph. The latent physical state

graph generator

Shared type-conditioned functions instantiate this graph for any number of objects, and a nested factor graph specifies which state dimensions are transmitted along each active object edge. One physical situation, consisting of latent physics and transition noise, is passed through the graph of every candidate action, so the model represents all candidate futures jointly: replacing

Counterfactual predictions are anchored on the observed successor, and the graph confines each action contrast to the objects it reaches. On this model we build Counterfactual Advantage Planning (CAP). Candidate plans are ranked by their advantage over an incumbent plan within shared situations, switches with a high probability of harm are rejected, and each executed transition is converted into hindsight advantages that update the physics posterior, the incumbent of the next attempt, and an amortized proposal policy.

Consider a robot arm facing two blocks. In the factual trajectory, the gripper pushes the red block, which then collides with the blue block. From the observed displacement and contact response, the model infers latent mass, friction, and compliance. The factual action activates gripper
red and red
blue edges. To evaluate a counterfactual grasp, the model retains the same latent physical state but regenerates the graph: the pushing edge is replaced by grasp contacts and the collision edge may disappear. To evaluate a stronger push in another direction, the same edge types may remain active while their weights and downstream collision topology change. A planner can compare these complete trajectories, including success, collision, and uncertainty, rather than attempt to fit a scalar counterfactual value that was never observed. If the factual push fails to separate the blocks, the hindsight advantage identifies the push that would have separated them under the same latent physics and contact noise. That push becomes the incumbent of the retry, which reuses the inferred mass and friction, and a stronger alternative is adopted only if it rarely does worse than the incumbent in the same situations.

Our contributions are:

•

We formulate a counterfactual world model as the joint law of all candidate futures, generated by passing one abducted physical situation through an action-grounded causal graph. We show that factual data determine only the marginals of this law, that anchoring with the average effect incurs an error equal to the conditional variance of the individual effect, and we enforce action-invariant abduction with a round-trip loss that needs no counterfactual labels.

•

We introduce a variable-cardinality object–factor graph in which actions control both edge activation and edge strength, enabling topology-changing interventions such as contact creation, removal, and force redirection.

•

We propose Counterfactual Advantage Planning, which ranks plans by paired advantages within shared situations, constrains the individual-level probability of harm, and converts executed transitions into hindsight advantages for physics updates, retries, and critic-free advantage-weighted policy learning. Paired advantages reduce estimator variance by the factor 
 and cancel model errors common to both branches.

•

We derive sufficient conditions for

•

We develop a paired-intervention evaluation that measures counterfactual accuracy, graph recovery, compositional generalization, and decision quality against simulator ground truth.

Related Work

2.1 
Causal World Models

Structural causal models distinguish conditioning from intervention and counterfactual queries 
[
14
]
. Causal representation learning extends this distinction to latent variables, seeking independent and reusable mechanisms rather than entangled predictive features 
[
17
]
. Causal World Models apply these principles to physical scenes by inferring unobserved object properties and predicting intervention outcomes 
[
12
]
. CausalVAE as a plug-in estimates a sparse latent adjacency matrix and imposes the differentiable acyclicity constraint

(2)

following continuous DAG learning 
[
20
, 
5
]
. This is suitable for directed relations among a fixed set of same-slice latent variables, but its adjacency parameters do not directly transfer when objects enter or leave a scene.

A second line emphasizes physical context and object compositionality. DALI infers latent environment dynamics from interaction history 
[
16
]
. Factored world models and FOCUS decompose scenes into objects and exploit shared relational dynamics for manipulation and exploration 
[
2
, 
7
]
. FIOC-WM learns modular object interactions, Dyn-O separates dynamics-aware object factors, and SPARTAN learns sparse context-dependent local structures robust to distractors and dynamics changes 
[
6
, 
19
, 
11
]
. These models improve transfer, but latent physical context is generally consumed as a global predictor input, while local edges are learned for factual prediction. They do not jointly require that a replacement action regenerate an executable graph under the same abducted physical realization. Our method treats the local graph itself as an action-dependent causal mechanism and separates edge existence from edge strength.

2.2 
Counterfactual World Models and Control

CF-GPS introduced counterfactual trajectory generation for reinforcement learning by abducting exogenous randomness from an observed trajectory and replacing the policy 
[
3
]
. This gives lower-variance comparisons because factual and counterfactual trajectories share the inferred scenario, but it relies on a correct causal simulator. Confounded model-based RL highlights a complementary problem: if a behavior policy observes privileged state that is omitted from the learner’s history, observational and interventional action effects can differ 
[
8
]
. FOHL infers latent heterogeneity from factual observations before treatment replacement 
[
21
]
; this motivates our physical-state abduction, although sequential manipulation requires time-varying objects, actions, and mechanisms rather than a static treatment–outcome graph.

Counterfactual Quotient Models isolate action-dependent future components by comparing candidate predictions and cancelling their common mode 
[
4
]
. This is useful for control because action-independent prediction error need not corrupt action ranking. However, an action-effect representation does not identify which object interactions produced the effect and offers no direct mechanism for transfer across changing object sets. Our approach generates structured counterfactual trajectories from an action-conditioned graph and evaluates them with model-predictive control. It therefore complements common-mode cancellation while retaining an inspectable and reusable dynamics mechanism. Model-based agents such as DreamerV3 and TD-MPC2 score imagined rollouts with learned critics and sample the future of each candidate independently 
[
9
, 
10
]
; CAP instead computes advantages from coupled counterfactual rollouts that share an abducted situation, which also exposes the individual-level probability of harm.

Experimental Design

Environments and data.

CausalWorld supplies controlled interventions on masses, sizes, colors, robot properties, and task variables 
[
1
]
. Multi-task robotic suites such as Meta-World or ManiSkill-style environments add pushing, grasping, placement, articulation, and variable object composition. At selected factual states, the simulator is cloned and alternative actions are executed with matched initial state and random seed. This produces paired unit-level counterfactual trajectories and, when simulator contacts are exposed, graph supervision.

Generalization protocol.

Training and test distributions are separated along object instance, object count, layout, mechanism composition, mass and friction, policy, and distractor dimensions. Interpolation and extrapolation of physical parameters are reported separately. Factual-only training and training with paired counterfactual supervision are also separated so that intervention data requirements remain explicit.

5 
Conclusion

Conventional world models predict what usually follows an action but need not discover why. Causal world models expose reusable mechanisms, while counterfactual models compare alternative actions in the same factual situation. WhatIf combines these requirements in a counterfactual world model: it abducts latent physics, treats the action as a generator of sparse graph topology and edge strength, and passes one shared situation through the regenerated graph of every candidate action. Counterfactual Advantage Planning turns the resulting coupled futures into decisions, comparing plans within shared situations, bounding the probability of harm, and learning from the actions that were not taken. The resulting research question is testable: whether explicit action-grounded mechanisms improve counterfactual accuracy, decision quality, compositional generalization, and robotic control beyond equally expressive predictive, object-centric, and sparse-attention models.
