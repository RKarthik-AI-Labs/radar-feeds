Dynamic Watermarking and Action-Conditioned Latent Dynamics for
Secure Vision-Based Controllers
Aditya Parameshwaran1, Navid Aftabi 2, Abhishek Hanchate 3,
Satish T. S. Bukkapatnam 3, Dan Li 2 and Yue Wang1∗
Abstract— Replay attacks are difficult to detect in vision-
based control systems because previously recorded observations
can remain plausible while the physical system follows a
different trajectory. This paper develops an adaptive dynamic-
watermarking framework for detecting replay attacks against
vision-based controllers. A private Gaussian watermark is
added to the nominal control input, and a control input-
conditioned latent dynamics network (LDN) predicts the cor-
responding evolution in a compact latent space learned from
image observations. Attack evidence is obtained from the
discrepancy between the predicted latent response and the
latent representation of the received image observation. A
reinforcement learning policy adjusts the watermark covariance
using the latent state and detector confidence, thereby balancing
attack detectability, watermark energy, and control distortion.
A Lagrangian penalty on task-specific safety costs is included
during training to limit the adverse effect of the excitation. The
framework is evaluated on a vision-guided UR5 manipulation
task in which the robot reaches a target while avoiding a moving
human arm under nominal and replay-attacked observations.
I. INTRODUCTION
Vision-based neural controllers are powerful in cluttered,
human-robot settings, but their sensing channel is vulnerable
to replay attacks: an adversary can replace the current image
stream with previously recorded observations that remain
visually plausible. In safety-critical tasks, such attacks can
conceal a physical deviation while the robot continues to
act on stale visual feedback. Dynamic watermarking (DWM)
addresses this by injecting a private excitation into the
control input and checking whether the current measurements
contain the corresponding response [1], [2]. Existing methods
typically form this consistency check from known state-space
dynamics and low-dimensional measurements [3], [4]. That
assumption does not directly apply to a black-box neural
controller where the observations can be high-dimensional
images with unknown dynamics. We address this gap with
a control input-conditioned latent dynamics network (LDN)
that predicts the evolution of image representations in a
compact visual latent space, enabling watermark-response
residuals to be formed without requiring an explicit analytical
model of the image dynamics [5], [6].

We condition the LDN on a privately watermarked control

latent representation of the received observation. The result-
ing latent space residual provides replay evidence, while a
learned DWM policy adapts the watermark covariance to
balance detectability, actuation effort, and task performance.
The framework thereby combines latent dynamics prediction,
adaptive watermarking, and supervisory fallback in a single
vision-based replay-defense architecture as seen in Figure 1.
Our contributions are summarized as follows:
1) We detect replay attacks by verifying a private
DWM signal through predictions from a control input-
conditioned LDN.
2) We characterize how replay-induced watermark mis-
match alters the MCUSUM detector statistic and de-
velop a Bayesian belief update that quantifies online
detection confidence and serves as a compact input to
the DWM policy.
3) We train the DWM policy with a Lagrangian penalty
on robot–human separation violations and evaluate
supervisory stopping under replay. We demonstrate
the framework in a simulated human-robot task where
misleading visual feedback can lead to unsafe motion.
II. RELATEDWORK
A. Replay Attacks and Dynamic Watermarking
Replay attacks are particularly challenging because prere-
corded measurements can remain plausible after the physical
system has diverged, concealing unsafe behavior. Similar
threats arise in visual verification systems, where replayed
images can mislead face age verification [7], and detection
methods combine spatial and temporal features to identify
video replay attacks [8]. Dynamic watermarking (DWM)
helps detect replay attacks by injecting a private random
excitation and checking whether the measurements reflect
the system’s response to the current control input [1].
Prerecorded measurements that remain plausible will fail
this response test and thus reveal the attack’s occurrence.
This principle was then formalized with innovation-based
detector statistics for linear time-invariant and time-varying
systems [2], [3], and later extended to sequential detection
rules that balance information gain and control cost [4].
These methods provide the theoretical foundation for replay
detection, but their residuals are generally derived from
explicit plant and observation models with low-dimensional
measurements. For a black-box neural controller operating

The excitation must also be selected without unnecessarily

image representations instead of low-dimensional state es-

supervisory fallback to an observation-independent controller

B. Latent Dynamics Models for Visual Control
In visual control, latent dynamics models provide a com-
pact representation in which action-conditioned dynamics
can be rolled forward over multiple steps. PlaNet uses
learned latent dynamics for online planning from pixels [10],
while Dreamer learns behavior through imagined latent tra-
jectories [11]; DayDreamer demonstrates the use of world
models for learning directly on physical robots [6]. TD-
MPC further shows how task-oriented latent dynamics can
support short-horizon trajectory optimization for continuous
control [12]. Building on this line of work, our LDN au-
toregressively predicts future image-derived latents, feeding
each predicted latent and the next privately watermarked
control input into the subsequent prediction. We compare this
predicted trajectory with latents encoded from subsequently
received images; their discrepancy supplies replay evidence
to the detector. The detector confidence and current latent
state then inform adaptive watermark selection.

B. Replay-Attack Model and MCUSUM Detection and Belief
a) Replay-Attack Model:Replay attacks seek to remain
statistically consistent with nominal operation by replacing
the current visual stream with previously recorded legiti-
mate observations while the physical robot evolves under
potentially manipulated control inputs [9]. We assume that
the adversary has access to the sensing channels and can
observe and replace the RGB-D observations received by the
controller and detector. The adversary follows a two-stage
strategy: first, it records a sufficiently long benign trajectory
xt during closed-loop operation without active intervention;
subsequently, it replays the recorded observations. Thus,
during replay, the controller and detector receive the spoofed
observationx ′
t, whereas the physical robot receives the
adversarial control inputF(x ′
t) =u a

the control policy on spoofed observations. In the considered
threat model, it is assumed that the adversary cannot directly
observe or measure the secret dynamic-watermarking signal
injected into the control channel, meaning their replayed
observations fail to account for the real-time physical ex-
citation.

misleading feedback. A separate test therefore evaluates

Figure 3, in the belief panel(Top), the red curve shows

that the policy visible, replay attacked observations show the
safety metric to be increasing, whereas in reality the true
safety metric actually decreases caused by the attack. With
Simplex active, the live robot maintains greater separation
from the human arm and its end-effector speed approaches

entered the 3 cm proximity zone, compared with none with
Simplex. The reported minimum clearance increased from

Of 30 switches, 28 followed attack onset and two occurred
early. Watermarking supplies attack evidence, and Simplex
uses that evidence to initiate a protective stop. A limitation
remains that false attack detections can still interrupt the task
and reduce performance.
VI. CONCLUSION
This paper combines private dynamic watermarking,
action-conditioned latent dynamics, Bayesian attack confi-
dence, and Simplex stopping for vision-based control. The
detector compares latent predictions conditioned on the ap-
plied watermarked commands with representations encoded
from the received RGB-D images. A trained PPO policy
selects the watermark covariance, while a Lagrangian sep-
aration penalty discourages watermark choices that bring
the robot too close to the human arm. The analysis derives
an approximate replay-residual covariance and a sequential
belief update under the stated modeling assumptions.
Reach-avoid experiments evaluate this visual detector and
trained watermark policy. Watermarking produced post-onset
belief crossings without earlier crossings in 29 of 30 replay
episodes, with a median delay of 1.23 s. Simplex stopping
reduced episodes entering the 3 cm proximity zone from 20
of 30 to none in the separate safety test. Six clean episodes
crossed the belief threshold, and two switches in the safety
test occurred before attack onset, showing that unnecessary
intervention remains a limitation. These results demonstrate
replay detection and reduced motion under misleading feed-
back within the evaluated task; the training penalty and
stopping response do not establish a formal safety guarantee.
Future work will use neural network verification tools
to develop formal safety guarantees, extend the framework
to physical robot experiments with sensing and actuation
delays, and improve latent dynamics prediction to reduce
false alarms. Further evaluation will also consider continued
human motion after the robot stops.
