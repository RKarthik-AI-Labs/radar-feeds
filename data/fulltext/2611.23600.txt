Abstract

Path-based attribution methods like Integrated Gradients (IG) are widely used to interpret neural networks, yet imperceptible input perturbations can drastically alter their attribution maps while leaving model predictions unchanged. We introduce Entropy-Regularized Integrated Gradients (ERIG), a path-based attribution method that intrinsically attenuates gradient irregularities along the integration path using maximum-entropy softmax weighting with temperature parameter 
. We quantify the effect of the entropy-regularized weighting on attribution stability by using the Lipschitz properties of the model and its gradients. We decompose the attribution change under an input perturbation into a model-response term, whose bound is 
-independent, and a weight-variation term, whose bound decreases at rate 
. Their ratio 
 characterizes the trade-off between gradient information and attribution stability. We further add a new dimension to attribution evaluation by adapting classical noise estimators into two metrics, LINV-C and LINV-P, that quantify attribution noise. Across eleven CNN and Transformer backbones on ImageNet and Oxford-IIIT Pet datasets, our experiments show that, relative to the next-best method, ERIG remains comparable on ranking-based fidelity metrics while improving localization by 
 and attribution stability by 
 on average, reaching up to four orders of magnitude improvement over IG — all at negligible additional computational cost.

 balances attribution sharpness against over-concentration, consistent with the balance characterized by 
 in 
Theorem
 
2
.

6 
Conclusion

We present Entropy-Regularized Integrated Gradients (ERIG), a path-based attribution method that converts squared-gradient sensitivities into attribution weights using an entropy-regularized softmax. A temperature parameter 
 balances sensitivity preservation against attenuation of local gradient irregularities. Our Lipschitz analysis decomposes attribution perturbations into a model-response term with a 
-independent bound and a weight-variation term whose bound decays as 
. Their ratio, 
, characterizes the trade-off between gradient information and attribution stability. We also adapt established noise estimators into LINV-C and LINV-P. Across eleven models on ImageNet and Oxford-IIIT Pet, ERIG achieves fidelity comparable to the strongest baselines while substantially improving localization, stability, and noise suppression, all at a per-step cost within 
 of IDGI and 
 of IG. On FunnyBirds, it places first or second in all metrics. Future work will explore further adaptive temperature schedules 
, analytic 
 selection on 1-Lipschitz networks, and composition with gradient-flow corrections such as LibraGrad.
