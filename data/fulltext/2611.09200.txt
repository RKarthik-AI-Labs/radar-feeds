Abstract

We study Vietnamese statutory Article retrieval on DRiLL using features for provision identity and local form, same-law support, and citation and lineage evidence. Across five random seeds, selectors sharing candidates, listwise Qwen evidence, and learner settings improve official 
 from 71.77% to 72.91%, with the largest descriptive gain on two-Article questions. Added features account for 18.20% of the proposed selector’s total decision-tree split gain, measured as training-objective reduction, mainly from same-law and citation evidence. Cases reveal base provisions and amendments, companion recovery, distractor removal, and failures. These findings motivate research on other datasets, representations, and learners without establishing individual feature effects or requiring LightGBM, hand-crafted scores, or explicit graphs.

Introduction

Statutory Article retrieval connects questions with the provisions needed to answer them 
[
10
]
. Similar wording may miss complementary rules or amendments. Vietnamese statutes expose structure through 
Điều
 (Article), 
Khoản
 (Clause), 
Điểm
 (Point), instrument identifiers, and references. DRiLL includes questions requiring multiple Articles 
[
17
]
, making it suitable for examining relationships beyond individual similarity scores.

Our research question is: 
Does DRiLL contain structural and lineage-related evidence that can help retrieval beyond conventional similarity?
 Structure includes legal-unit form, instrument membership, and reference neighborhoods. Lineage-related cues connect base provisions with amendments, guidance, or introduced units. We examine these cues through a retrieval method.

We follow EDM’s DRiLL retrieval setting, conventional features, and staged design 
[
12
]
, simplifying the LLM-dependent stages to reduce LLM calls. We then add legal evidence to the internal controls. Final selectors share candidates, listwise judgments, and learner settings. Five-run results, split-gain importance, and cases characterize DRiLL evidence through LightGBM and explicit features. Future work can explore other learners, representations, and datasets.

2 
Related Work

Vietnamese retrieval systems.

DRiLL systems combine lexical and semantic matching with richer Article context. Hieu and Do use hybrid retrieval and a cross-encoder, with reconstructed titles and hard-negative training 
[
6
]
. Their design enriches individual candidates with document context. Hoai et al. use LLM augmentation and hierarchical chunking to form coherent retrieval units 
[
7
]
. This addresses the internal organization of long legal texts. ViDRILL combines sparse and dense retrieval with semantic reranking, dual-level chunking, and hard-negative mining 
[
15
]
. These pipelines improve matching and candidate quality. We examine additional evidence connecting candidates with other provisions.

LLM evidence and staged ranking.

HyDE embeds a generated hypothetical document to retrieve real corpus passages 
[
5
]
. This enriches the query view, while retrieved legal texts remain the evidence for answering. EDM combines hybrid retrieval, learned ranking, and LLM relevance judgments 
[
12
]
. It also uses citation expansion and combines matching scores with document-form cues. Our internal controls reuse this feature design while simplifying the LLM stages. The comparison examines added legal evidence under shared final-selection inputs. We distinguish logical LLM evaluations implied by these designs from measured latency or monetary cost.

Structural and lineage context.

G-DSR incorporates legislative structure into dense retrieval through a graph neural network 
[
11
]
. This captures dependencies beyond an isolated Article. LegisSearch combines a legislative graph, contextual embeddings, and LLM query expansion for Italian legislation 
[
3
]
. Its typed citation and amendment relations provide a precedent for representing lineage. These approaches motivate examining the direction and type of legal connections. Extracted DRiLL links remain potential relevance cues. A relation may identify a missing companion or a topical distractor, so its value depends on the query and other candidates available for selection. We study same-instrument support, reference neighborhoods, and amendment-related patterns through explicit features and cases.

7.3 
Transfer and inference work

Reusing instrument aggregation and directional summaries requires mapped units, stable IDs, adapted Article/Clause/Point patterns and action lexicons, and rebuilt reference edges. Numbering differences, consolidated texts, sparse citations, and broad references can reduce coverage or add noise. Transfer is motivated by statutory-graph retrieval 
[
11
]
 but remains unvalidated.

EDM additionally uses offline law/Article descriptions, Top-100 LLM pair scores, a second learned ranker, and ten pairwise plus one collective final judgment 
[
12
]
. Our path uses HyDE and one collective Top-10 judgment. The nominal online work is therefore up to 
 versus 
 logical LLM evaluations. Final selection alone is 11 versus 1. Batching, caching, retries, token lengths, and neural reranking are outside these counts, so they do not establish API-request, cost, or latency ratios.

8 
Conclusion

DRiLL cases demonstrate structural relationships and legislative lineage. Split-gain importance and paired selection changes illustrate their use by our retrieval method. Shared-input final selection improves official 
 from 71.77% to 72.91%, especially for two-Article questions. These findings motivate cross-dataset validation and alternative retrieval, representation-learning, or multi-Article reasoning methods. They establish neither the individual benefit of every feature nor the necessity of LightGBM, hand-crafted scores, or explicit graphs.

Limitations and Ethical Considerations

Scope.
 Evaluation covers DRiLL only. Cross-dataset validation, expert review of parsed links, and matched latency/cost measurements remain future work.

Attribution.
 We have not yet performed ablations of individual legal features or their subgroups. First-ranker capacities differ without a matched-capacity comparison. Final selectors share the proposed Top-10 but use different development thresholds. Gain describes fitted-tree use, not causal importance.

Legal use.
 Heuristic links and amendment cues do not establish current legal validity or complete version histories. Retrieved Articles require qualified review of operative provisions and effective dates. Hypothetical passages are not legal advice. Confidential queries require safeguards when using external LLM services.
