Abstract.

The Query Reformulation Task of the 2026 SIGIR Workshop on eCommerce assesses whether systems can reformulate task-oriented e-commerce queries that describe activities or goals into expressions better suited for product retrieval, and then retrieve the products required to complete the task through a standard BM25 pipeline. Unlike queries that directly specify products, task-oriented queries often encode product needs implicitly in activity-level semantics, creating a pronounced lexical gap between query terms and the product catalog. Moreover, the Automatic and Interactive protocols impose different requirements on the number of reformulations. A successful system must therefore preserve the original task intent while improving catalog-level retrievability, and select either a single query or multiple complementary queries according to the protocol.
We present TaskBridge, a catalog-anchored query reformulation framework for task-oriented e-commerce search. TaskBridge consists of the 
Catalog Semantic Anchoring
 module, the 
Unified Five-Candidate Rewriting
 module, and the 
Protocol-Aware Selection
 module. Catalog Semantic Anchoring constructs a phrase lexicon and semantic anchors from the product corpus, providing task queries with searchable expressions grounded in catalog vocabulary. Unified Five-Candidate Rewriting generates multi-perspective candidates around the original query, preserving its task intent while covering different interpretations of the underlying product needs. Protocol-Aware Selection combines BM25 retrieval results, query–product reranking scores, and weighted Jaccard overlap to select one reformulation under the Automatic setting and up to four complementary reformulations under the Interactive setting. The selected queries are then executed independently using BM25 and converted into the six-column retrieval-result format required by the challenge. Experimental results show that TaskBridge effectively improves product retrieval for task-oriented queries. The multi-reformulation Interactive setting further exploits the complementarity among candidates, yielding additional gains in overall retrieval effectiveness.
TaskBridge achieved 
first place
 on the SIGIR 2026 eCommerce Challenge.

e-commerce search, query reformulation, BM25, interactive retrieval

1. 
Introduction

Queries in e-commerce search do not always specify products explicitly. Users often express their needs through activities, scenarios, or desired outcomes. For example, “make lasagna” describes a cooking task without explicitly listing the baking dish, ingredients, or kitchenware required. Such task-oriented queries encode product needs in high-level intent, leaving no direct lexical correspondence between the query terms and product titles. Task-oriented query reformulation aims to preserve the original task intent while making relevant product expressions explicit, so that standard lexical retrieval can retrieve the products required to complete the task
(
Singh et al., 2012
; 
Gollapudi et al., 2012
; 
Wu et al., 2017
; 
Shah and White, 2021
; 
SIGIR Workshop on eCommerce Organizers, 2026
; 
Zhang et al., 2026b
; 
TREC Product Search and Recommendations Track, 2026
)
. The central challenge is therefore not simple query expansion, but the transformation of a query into catalog-matchable product expressions without changing its task meaning.

This transformation involves two types of risk. When a query is under-reformulated, the original query may describe only the task goal and fail to reach the categories or entities that represent the required products in the catalog, making relevant products difficult to retrieve. When a query is over-reformulated, newly added terms may introduce products that the task does not require, weakening the original intent. Because product titles and category names in a catalog do not necessarily follow users’ everyday expressions, generating seemingly plausible product terms with a language model alone does not guarantee a match to the actual catalog. Reformulation must therefore bridge the lexical gap between task intent and catalog expressions while controlling the catalog grounding and extent of the added content
(
Maji et al., 2019
; 
Manchanda et al., 2019
; 
Hirsch et al., 2020
)
.

More generally, automatic query-expansion research treats the mismatch between short keyword queries and underlying information needs as a central retrieval problem, and organizes solutions around expansion sources, term selection, and weighting strategies
(
Carpineto and Romano, 2012
; 
Azad and Deepak, 2019
)
. Neural information retrieval provides a complementary route by learning representations that bridge query and document vocabulary
(
Mitra and Craswell, 2018
)
. Related work also explores multimodal state-space fusion 
(
Li et al., 2024
)
. Sentence embeddings and dense dual-encoder retrieval represent text for semantic matching
(
Reimers and Gurevych, 2019
; 
Karpukhin et al., 2020
)
. Language-guided visual correspondence has also been studied 
(
Song et al., 2024
)
, whereas neural passage reranking estimates the relevance of retrieved candidates
(
Nogueira and Cho, 2019
)
. Recent advances in ranking calibration 
(
Huang et al., 2026b
)
 and adaptive composition strategies
(
Huang et al., 2026a
)
 further enhance retrieval quality through structure-aware optimization 
(
Ye et al., 2025
)
. More recently, Query2doc uses language-model-generated pseudo-documents to expand queries for sparse and dense retrieval
(
Wang et al., 2023
)
. And video-LLM research also studies fine-grained semantic understanding 
(
Hu et al., 2025
)
. These general approaches motivate the use of semantic anchors and a reranking proxy here, while TaskBridge keeps the final retrieval stage lexical and catalog-grounded.

This issue is particularly pronounced in lexical product retrieval. BM25 primarily ranks products according to term-level matches between the query and product titles
(
Robertson and Walker, 1994
; 
Robertson and Zaragoza, 2009
)
; when a task-oriented query contains none of the product terms used in the catalog, BM25 cannot infer the required products from the task goal. Similar challenges arise in composed retrieval tasks, where multi-modal queries must be mapped to catalog items through semantic anchoring 
(
Chen et al., 2025
)
. Conversely, adding a large number of product terms to the query may increase term overlap, but can also introduce products that are related to the task yet unnecessary. Efficient hybrid search techniques
(
Yang et al., 2026a
)
 and robust noise-mitigation strategies
(
Yang et al., 2026b
; 
Hu et al., 2026
)
 address related challenges in bridging semantic gaps while maintaining retrieval efficiency. Task-oriented query reformulation therefore requires a controllable mapping between task semantics and catalog vocabulary
(
Maji et al., 2019
; 
Manchanda et al., 2019
)
.

The Query Reformulation Task at the 2026 SIGIR Workshop on eCommerce evaluates this problem under the Automatic and Interactive settings
(
SIGIR Workshop on eCommerce Organizers, 2026
; 
TREC Product Search and Recommendations Track, 2026
)
. The Automatic setting requires one reformulation for each original query, whereas the Interactive setting permits up to four reformulations. These settings examine whether reformulated queries can support task-oriented product retrieval from single-query and multi-query perspectives, respectively, and provide an evaluation scenario for studying the transformation between task intent and product-catalog expressions.

To address this task, we propose TaskBridge, a catalog-anchored reformulation framework for task-oriented e-commerce queries. TaskBridge constructs a phrase lexicon and semantic anchors from product titles, generates candidates with the original query, retrieval feedback, and lexicon constraints, and selects queries according to the submission setting. It then submits the catalog-grounded reformulations to standard BM25 retrieval.

The main contributions of this paper are as follows:

•

We analyze the central challenge in task-oriented e-commerce query reformulation: preserving task intent while converting implicitly expressed product needs into vocabulary that matches catalog expressions;

•

We propose TaskBridge, a catalog-anchored reformulation framework that integrates Catalog Semantic Anchoring, Unified Five-Candidate Rewriting, and Protocol-Aware Selection under the Automatic and Interactive settings;

•

2. 
Method

TaskBridge is an inference-time query reformulation framework for task-oriented e-commerce queries. It organizes catalog phrase resources, candidate query generation, and protocol-aware selection into a unified pipeline. First, it constructs catalog phrase resources for different downstream operations from the product corpus; it then generates candidate reformulations with complementary preferences around the original query; finally, it determines the submitted query set according to the Automatic or Interactive protocol. This design jointly considers task-intent preservation, catalog-vocabulary matching, and multi-query complementarity within a single selection framework. Figure

 illustrates the overall architecture.

TaskBridge does not involve model training. By default, it uses Qwen3.5-9B 
(
Qwen Team, 2026
)
 to generate query reformulations and make role-prompted judgments, Qwen3-VL-Embedding-8B 
(
Li et al., 2026a
; 
Bai et al., 2025
; 
Yang et al., 2025
)
 to retrieve query-relevant semantic anchors, and Qwen3-VL-Reranker-8B 
(
Li et al., 2026a
; 
Zhang et al., 2025a
)
 to estimate the relevance between the original query and product titles. These models respectively support candidate construction, anchor retrieval, and selection-agent scoring, while the final product ranking follows the BM25 retrieval pipeline specified by the challenge.

3. 
Experiments

3.1. 
Challenge Setup

TaskBridge is evaluated on the Query Reformulation Task of the 2026 SIGIR Workshop on eCommerce. The task focuses on task-oriented e-commerce queries and requires systems to transform users’ descriptions of activities or goals into queries suitable for product retrieval, with reformulation quality assessed based on the retrieval results. The evaluation includes the Automatic and Interactive protocols: Automatic submits one reformulation for each original query, whereas Interactive permits up to four reformulations 
(
SIGIR Workshop on eCommerce Organizers, 2026
; 
TREC Product Search and Recommendations Track, 2026
)
. TaskBridge uses the same product collection, task queries, catalog semantic resources, Unified Five-Candidate Rewriting module, and BM25 retriever under both protocols; the final difference lies in the number of reformulations that each protocol permits the system to retain.

3.2. 
Implementation Details

TaskBridge is an inference-time system with no training or fine-tuning. Candidate reformulations are generated by Qwen3.5-9B, semantic anchors are retrieved by Qwen3-VL-Embedding-8B, and relevance between queries and product titles is judged by Qwen3-VL-Reranker-8B 
(
Qwen Team, 2026
; 
Li et al., 2026a
; 
Li et al., 2026b
)
. Candidate generation performs at most two rounds of retrieval feedback; semantic-anchor retrieval retains the top 50 candidates; multi-role voting uses three roles and requires at least two votes to support reformulation; and the two lexicon-constrained routes each select phrases from at most 80 candidates. Candidate selection uses BM25 results with a depth of 100, reranks the top 10 products, and aggregates the top 5 reranking scores. Automatic retains one candidate, whereas Interactive retains at most four. Each selected reformulation is then executed independently with BM25, retaining the top 100 products.

3.3. 
Evaluation Metrics

The challenge platform reports Task Completion NDCG@10, Essential Product Recall@100, and MAP. Task Completion NDCG@10 measures the extent to which the top ten products support task completion; Essential Product Recall@100 measures the coverage of essential products among the top 100 results; and MAP measures the average precision over the complete ranking list. These metrics reflect the quality of the result head, essential-product recall, and overall ranking performance, respectively 
(
SIGIR Workshop on eCommerce Organizers, 2026
; 
TREC Product Search and Recommendations Track, 2026
)
.

4. 
Conclusion

This paper presented TaskBridge, a catalog-anchored framework for task-oriented e-commerce query reformulation. By combining catalog semantic anchoring, five complementary rewriting strategies, and protocol-aware selection, TaskBridge maps implicit product needs to catalog expressions for BM25 retrieval. Interactive achieves higher scores than Automatic in both evaluation phases, suggesting that complementary reformulations can better support task-oriented product retrieval.
