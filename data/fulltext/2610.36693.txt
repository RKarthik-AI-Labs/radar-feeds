Abstract

Security pipelines often fuse several data sources and protect a downstream
model with a cross-source anomaly detector. Such detectors are usually judged
by their clean false-positive rate and by how well they catch random
corruption. Neither metric captures an adversary who controls some sources
and targets a specific downstream decision. We propose a threat-quantification
score, the 
task-conditioned visibility margin
, defined as the least
detector evidence a manipulation must reveal per unit of harm to the
downstream task. The margin has a closed form, takes about 
s per
certificate, yields a minimum-evidence attack, and identifies task-relevant
directions the detector cannot see. On a 17-channel industrial telemetry benchmark, a
detector that flags 82.8% of random single-channel corruptions is first-order
blind on 64.7% of task/subset pairs in an audit of one- to four-channel
subsets. Task-conditioned manipulations cause more than twice as many falsely
reassuring errors as generic stealthy ones, and more of them evade the alarm on
four of five splits, mainly at small budgets. Used to prioritize audits on
held-out subsets, the margin finds a median of four verified failures in ten
checks, compared with none for random order and two for alarm-based ranking,
and performs comparably to the task-gradient baseline. It also ranks vulnerable
configurations of the learned GDN and TranAD detectors and of a five-source
crop-yield panel.

I 
Introduction

Analysts rarely have the budget to verify every data source that feeds a
model. When several sources are fused and checked by a cross-source anomaly
detector, a practical question follows: 
which source subsets should be
audited first?
 Standard detector metrics do not answer it. A detector can
flag most random corruptions and still miss the coordinated change that a
downstream decision depends on.

The mechanism is geometric. A detector constrains only the directions its
residual responds to; when the task depends on the remaining directions, a
coordinated change across sources moves the decision while the residual
barely moves.

This is the static, task-conditioned form of unobservable false-data
injection 
[
1
]
. We turn it into a per-subset threat score for
learned, nonlinear detectors and downstream models, and use the score to rank
audits.

Related Work

Stealthy false-data injection.

Liu et al. 
[
1
]
 showed that an attacker who knows the
measurement model of DC state estimation can inject a change in the

corrupt

Evading anomaly detectors in industrial systems.

Urbina et al. 
[

VII 
Limitations and Conclusion

Because 
 is a local first-order screen rather than a global predictor of finite-budget damage across heterogeneous operating states, the pooled state-and-attack margin–harm rank correlation on Hydraulic is weak (
). Accordingly, 
 is used to prioritize verification within an audit, not to estimate calibrated failure probabilities. Our evaluation covers a laboratory hydraulic rig and a five-source crop-yield panel across PCA, reconstruction, GDN, and TranAD detectors; broader architectures, physical process constraints, and time-varying source compromise remain to be evaluated.

Clean anomaly detection and resistance to targeted source manipulation are distinct properties. The margin makes the local task–detector trade-off explicit and directs finite verification toward the most weakly monitored cases.
