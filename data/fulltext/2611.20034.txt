Abstract

Search agents trained with reinforcement learning decide what to retrieve, when to search again, and when to answer. In real corpora, one claim is copied and paraphrased across many pages while the primary source settling a question appears once. We show that reinforcement-learned search agents grant echoes the credence of independent sources and lose their ability to abstain. Across five base models from 3B to 32B and seven released RL agents on multi-hop Wikipedia questions with primary evidence intact, eight paraphrased false-claim echoes overturn 58–73% of previously correct answers, while exact-match RL drives deleted-evidence abstention below 15%. Conditioning on retrieval localizes the failure to evidence acquisition: once primary evidence enters context, agents overrule eight echoes as reliably as one, whereas echoes fill top ranks and halt search early, so larger budgets, forced search turns, or passive passage filtering alone fail to recover displaced evidence. We introduce 
EchoSpec
, specifying reliability across five corpus transformations with known target answers and training in two stages. Verification warm-start teaches multi-hop bridge-entity recovery past echoes, after which group RL optimizes an evidence-gated 
-reward with an outcome-independent acquisition bonus and scale-preserving advantages retaining the optimal rule 
. Across Qwen2.5-3B, Qwen2.5-7B, and Llama-3.1-8B backbones on nine evaluation suites, 
EchoSpec
 cuts eight-echo deference by 30–44 points on Qwen2.5-7B down to 29–31%, nearly quadruples eight-echo reach to 26–27% with zero-shot transfer to unseen BGE and BM25 retrievers, and raises standalone QA precision to 47–55%. Code is publicly available at 
https://github.com/unihsy/EchoSpec
.

We trace both failures directly to how search agents are trained. Across five base models from 3B to 32B and seven RL agents on multi-hop Wikipedia questions with primary evidence intact, eight paraphrased echoes of a false claim overturn 58–73% of answers correct on the clean corpus in 
Figs.

Conditioning on retrieval localizes echo deference to 
evidence acquisition

Echoes succeed by keeping primary evidence out of view: they fill top retrieval ranks, halt search after one or two queries, and slash primary-evidence reach from 53–72% to 20–31%. We introduce 
EchoSpec

EchoSpec

ABSTAIN

Stage 1 warms up the policy on grounded multi-hop verification trajectories so the agent learns to query bridge entities past Turn-1 echoes. Stage 2 runs group RL with an evidence-gated reward scoring 
 when grounded or rightly abstaining, 
 on answerable refusals, and 
 when wrong, with an acquisition bonus preserving 
 and scale-preserving advantages preventing collapse.

6  Conclusion and Limitations

Reinforcement-learned search agents follow paraphrased echoes because standard QA corpora conflate claim frequency with provenance, and they lose abstention because binary exact-match rewards leave wrong guesses unpriced. Larger budgets, forced turns, or passage filtering fail once policies stop on bare claims or query decoys. 
EchoSpec
 resolves both via five corpus transformations, a bridge-entity verification warm-start, and an evidence-gated 
-reward with an acquisition bonus and scale-preserving advantages, generalizing across Qwen2.5 and Llama-3.1 backbones and unseen dense and lexical BM25 retrievers.

Limitations.

While we test up to eight turns over dense and BM25 indices of 21M Wikipedia passages, live web search adds dynamic rankings, SEO manipulation, and compromised sources, and world construction requires multi-hop items with known evidence, motivating open-ended multi-source synthesis.
