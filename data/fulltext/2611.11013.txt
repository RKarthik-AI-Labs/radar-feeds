Abstract

Click-through rate (CTR) prediction is a core component of recommendation and
advertising systems. Large language models (LLMs) offer rich semantic
priors that improve prediction quality, particularly in cold-start and
long-tail settings. Injecting a full-scale LLM into a CTR pipeline is,
however, prohibitively expensive at inference time: existing LLM-enhanced CTR
methods avoid this cost by caching LLM outputs offline rather than
serving the LLM online. We propose a resource-efficient pipeline,

SlimFM
, that makes online LLM-based semantic modeling
practical: (1) dependency-aware structured pruning compresses LLaMA3-8B by
44% to a 4.5B-parameter backbone; (2) LoRA-based field-aware fine-tuning
trains the pruned model to produce discriminative field-level semantic
embeddings from lightweight textual prompts; (3) a semantic-guided fusion
module injects these embeddings into the feature interactions of a
conventional CTR model. Across four datasets and six CTR backbones, SlimFM retains over
99% of uncompressed-model accuracy while reducing inference latency by up to

 relative to existing LLM-enhanced baselines.

Introduction

CTR prediction is a critical component in recommender systems and digital
advertising 
(
Guo et al., 2017
; 
Zhou et al., 2018
; 
Gligorijevic et al., 2019
)
.
Large language models can enrich this pipeline with semantic and world
knowledge that ID-based models lack, particularly for cold-start items and
long-tail users 
(
Xi et al., 2024
; 
Li et al., 2023
; 
Qiu et al., 2024
)
. The primary obstacle is computational cost: modern CTR backbones comprise on the order of 1–10M parameters, while
contemporary LLMs such as LLaMA3-8B comprise 8B or more, making inference
with a complete LLM infeasible under real-time serving constraints. Prior LLM-enhanced CTR methods therefore pre-compute and cache LLM outputs
offline, serving only the CTR model online.
Representative methods such as KAR 
(
Xi et al., 2024
)
, LLM-CF

(
Sun et al., 2024
)
, CTRL 
(
Li et al., 2023
)
, and EASE 
(
Qiu et al., 2024
)

incur, on average, over 290 times higher computational cost than standard CTR
baselines 
(
Cui et al., 2025
)
. This approach
transfers the computational burden from online serving to offline preprocessing
rather than eliminating it; moreover, cached outputs must be regenerated
whenever the item or feature catalog changes. This work investigates whether structured pruning of the LLM, combined with
parameter-efficient fine-tuning, can compress the backbone sufficiently to
enable online serving within the same request path as the CTR model, without
significant degradation in semantic quality.

Contributions.

•

A dependency-aware structured pruning procedure that compresses
LLaMA3-8B to a 4.5B-parameter backbone (44% smaller) while preserving the
representational capacity needed for downstream semantic-embedding
extraction.

•

Field-aware LoRA-based fine-tuning and a semantic-guided
fusion mechanism together enable a pruned LLM to be served online
rather than cached offline inside a latency-critical CTR pipeline.

•

Across four datasets 
 six CTR backbones, 44% parameter reduction
retains 
99% of baseline accuracy at up to 
 lower inference
latency than existing LLM-enhanced CTR baselines.

Conclusion

We show that dependency-aware structured pruning of LLaMA3-8B, combined with
field-aware fine-tuning and semantic-guided fusion, enables online LLM-based
semantic modeling within a latency-critical CTR pipeline while retaining over
99% of full-model accuracy. These results demonstrate that structured pruning
is not merely an optimization but a necessary mechanism for practical
LLM-enhanced CTR deployment. Our evaluation is restricted to four CTR datasets and a single LLM family (LLaMA3-8B), so generalization to other backbones, domains, and LLM architectures remains to be verified.
