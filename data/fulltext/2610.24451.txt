1 Assessing the CAD Capabilities of General-Purpose AI Models:  A Case Study of GPT-6 Astra Qiang Zou*, Jiajun Shen, Zhijie Yang, Yuhao Wang, Shitianyi Leng, Dongshuo Yin State Key Laboratory of CAD&CG, Zhejiang University, China Abstract— Recent advances in general-purpose AI models and agentic systems have enabled them to perform some CAD tasks. A systematic assessment is now needed to establish what these systems can accomplish and how well their outputs meet engineering requirements. This paper presents an empirical study of GPT-6 Astra, a SOTA general-purpose AI model, across four basic CAD tasks: model understanding, generation, editing, and evaluation. We design test cases spanning different levels of complexity and engineering requirements. The results show that GPT-6 Astra can support multiple stages of CAD workflows, with successful outcomes in cases involving nontrivial geometric complexity. This suggests that general-purpose AI is beginning to offer practical value in almost all steps in CAD workflow. However, gaps remain between generating visually plausible geometry and producing valid, functional, and editable CAD models. Recurring errors in freeform geometry, parameter dependencies, and mating relationships between parts limit engineering usefulness. Agentic iterative refinement can improve results but does not consistently resolve critical errors; long completion times further constrain practical use. Keywords—Computer-aided design, general-purpose artificial intelligence, agentic systems, CAD evaluation, solid modeling, parametric feature modeling I. Introduction Recent advances in foundation models, code generation, and tool use have enabled general-purpose AI systems to perform a growing range of computer-aided design (CAD) tasks. For example, they can interpret design inputs, execute modeling commands, inspect geometry, and revise outputs using feedback [1–3]. Systematic assessment is therefore needed to establish which CAD tasks these systems can support, whether their outputs meet engineering requirements, and where specialized CAD methods remain necessary.  CAD models encode design intent and support subsequent editing, analysis, and manufacturing. Their quality therefore depends on geometric accuracy, solid validity, parametric editability, and conformance to functional and process requirements [4, 5]. A model may reproduce a part’s visible appearance well while containing disconnected passages or incompatible assembly interfaces. It may also match the initial geometry yet lose required features when a dimension changes. These failures highlight the need to assess the accuracy, validity, usability, and behavior under modification of AI-generated CAD models. Relevant evaluation studies exist. Picard et al. [6] investigated drawing interpretation, CAD generation, and machining feature recognition, although their tests used an oversimplified case: block with a blind hole. More recent benchmarks assess CAD generation, reconstruction and editing [7–11]. CADWorld evaluates agents operating FreeCAD through its graphical interface [12]. These studies address different subsets of CAD work, and most of their test cases are synthetically generated and differ substantially from those encountered in engineering practice. This paper presents the first systematic, in-depth empirical assessment of general-purpose AI capabilities covering the very four basic CAD tasks of model understanding, generation, editing, and autonomous evaluation [13]. The full report can be found in the Appendix. Specifically, we use GPT-6 Astra as a case study, conducting six kinds of CAD tests: part classification, machining feature recognition, CAD generation from engineering drawings, text, and rendered images, and parametric reconstruction from boundary representation (B-rep) models. The cases span different levels of complexity and engineering requirements. All cases are drawn from real engineering practice. The evaluation results suggest emerging practical value for AI in design intent understanding in conceptual design and initial geometric modeling for detailed design. AI can now effectively interpret design intent from text and images, construct geometry, build parts and assemblies, and develop designs from functional requirements. However, recurring errors in freeform geometry, parameter dependencies, and mating relationships between parts limit the usefulness of the outputs. Refinement corrects some defects but leaves others unresolved or exacerbates other geometric errors; moreover, long completion times impose a further practical constraint. Professional verification remains necessary in AI+CAD.   * Corresponding author. Qiangzou@cad.zju.edu.cn

Assessment Design This section provides a high-level overview of the assessment objectives, design rationale, and experimental settings. Detailed descriptions are available in the full evaluation report in the Appendix: Chapter 1 presents the overall framework and settings, while the first sections of Chapters 2–7 describes the corresponding task and evaluation criteria in each tested CAD tasks. A. Assessment objectives and design rationale The objective of this study is to determine whether today’s AI-generated CAD models are suitable for engineering use. This requires examining whether general-purpose AI can understand mechanical structures, develop designs autonomously from requirements, and repeatedly evaluate and revise its work as engineers do. These questions motivate a joint assessment of four basic CAD tasks: understanding, generation, editing, and evaluation. Understanding parts and features involves interpreting geometric structure and engineering meaning [14, 15], while generation expresses a design in a CAD representation [16]. Editing tests whether the model preserves intended relationships when design parameters change [5]. Evaluation supports these activities through checking and iterative refinement. Together, these tasks provide a basis for assessing both the creation of CAD models and their suitability for subsequent use. Also, they can be easily decomposed into the six kinds of CAD tests as shown in Table I, which can be connected to low-level detailed CAD workflow. Engineering suitability depends on both the quality of the initial model and its behavior during subsequent modification. A CAD model must represent the intended geometry and function, maintain solid validity, and preserve required relationships as design parameters change. We therefore assess geometric accuracy, solid validity, functional adequacy, and parametric editability separately. We also examine the agent’s checking and refinement process to determine whether successive revisions correct defects, preserve requirements already satisfied, and improve model quality. Together, these assessments establish what the system can accomplish and how reliably its outputs can support engineering design. TABLE I   Main test sets and task-specific sources of complexity. Case design principles Cases are drawn from engineering practice and selected to examine how geometric complexity and interactions among features, components, and parameters affect engineering usefulness. Case selection follows the capability being assessed, with distinct design considerations for understanding, generation, editing, and iteration/evaluation. For understanding, cases test interpretation at both the part and feature levels. Overall geometry must support identification of the part, while local geometry must support recognition of machining features and their relationships. Intersecting features, nested structures, and ambiguous boundaries are included to probe interpretation beyond isolated, readily recognizable shapes.  For generation, cases vary both the input representation and the extent to which the design is specified. Drawings, rendered images, and text impose different interpretation demands, while functional requirements leave design decisions to the system. Internal structures and mating interfaces introduce requirements that cannot be assessed from external appearance alone. For editing, cases exercise dependencies among features and components, testing whether reconstructed parametric models preserve required geometry and relationships when dimensions change. Initial geometric agreement and subsequent editability are therefore assessed separately.  For evaluation, it is examined throughout the tests by relating the agent’s own checking and refinement to independently verified output quality. This establishes whether revisions correct defects, preserve requirements already satisfied, or introduce new errors. Complexity is defined within each task according to the interpretation demands, feature interactions, and parameter dependencies involved. These levels support comparisons within tasks, while cross-task analysis examines recurring difficulties in meeting engineering requirements. C. C. Experimental setting and verification All tests use GPT-6 Astra at the MAX reasoning setting in the ChatGPT client, with Codex mode connected to a Linux server. Each run uses an independent conversation with the memoryless working mode. The agent can read the supplied files, execute code, configure its working environment, and use available tools subject to the task instructions. Web search is enabled in the main tests and disabled in selected supplementary runs. Within a single conversational turn, the agent can carry out multiple cycles of execution, checking, and revision. For all the details of the setup, please refer to Table 1-2 in Appendix. Output quality is verified separately from the agent’s own assessment, using geometric comparisons, solid-validity checks, and inspection against task-specific engineering requirements as appropriate. For parametric reconstructions that pass the initial solid-validity checks, prescribed manual dimension changes test whether the model rebuilds correctly and preserves the required relationships. These tests measure the editability of the generated model. Recorded completion times cover the full task execution, including environment setup, tool use, and autonomous refinement. The full evaluation report in the Appendix provides the complete task instructions, case descriptions, task-specific procedures and verification criteria, and detailed results. III. Part classification This task evaluates whether GPT-6 can correctly classify parts from the geometric and topological information in 3D CAD models. The test set contains 12 models spanning 10 part classes, with four simple, four moderately complex, and four complex cases. Figure 2 shows representative examples. 
   Simple: screw/bolt Moderate: pulley Complex: crankshaft Figure 2  Part classification examples at different levels of complexity With the names of 10 candidate part classes provided and web search enabled by default, GPT-6 correctly classified all 12 models, achieving 100% accuracy. Mean completion times for simple, moderately complex, and complex cases were 2 min 30 s, 2 min, and 2 min 30 s, respectively. The longest individual test took 4 min. Two additional conditions were tested separately: withholding the candidate class names and disabling web search. Two representative complex cases were selected for each condition, and all were classified correctly. A classification error arose when both conditions were applied together: with no candidate class names and web search disabled, GPT-6 misclassified a quick-disconnect coupling as a check valve (one-way valve), as shown in Figure 3. It identified the internal spring, valve stem, and valve disc, but treated the function of the valve mechanism as the purpose of the coupling as a whole.

6 B. Feature recognition This task tests whether GPT-6 can identify individual machining features in a B-rep model and recognize the relationships between them. It must identify both the general feature type and more specific forms, such as through holes and blind holes, without a list of candidate feature types. The test set contains two simple, two moderately complex, and two complex models. Figure 4 shows representative examples. 
   Simple Moderate Complex Figure 4  Feature recognition examples at different levels of complexity Feature recognition was correct in three of the six cases. In two others, GPT-6 identified the actual features correctly but also labeled some groups of faces as features when they were not. In the remaining case, most features were recognized correctly, with a small number of errors. Mean completion times for simple, moderately complex, and complex cases were 30 min 30 s, 40 min 30 s, and 1 h 25 min, respectively. The longest individual test took 2 h 1 min. Results for the same model were similar with web search enabled and disabled. Each condition gave better results for some distinctions, such as whether holes were through or blind. A recurring error was to label groups of faces that did not constitute machining features as features, producing false positives. In complex models, GPT-6 also treated two separate circular holes as a single feature and mistook an external cylindrical surface for a hole (Figure 5). 
  Two circular holes identified as one feature The orange regions are spatially separate Cylindrical surface misidentified as a hole The orange region is on the outside of the solid Figure 5  Errors in distinguishing feature instances and identifying feature types in complex models In summary, current capabilities and limitations are: • When features are independent or have only simple nesting relationships, GPT-6 can correctly recognize all machining features, including holes, slots, pockets, and fillets. • When features are nested at multiple levels, intersect, or are truncated, GPT-6 can recognize most machining features but still makes some errors. In models with complex overall shapes, it may identify groups of faces as features when they are not. It also has difficulty recognizing internal features when the geometry is densely packed and internal structures are largely hidden from view.

CAD generation from engineering drawings This task evaluates whether GPT-6 can combine information from multiple views, section views, and dimension annotations to reconstruct the 3D geometry of parts, while ensuring that separately generated parts meet the assembly requirements. The test set contains five single-part cases and three cases involving multiple parts. Two cases are simple, three are moderately complex, and three are complex. Figure 6 shows representative reference models. 
   Simple Moderate Complex Figure 6  Examples for CAD reconstruction from engineering drawings at different levels of complexity All parts passed the solid validity checks in six of the eight cases. The other two cases failed because of defects such as self-intersections and non-manifold connections. Of the three cases involving multiple parts, one could be assembled successfully, one had interference between components, and one could not be assembled because of missing structures and incorrect dimensions. Mean completion times for simple, moderately complex, and complex cases were 26 min 30 s, 43 min 40 s, and 31 min 20 s, respectively. The longest individual test took 1 h 8 min. Typical errors involved inaccurate reconstruction of local structures and incorrect parameters at mating interfaces. In assemblies, the resulting mismatches in mating dimensions caused problems such as overlapping solid bodies (Figure 7). 
   Reference model Dovetail mating profile Generated model Deviation in the reconstructed dovetail profile Generated assembly Red regions show overlapping components Figure 7  Dovetail profile reconstruction errors and assembly interference In summary, current capabilities and limitations are: • GPT-6 recovers the main geometry reasonably well when the relationships among drawing views are clear and the part primarily contains conventional holes, slots, and regular patterns. For parts with multiple steps, nested pockets, or complex transitions in thin walls, however, it remains prone to misinterpreting the structural arrangement and omitting local features. Invalid solids also occur in some cases. • For assemblies with few parts and clearly specified mating relationships, GPT-6 can generate part models that fit together. When an assembly has more parts and more complex mating requirements, it struggles to reproduce critical mating dimensions accurately, leading to interference or preventing assembly altogether. • In some complex cases, GPT-6 approximates geometry that would conventionally be modeled using draft, shell, or loft operations with basic extrusions and cuts. These approximations do not consistently preserve the required local geometry.

The test set contains four simple, four moderately complex, and four complex cases. Two additional complex assemblies were used for stress testing, and four complex cases were also tested with web search disabled. Figure 8 shows representative reference models. 
   Simple (single part) Moderate (single part) Complex (assembly) Figure 8  Examples for CAD generation from text at different levels of complexity All 12 cases received full scores on the listed requirement checks, and 11 passed the solid validity checks. These are separate outcomes: a full requirement score does not establish complete engineering functionality. Mean completion times for simple, moderately complex, and complex cases were 42 min, 1 h 0 min 15 s, and 1 h 37 min 30 s, respectively. The longest test took 3 h 17 min. Of the two stress-test cases, one received a full requirement score, but neither passed the solid validity checks. In the four cases tested with web search disabled, all four received full requirement scores, and two passed the validity checks. Typical errors included leaving gaps where structures should connect continuously and adding unnecessary components that served no supporting, connecting, or motion-transmitting function (Figure 9). 
  Incomplete local connection The red circle marks a gap at the connection Unnecessary component The orange component serves no functional purpose Figure 9  Typical errors in CAD generation from text In summary, current capabilities and limitations are: • For parts with clearly specified dimensions and feature positions, or mechanisms with clear load, motion, and mounting requirements, GPT-6 can develop a design from the text, determine its dimensions, and generate individual part models or assemblies. During iteration, it can add missing features and adjust component positions. The mean number of iterations across the 12 cases was four. • For models requiring continuous surface transitions or coordinated motion between components, GPT-6 may leave gaps where parts should join, or leave drive components unsecured or disconnected so that motion cannot be transmitted. The test set contains two simple, two moderately complex, and two complex cases, with an additional complex housing used for stress testing. Tests also varied the combination of input views and included conditions with web search disabled. Figure 10 shows representative examples. 
   Simple Moderate Complex Figure 10  Examples for CAD reconstruction from images at different levels of complexity All 21 test runs produced STEP models, and every solid in the outputs passed the solid validity checks. For the six cases tested using six orthographic views and one axonometric view, the mean silhouette intersection over union (IoU) across views ranged from 0.8790 to 0.9915 (Table IV). GPT-6 recovered the main shape and component layout reasonably well, but local structures and absolute dimensions still showed substantial discrepancies. For example, the reconstructed control arm in Figure 11 retains the overall outline, but its smooth sidewall surfaces are replaced by segmented surfaces, the root fillets are missing, and local steps are introduced. Parametric reconstruction and editability This task tests whether GPT-6 can use the geometry and topology of an existing model to reconstruct sketches, modeling features, and parameter dependencies, and produce an editable CAD model that matches the input geometry. The test set contains five simple, three moderately complex, and three complex cases. The three complex cases were also tested with web search disabled. Figure 12 shows representative examples. 
   Simple Moderate Complex Figure 12  Examples for parametric reconstruction from B-rep models at different levels of complexity All 11 cases produced parametric models in SOLIDWORKS format. Six models (54.5% of the total: five simple and one moderately complex) passed the solid validity checks and were therefore eligible for parameter-editing tests. The remaining two moderately complex models and three complex models contained invalid solids. They were excluded from parameter-editing tests because they failed the validity prerequisite; their behavior under editing was not tested. Each of the six eligible models underwent 10 single-parameter edits, with only one dimensional parameter changed at a time. An edit passed if the parameter change took effect, dependent geometry updated correctly, the edited model passed the solid validity checks, and the model could be restored to its original state. Of the 60 edits, 52 passed (86.7%). Four models passed all 10 edits, accounting for 36.4% of all 11 cases: three simple models and one moderately complex model. Mean completion times for simple, moderately complex, and complex cases were 7 min 36 s, 13 min 20 s, and 50 min, respectively. The longest individual test took 1 h 52 min. None of the three models tested with web search disabled passed the solid validity checks. The reconstructed models contained sketches, features, and dimensional parameters and resembled the reference shapes, yet incomplete parameter dependencies limited their editability. In one case, increasing the housing depth did not correctly update the dependent hole features, and newly added material closed the existing hole openings. For models with complex surfaces, GPT-6 often uses a series of extrusions to approximate the original surfaces with planar faces. Smooth surfaces become densely segmented and stepped, producing large geometric errors. Cross-Task Analysis (1) GPT-6 demonstrates capabilities across several kinds of CAD tasks GPT-6 can classify parts and recognize geometric and machining features in existing B-rep models, and generate 3D models from engineering drawings, images, or text descriptions. In some tasks, it can also select a design concept from functional requirements, determine dimensions, and perform engineering analyses and checks. The tests include successful cases of feature recognition in relatively complex parts, generation and reconstruction of moderately complex models, and construction of relatively complex assemblies. (2) Generated models do not always form valid solids or meet functional requirements Most generated CAD models resemble the required shape, but errors can remain in their internal structures, critical dimensions, and feature layouts. These errors occur in both individual parts and assemblies: internal passages may not connect or remain separate as required, and components may lack the connections needed to secure them or transmit motion. Some errors have little effect on appearance but directly compromise essential functions. Shape complexity and incomplete input information do not fully explain these failures. Errors also occur in simple models, and even complete information about the target geometry does not guarantee a correct reconstruction. (3) Creating a 3D model does not guarantee that parametric edits will work correctly For models with simple shapes, few features, and straightforward dependencies, GPT-6 can already create editable parametric models that support dimensional changes and the corresponding updates to dependent features. Even some geometrically simple models have incomplete feature dependencies, while complex reconstructions frequently contain invalid solids. Changing a dimension can close a hole opening, remove a local feature, or displace a component. GPT-6 cannot yet consistently produce parametric models that behave correctly when edited. (4) GPT-6 can check and revise its own models, but cannot yet correct errors consistently During modeling, GPT-6 can inspect its results, identify some problems, and revise the models repeatedly. These revisions contribute to successful outcomes in some complex tasks. Yet the tests show that error often fluctuates over successive iterations, and some changes increase it. GPT-6 is therefore still unreliable at identifying the source of an error, deciding what to modify, and selecting the appropriate operation. Dependencies among geometric features and parameters complicate this process: a single change may affect several parts of the geometry and multiple design requirements. GPT-6 does not yet fully identify these dependencies or reliably anticipate and control the consequences of an edit. As a result, correcting one problem can introduce new errors. (5) Long modeling times limit practical use 

12 GPT-6 often cycles repeatedly through model generation, inspection, and correction, which takes considerable time. Mean completion times for several kinds of modeling and reconstruction tasks were in the tens of minutes. Some complex cases took more than an hour, with the longest taking 3 h 41 min. These times make it difficult to provide the rapid feedback and frequent revisions needed in design work. Despite progress in modeling performance, practical use remains limited. 13 V. Conclusion This study assessed GPT-6 Astra across CAD model understanding, generation, editing, and autonomous evaluation. The system interpreted geometry, constructed parts and assemblies, and developed designs from functional requirements. These successes indicate emerging practical value in conceptual design and initial modeling for detailed design. Engineering reliability remains limited by errors in connectivity, part mating relationships, and parameter dependencies. Geometric similarity and valid individual solids do not establish functional adequacy or reliable editability. Autonomous refinement corrects some defects but leaves others unresolved and can increase geometric error. Long completion times further constrain practical use. Reliable use requires professional verification of the initial model and its response to modification. Preserving the geometric and functional relationships needed for engineering use, while correcting errors without disrupting those relationships, remains a central challenge for CAD agents. Chapter1Evaluationobjectivesanddesign
1.1 Evaluationobjectives
This report systematically evaluates how well OpenAI's GPT-6 can understand and generate
CAD models. It examines how performance varies with task complexity, the strategies evident in
its execution records, and what it can and cannot do. The findings help assess which CAD tasks
GPT-6 is close to handling in practice, where its capabilities still fall short, and which research
directions deserve priority.
• Model understanding: Assess how well GPT-6 understands a CAD model's geometry and
topology, and what themodel represents in engineering terms.
• Model generation: Assess whether GPT-6 can generate CAD models from engineering
drawings, text descriptions, and rendered images, and construct a plausible parametric
modelinghistory from an existing CADmodel.
• Analyze theobservable steps GPT-6 takes to complete each task and establish what it can and
cannot do.
• Synthesize the test results to assess GPT-6’s overall CAD capabilities and identify directions
forfuture research in intelligent CAD.

cannot be identified uniquely, give themostlikely class and explain theuncertainty."
