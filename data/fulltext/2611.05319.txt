Abstract

In LLM-based recommendation, 
memory
 effectively plays the role of a user-level agent, typically built on top of a memory-augmented backbone. Yet under this paradigm users and items remain asymmetric participants: only the user is given a structured profile; that profile is consumed inside a bandwidth-limited reranker prompt; and it is frozen after warmup. Starting from this observation, we recast LLM-based recommendation as an 
agentic-communication
 process in four stages: 
Build
 a self-description, 
Transmit
 it along the collaborative graph, 
Understand
 the other side, and 
Sync
 it from the next interaction. Analysing the dominant memory-augmented paradigm through this formulation, we identify three open communication challenges that current pipelines leave unaddressed: the item side never produces a comparable self-description (one-sided 
Build
); profile-level signals are routed only into a bandwidth-limited reranker prompt rather than into the retrieval stage where they would not compete for context tokens (prompt-trapped 
Understand
); and the user’s description is frozen after warmup with no principled update rule (absent 
Sync
).
We instantiate the missing stages as 
Harp
 (
H
omophily-aware 
A
gentic 
R
ecommendation via dual 
P
rofiles
), a training-free, three-operator addition to a frozen LLM backbone: a rule-extracted symmetric item profile (zero extra LLM calls), a channel-decomposable profile-level homophily 
 consumed at retrieval time rather than inside the reranker prompt, and a single-pass reflective update realising 
in-context
 profile evolution without any gradient step. On four public benchmarks (Yelp, Books, MovieTV, Goodreads), 
Harp
 lifts Hit@1 over the strongest published baseline by 
–
, with family-wise Holm-corrected 
 on every dataset. Ablations and a per-case attribution study show that the operators contribute complementary gains, with the retrieval-side gate as the main source; we also report the trade-off introduced by the reflective pass. Our contribution is a unified formulation of agentic communication for LLM-based recommendation, together with a training-free three-operator instantiation on top of it.

Large language models (LLMs) are becoming the central reasoning engine of next-generation recommender systems 
Wu et al. (2024)
; 
Geng et al. (2022)
;

;

; 
Xu et al. (2025)
; 
Chen et al. (2026)
; 
Tang et al. (2026)
, a user is modelled as an LLM agent with persistent memory, and personalised recommendations are produced by routing collaborative-neighbour memories into a reranker prompt. Yet this paradigm provides only a partial answer: the user side is equipped with memory while the item side has no structured profile aligned with the user’s; the user profile is consumed solely inside a bandwidth-limited reranker prompt; and the profile built at warmup is not revised from recommendation outcomes afterwards. We recast LLM-based recommendation as a four-stage agentic-communication process between user and item agents (

–
Transmit
–
Understand
–
Sync
), and show that the dominant paradigm implements only a partial version of it, leaving three communication challenges open (Figure 
1
).

The four-stage framing and a motivating observation.

The stages 
Build
–
Transmit
–
Understand
–
Sync
 borrow directly from heterogeneous message passing on graphs 
Hu et al. (2020)
; 
Schlichtkrull et al. (2018)
 and from the classical study of homophily on social networks 
McPherson et al. (2001)
. The decomposition plays a 
dual
 role: as an 
analytical tool
 that asks which of the four stages a given pipeline implements and how well, and as a 
generative scaffold
 that prescribes the operator to add once a missing stage is identified. The four-stage view is thus an analytical framework for diagnosing a pipeline, not a model in itself. Recommender systems have passed through three architectural generations (collaborative filtering 
Koren et al. (2009)
; 
Sarwar et al. (2001)
, graph neural networks 
He et al. (2020)
; 
Veličković et al. (2017)
, and the recent agentic paradigm 
Wu et al. (2024)
), yet the collaborative signal pervading all three is indexed by interaction 
overlap
 rather than description-level compatibility, hence size-blind: whenever two agents’ description sets differ sharply in breadth, raw interaction-overlap similarity inflates the match for reasons of degree rather than relevance, and the pre-rerank candidate list inherits that inflation. An agentic communication pipeline therefore needs a profile-level, 
asymmetric
 coverage measure read from the query side’s vantage, which is the role the channel-decomposable homophily 
 of §
3.2
 plays once the item side is also allowed to speak.

Three communication challenges and three operators.

Applied to the memory-augmented family on four public benchmarks (Yelp, Amazon Books, MovieTV, Goodreads), this analysis identifies three open challenges where the communication falls silent. 
(Challenge 1) Asymmetric 
Build
.
 The user side carries a multi-field profile while the item side has no profile in the same schema. 
(Challenge 2) Bandwidth-trapped 
Understand
.
 Concatenating the user profile into the reranker does not move ranking accuracy: by the time the reranker is called, its context is fixed and the context budget is dominated by the memory block; 
Understand
 is the stage where profile-level signal must enter 
before
 the reranker is called. 
(Challenge 3) Absent 
Sync
.
 Profiles built at warmup are not calibrated against the feedback that warmup itself produces.

Each challenge is addressed by a primitive already validated elsewhere, composed without retraining. 
Against Challenge 1
, a structured-JSON user profile, in the spirit of agent-maintained profiles in prior agentic recommenders 
Zhang et al. (2024)
, is used on the user side, and a schema-aligned item profile is introduced via rule extraction at no extra LLM cost. 
Against Challenge 2
, the collaborative retrieval score is re-weighted by a profile homophily that generalises the scalar edge-homophily ratio 
Zhu et al. (2020)
 to a sum over semantic channels; homophily is used here as an analytical quantity, not as an optimisation target. 
Against Challenge 3
, a single Chain-of-Thought reflective pass 
Wei et al. (2022)
; 
Shinn et al. (2023)
; 
Madaan et al. (2023)
 optionally guarded by a conservative critic is added, realising 
in-context evolution
 of the user profile without any gradient update, a 
self-evolving
-agent primitive in the sense of the recent survey 
Gao et al. (2025)
. The composition is 
Harp
 (
H
omophily-aware 
A
gentic 
R
ecommendation via dual 
P
rofiles
), a training-free addition to a frozen LLM backbone that operates entirely through the 
LLM-as-agent
 interaction loop; its novelty lies in the systematic identification of these three primitives as the complementary response to the three challenges within the memory-augmented agentic family, each separable in ablation (§
4
).

Our main contributions are as follows:

•

Formulation.
 An 
agentic-communication
 formulation that models LLM-based recommendation as a four-stage process 
Build
–
Transmit
–
Understand
–
Sync
 and, used analytically, surfaces the three communication gaps that the dominant memory-augmented agentic paradigm leaves open.

•

Method.
 
Harp
, a three-operator training-free addition to a frozen LLM backbone: a rule-extracted symmetric item profile, a channel-decomposable homophily-gated retrieval, and a single-pass reflective update, each aligned with one of the three gaps.

•

Design rationale.
 Three design disciplines that predict where operator-level gains should appear: the channel-decomposability of profile-level homophily, the retrieval-vs-prompt design rationale, and the one-pass conservative update.

•

Empirical evaluation.
 On four public benchmarks, 
Harp

Extended Related Work

Multi-agent communication and skill-based self-improvement.

A rich literature studies how artificial agents develop communication protocols 
Foerster et al. [2016]
, 
Lazaridou et al. [2016]
, 
Mordatch and Abbeel [2018]
, 
Ren et al. [2020]
, and recent work extends this to LLM-based agents communicating in natural language 
Li et al. [2023]
, 
Hong et al. [2024]
, 
Du et al. [2023]
, typically in multi-round dialogues. A parallel line shows that LLM agents can accumulate reusable skills through experience 
Wang et al. [2023]
, 
Zhu et al. [2023]
, 
Madaan et al. [2023]
, 
Shinn et al. [2023]
: Voyager builds a skill library in Minecraft, GITM decomposes tasks into learnable skills, and self-refine approaches demonstrate iterative LLM self-improvement. 
Harp
 uses both lenses orthogonally: user and item agents exchange one-shot structured messages measured by ranking accuracy rather than dialogue fluency, and the reflective update operates on the agent’s 
profile
 under a acceptance rule (Eq. 
10
) rather than accumulating a skill library. The reranker-prompt bandwidth constraint (C1 in §
3.1
) and the channel decomposability of Remark 
1
 matter most in this one-shot, high-throughput setting.

LLM-based agents for recommendation (detailed thread).

A growing body treats recommendation as an LLM-agent task 
Zhang et al. [2024]
, 
Huang et al. [2025]
, 
Wang et al. [2024]
, 
Friedman et al. [2023]
, 
Xu et al. [2025]
, 
Tang et al. [2026]
: AgentCF 
Zhang et al. [2024]
 pioneers the user/item-as-agent formulation with free-text memories on both sides, but neither side carries a structured, channel-aligned profile; InteRecAgent 
Huang et al. [2025]
 introduces tool-augmented user agents; iAgent 
Xu et al. [2025]
 keeps a static per-user profile with no reflective update; i
2
Agent 
Xu et al. [2025]
 adds a per-user reflection loop but 
without
 an acceptance rule, so every proposed revision is applied; RecBot 
Tang et al. [2026]
 supports session-level dynamic memory; RecAgent 
Wang et al. [2025]
 simulates user behaviour. Relative to this line, three design choices in 
Harp
 are jointly novel: (i) a structured profile on the 
item
 side with the same four-channel schema as the user side (no prior work gives the item agent a structured, channel-aligned profile), (ii) a homophily signal consumed during 
retrieval
 rather than inside the reranker prompt, and (iii) an acceptance check on the reflective update (self-reflective predecessors apply every proposed revision). A parallel line develops general agentic memory architectures beyond recommendation (A-MEM 
Xu et al. [2026]
, Memory-R1 
Yan et al. [2026]
, Zep 
Rasmussen et al. [2025]
, H-Mem 
Ye et al. [2026]
) offering infrastructure pluggable at the 
Transmit
 stage; a recent dynamic-graph thread 
Tao et al. [2026]
 makes retrieval augmentation itself task-aware, conceptually close to our 
Understand
 routing.

Tuning- and grounding-based LLM recommendation.

A parallel line tunes or grounds the LLM against the recommendation objective rather than operating it as an agent: TALLRec 
Bao et al. [2023]
, CoLLM 
Zhang et al. [2025b]
, LLaRA 
Liao et al. [2024]
, BIGRec

; surveys

 place these on a spectrum of 
what
 in the LLM is adapted. 
Harp
 is complementary and sits at the 
interaction-loop
 end: it keeps the LLM frozen, leaves the collaborative signal in place, and restructures 
how
 user and item agents exchange profiles along the pipeline; it can in principle be layered on top of any of these backbones. The retrieval-vs-prompt rationale of §
3.3
 is motivated by adaptive retrieval in retrieval-augmented generation 
Asai et al. [2024]
, 
Jiang et al. [2023]

Relation to adjacent LLM-agent currents.

Three concurrent LLM-agent currents intersect with 
Harp
 without overlapping its main contribution: the 
self-evolving-agent
 line 
Gao et al. [2025]
, 
Zhang et al. [2026]
 (replacing fine-tuning with in-context rewrites; our

 view

Appendix B 
Experimental Setup Details

B.1 
Dataset Details

We evaluate on four public datasets widely used in recommendation research, used under their original terms of use for research, encompassing diverse domains such as e-commerce, social reading, entertainment, and local services. As stated in §
4
, we adopt the versions of these datasets augmented with natural-language user instructions from 
InstructRec
 
Xu et al. [2025]
. The original data sources and their characteristics are summarised below.

Books.

Derived from the 
Amazon review dataset
 
Ni et al. [2019]
, this subset focuses on book recommendations. It is characterised by extremely sparse interactions and a very large item space; user preferences in this domain are typically stable and highly content-driven, centred on specific genres, authors, or themes.

Goodreads.

Collected from the 
Goodreads
 social book-cataloguing website 
Wan et al. [2019]
, this dataset is comparatively dense. It features strong community interactions and rich metadata about books, including series information, and users often exhibit series-aware reading behaviours and are influenced by social signals.

MovieTV.

Also originating from the 
Amazon review dataset
 
Ni et al. [2019]
, this dataset covers movies and TV shows. The domain is marked by volatile user preferences often influenced by immediate context or trending content; while metadata such as genre and cast are important, item recency frequently plays a critical role in user decision-making.

Yelp.

Sourced from the 
Yelp Dataset
, this dataset consists of reviews for local businesses such as restaurants and services. It is characterised by strong categorical constraints (e.g., cuisine type) and by the critical importance of attributes like price range and location; user preferences are often highly context-dependent.

B.2 
Baseline Details

This appendix provides detailed descriptions of the baseline models used in our comparative evaluation in §
4
. Following the categorisation in the main text, we group the baselines into three families.

(I) Traditional recommenders.

These models mostly represent the pre-LLM paradigm (P5 being the exception), in which historical information is encoded as dense latent vectors without explicit semantic memory.

•

LightGCN
 
He et al. [2020]
: a graph-collaborative-filtering model that simplifies the GCN design by removing feature transformation and nonlinear activation, learning user/item embeddings via linear propagation on the user–item interaction graph.

•

SASRec
 
Kang and McAuley [2018]
: a self-attentive sequential recommender that models the entire user sequence to capture long-term and dynamic dependencies over the history.

•

P5
 
Geng et al. [2022]
: a unified framework that formulates recommendation tasks as sequence-to-sequence language modelling over a pre-trained T5 backbone, representing users and items as sequence tokens within personalised prompts.

(II) Single-sided LLM / agentic methods.

These approaches operate on raw interaction histories or static profiles without maintaining a persistent, structured, and evolving semantic memory.

•

Vanilla LLM
 
Liu et al. [2023]
: direct zero-shot prompting of an instruction-tuned LLM (
gpt-4o-mini
 in our protocol), in which the user’s history is serialised into a natural-language context and the LLM selects from the candidate list; a baseline measuring the LLM’s inherent capability without any memory design.

•

iAgent
 
Xu et al. [2025]
: an LLM agent with a static per-user profile constructed from historical interactions and descriptive data; the profile is fed into the LLM as context but does not adapt over time after the initial construction.

•

i
2
Agent
 
Xu et al. [2025]
: an extension of 
iAgent
 that adds a self-reflection mechanism, allowing the agent to refine its internal state after recommendations, with reflections confined to the individual agent’s experience with a specific user.

•

AgentCF
 
Zhang et al. [2024]
: an agent-based collaborative-filtering framework in which user- and item-agents interact autonomously and update their own preferences/characteristics, with memory updates localised to the interacting agents.

•

RecBot
 
Tang et al. [2026]
: a conversational recommender in which an LLM maintains a dynamic dialogue history and updates user-preference estimates during multi-turn sessions; dynamics are bounded to the current session.

(III) Memory-augmented agentic methods.

•

MemRec
 
Chen et al. [2026]
: the strongest published memory-augmented agentic recommender on these InstructRec splits, and our primary end-to-end baseline.
