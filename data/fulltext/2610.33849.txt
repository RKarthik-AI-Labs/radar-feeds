Abstract—Query-time filters that screen each retrieved docu-
ment before it reaches the generator are the dominant published
defense against retrieval-augmented generation poisoning, and
they report near-perfect detection on domain benchmarks. We
show that this performance is predicted by how the poison set
was built rather than by what the filter reads: removing a single
construction cue destroys the deployed decision before it destroys
the ranking. Two statistics computed from a poison set and its
clean corpus alone, with no defense, scores or labels, profile
the cue: containment, how often the target question appears
verbatim in the poison, and length overlap. Containment captures
a construction choice used in PoisonedRAG: its released code
prepends the target question to every inserted passage. Of the
21 published defense evaluations we survey, 8 verifiably run
poison built this way and 5 substitute a length cue; separately,
12 report no false-positive rate at the operating point they deploy.
Deleting the prefix from the attack authors’ own passages, and
nothing else, leaves 0.93 of them still retrieved for their target and
takes RAGuard, a published content filter we re-implement, from
AUROC 0.953 to 0.758 and its miss rate from 0.191 to 0.763.
Across four corpora, four attack families and ten per-document
defenses on one shared candidate set, the profile predicts where
detection saturates. Padding medical poison to clean length with
word-shuffled filler that carries no content takes TrustRAG’s
set-level stage from a miss rate of 0.000 to 0.827, and one learned
scorer of ours from 0.000 to 0.986 (0.321 at a matched 5% false-
positive rate) while its AUROC holds at 0.953 and the share
of injected poison reaching the context rises from 0.00 to 0.94.
The ranking survives; the decision does not. AUROC alone can
conceal this collapse, and a defense-aware attacker defeats our
own scorer, so we draw no defense claim from it. A detection
result is evidence only when it is reported with its poison set’s
construction profile and the false-positive rate at the threshold
actually deployed; we release the script that computes the profile.
Index Terms—retrieval-augmented generation, data poisoning,
benchmark validity, cost-sensitive classification, adaptive attacks

Ranking is not deciding.On medical poison padded to the
clean length with word-shuffled filler — identical length and
vocabulary, no coherent content — our learned scorer keeps
AUROC 0.953 and misses 0.321 at a matched 5% false-
positive rate, while at the threshold it deploys it misses 0.986;
TrustRAG’s set-level stage moves from 0.000 to 0.827 under
the same edit. The ranking is degraded; the operating point is
gone; and AUROC-only reporting cannot tell the two apart.
Padding with real clean prose also collapses a GPT-2 perplexity
threshold [15], [16] and RAGuard’s chunk score, but by content
dilution, not length: a mean-token likelihood is set by its
padding, and shuffled filler returns their miss rates to 0.000
and 0.000 (Section VI-B); we lead with the detectors whose
collapse survives the placebo. Once a cue has driven score mass
to the extremes every threshold rule selects the same documents,
so benchmark validity and operating-point evaluability are one
quantity measured twice.
What this does not show.One of the ten defenses is our own
learned scorer, the one system whose features can be ablated.
Every negative result below indicts it first and the constructed
benchmarks second: a defense-aware attacker passes 0.977 of
its poisoned documents past it (Section VI-B), and we propose
no defense. The profile is validated at four corpora and no
more, and at single (corpus, attack, defense) points its direction
is wrong for 40 of 90 (Fig. 5). Per-source trust is a mechanism
reported beside a provenance leakage of 1.000; it is not a
detection result, and nothing here is a guarantee.

poison set, computable before any defense runs, that predicts
which published benchmarks saturate — measured on regener-

on the poison sets of 21 published defense evaluations and
on every question-targeted attack that releases poison as text
(Sections II-E and V).(II)The dissociation the profile exposes
and its two failure modes:calibration collapse, which leaves
the ranking usable and the deployed point dead and is partly

on the cells the profile flags, no operating-point claim — ours
included — is falsifiable, verified by building the best available
cost-derived rule and finding that it changes no decision in 12
of 26 cells (Section VI).
II. BACKGROUND, RELATEDWORK,ANDLINEAGE
A. Poisoning attacks on RAG
Attacks differ in objective (answer manipulation [2]–[4],
[17], jamming [5], backdoors [6], [7], [18], universal corrup-
tion [19], stance flipping [20], utility degradation [21], prompt
injection [22], [23]), in knowledge (black-box, gray-box with
retriever gradients [7], [19], [24], supply chain [18]), and
in budget, from N documents per target query [2] down to
one [3]–[6], [8].
Two properties matter here. First, black-box poison is fluent
text, so the standing reading is that perplexity separates only
gradient-crafted strings and jamming blockers [2], [4], [5],
and that adversarial decoding evades it outright [25]. On
PoisonedRAG’s own NQ passages that reading holds: a GPT-2
perplexity threshold misses 1.000 of them with the question
prefix its code prepends and 1.000 without it, at AUROC
0.773 and 0.729. On poison regenerated from that attack’s
template for a medical corpus the same threshold misses 0.062
at AUROC 0.993: our skeletons are perplexity-detectable and
the attack authors’ text is not, which is a statement about
corpora and generators, not about the prefix. Second, attacks
frame the injection as acorrection: CorruptRAG asserts that
“the latest data confirms” the false answer [3], AuthChain adds
authority cues and timestamps [4], and the Purpose attack
writes poison as a non-contradicting update [26] — the surface
form of a legitimate restatement, which no construction cue
marks.
B. Defenses by stage and granularity
Defenses differ in where they act and what they decide on
(Table I).Offline: duplicate hashing [2], DRS’s covariance
outlier score [13], provenance attestation [27], offline per-
source reliability [28].Retrieval-time: chunk-wise perplexity
and query-similarity outlier removal [9], source-aware [29]
and graph [30] re-ranking.Pre-generation, over the retrieved
set: perplexity [16], gradient-masked token probability [31],
k-means removal (TrustRAG stage 1 [11]), hierarchical clus-
tering [10], a natural-language-inference (NLI) contradiction
graph [12], min-cut selection [14], conflict-free assembly [32],
supervised injection filters [33], [34].Generation-time: isolate-
then-aggregate with a certified bound [35], rationale generation
and source-aware consolidation [36], [37], attention-based filter-
ing [38], [39], capability-based isolation [40].Post-generation:
answer activations [41] and forensic attribution [42], [43].
Three published works share the name RAGuard: a
misleading-retrieval benchmark [44] and two defenses. Here
“RAGuard” is Cheng et al.’s query-time chunk filter [9]; the
other, RAGuard-ZKIP [45], decides from generator outputs
and exposes no per-document decision or operating point, so
it is a capability row in Table XI, not a baseline.

D. Evaluating a security classifier
The failure modes measured here are catalogued by this
venue’s own literature: experimental bias [58], [59], Arp et
al.’s sampling bias, spurious correlations and inappropriate
baselines [60], base-rate and closed-world problems [61],
shortcut features a benchmark rewards and a deployment does
not [62], [63], and robustness that is an artifact of how the
attack was constructed [64]–[66]. That a ranking metric can
hide what a detector does at a deployable false-positive rate is
this community’s own lesson [67]–[69]. The control ladder of
Section V follows the adaptive-evaluation recipe.
E. The gap
Over Table I and the capability-only systems of Table XI—
whose rules are certified budgets, graph cuts, fixed sieves in
series, or static provenance and trust weights, none a harm-
conditioned threshold—no surveyed defense conditions its
operating point on deployment harm—thresholds are clean-
score quantiles [9], [13], fixed hyperparameters, or consistency
rules with no threshold semantics [10]–[12], [14]—and none
maintains per-source trust with a defined feedback channel [27]–
[29]. The third gap is measured rather than asserted (Table II).
Of 21 published defense evaluations, 8 verifiably run Poisone-
dRAG poison with the target question inside it — 3 ship the
attack’s attack.py line unchanged, and one releases poison
in which we measure the question in 500 of 500 passages —
5 substitute a length or repetition cue (a fragment repeated
2–10 times, or poison of 119–162 words against clean chunks
fixed at 100, where word count alone reaches AUROC 0.999),
2 evaluate a construction with no question in it (gradient-
optimised tokens, a rewritten gold passage), and 6 cannot be
determined from what was published. No false-positive rate
at the deployed operating point is reported by 12 of them;
only 1 reports a ranking metric at all; and the 3 that remove
the prefix [11], [70], [71] report attack success at one fixed
threshold with no false-positive side — two observe the filter
collapse, and the third’s text describes the prefix-removed
setting as its default while its released attack code has no
such path. One concurrent per-document detector reports an
AUROC beside a 5% clean-removal budget [72], on query-
inclusion poison. The attack literature has meanwhile named
query inclusion as a filterable artifact in order to build poison
without it [73]–[75]; CamoDocs’ fixed-threshold query detector
is the containment-only floor of Section V. None measures
what a published filter’s ranking and deployed point each lose
when the artifact is removed from the attack authors’ own text,
whether the loss is calibration or signal, or the length cue. That
is this paper.
III. THREATMODEL AND THESEMANTICS OF ACONTROL
A. System and trust boundary
In a deployment d, a dense retriever over an index I returns
a candidate set R(q) for a query q. Aquery-time admission
gate— the defense class measured here — scores each xi ∈
R(q) and emits an action ai ∈ {PASS,ESCALATE,BLOCK} ;
only passed documents enter the generator context. Ingestion
binds each document to a source identifier s(xi). The defender
controls everything except document content; the adversary
controls the content of documents in sources it owns or has
compromised. The deployment label d is a property of the
query path, not of a document, so no document field can select
the threshold applied to it.

B. Adversary and defender
G1–G3 (integrity, availability, stealth). Have a chosen x∗
passed so its payload reaches the generator [2], [3], [17];
have benign documents blocked or honest sources down-
trusted [5], [56], [78]; do the first with noESCALATEor
BLOCKattributable tox ∗ ors(x ∗).
C1. Insert or edit N documents per target query across S
controlled identities.
C2. Choose all document text and every self-declared field;
no such field is a scorer input.
C3. Query the system as a user and learn whether a document
reached the context: a hard-label oracle for decision-based
evasion [66].
C4. Corrupt a fraction ε of adjudication records (simulation
only, Appendix A; every pipeline result is atε= 0).
C5. Cannot alter model weights, the ledger except through C4,
orIother than by insertion.
C6. Per Kerckhoffs the adversary knows the algorithm, ρd, the
thresholds and the trust update rule. Every attack reported
here is black-box (C3 plus a surrogate encoder [79], [80]);
white-box results are labelled where they appear [65], [81].
D1–D4. Identities are bound by the identity provider, so Sybil
resistance is inherited, not provided [82]; adjudication is
independent of the gate; and the calibration split carries
no adversarial document.
Out of scope: retriever-weight and index tampering beyond
insertion [18], [83], generator-side effects of instructions that
reach the context [22], and the veracity of a fluent falsehood
with legitimate provenance.
C. What makes a rung an attack
Acontrolis a poison set that removes one profiled con-
struction cue (Section V) while holding the payload and the
attack family fixed. Each rung is a payload-preserving edit
available under C1 and C2 alone: no gradients, no surrogate,
no queries to the defender. A rung that lowers detection is
therefore an attack, not dataset hygiene. A rung counts as a
successfulattack only under a conjunction: the document is
retrieved after the edited corpus is re-embedded and re-ranked,
its payload is intact against the pre-edit text, and it passes the
gate. A length or lexical edit moves the embedding, and a
document that leaves R(q) has evaded nothing, so retrieval
survival is re-measured at every rung and printed beside the
miss rate (Section VI). The published attacks we reproduce
are scored under the same conjunction.
The defense-aware attacker.One query-based attacker is run,
on NQ, against our own scorer: the mimicry rung (question
and cue phrases removed, length matched) followed by hard-
label word substitution under C3 against the scorer’s admission
decision, at most 500 oracle queries and 60 iterations per
document, with a 22M-parameter surrogate encoder for retrieval
survival. The resulting documents are then scored by every
other defense unchanged, so for those defenses the number is
atransferattack and a lower bound on what an attacker tuned
to each of them would achieve [66].
IV. THEMEASUREDSTACK,AND THEDECISIONLAYER AS
INSTRUMENT
Every number here comes from one harness. This section
fixes it, then states the decision layer Section VI uses as an
instrument, not a contribution.
A. Corpora, poison, and defenses
Corpora.Four: general (NQ targets from PoisonedRAG [2]
over a BEIR sub-corpus [84], 24,295 documents), medical
(PubMedQA [85], 21,000), financial (FinQA [86] and TAT-
QA [87], 24,991) and legal (CUAD [88] contracts chunked
to a fixed 150 words, 33,530, which makes the clean legal
lengths constant). Each corpus carries 100 target questions split
40/20/40 by query into scorer-train, calibration and evaluation;
every cell is evaluated on 40 queries, NQ included. Retrieval
is all-MiniLM-L6-v2 [79], [80], no reranker, k=10 candidates.
The scale is small and fixed (400 candidates per cell and seed,
one 384-dimensional retriever, every clean-quantile threshold
of Table I fitted on the 20 calibration queries’ clean top- k);
Section VIII states what that costs. Realised corpus poison
rate 0.0147–0.0233; the scorer is calibrated at its training bags’
candidate-level prevalence π0=0.343 and deployed at 0.090–
0.746.
Poison.Four published families: PoisonedRAG ( N=5) [2],
CorruptRAG-AS ( N=1) [3], HijackRAG-style instruction
injection [17] and a jamming blocker [5]. In fifteen of sixteen
corpus–family cells our generator regenerates the poison from
the published template. The sixteenth, NQ under PoisonedRAG,
is the attack authors’ own text, assembled the way their code
assembles it (the target question, then the released passage;
Section V); deleting that prefix and changing nothing else is
the leading control of Section VI. Source identities are ours
— three of the four attacks specify no source model — and
the identifier is an exact label indicator here (provenance-only
AUROC1.000), so no trust number is a detection result.
Defenses.The ten defenses of Table I score one identical can-
didate set and return one keep/remove decision per document;
fitted rows use the condition’s own calibration split. Three rows
are one learned scorer of ours at three thresholds, and Table V
splits the last at two trust weights, so the paper counts eleven
table rows, ten defenses, and eight distinct score systems, and
every count below says which it uses. DRS is fitted as its
Algorithm 2 specifies, on the clean top-50 candidates of the 20
calibration queries: one held-out half defines the directions and
holds more documents than the encoder has dimensions, the
other supplies the 99th-percentile threshold, which its published
protocol takes in-sample with no held-out false-positive rate
(Table I prints the held-out one; Section VIII the rank-deficient
fit an earlier draft ran).
B. Metrics and statistics
Reported separately, no composite index: false-negative
(FNR) and false-positive ( FPR) rates at each defense’s
deployed rule and at matched FPR; area under the receiver-

Two quantities profile a poison set before any defense runs;
a ladder of controls removes what they find (Fig. 1 states
both halves in advance). This section defines them; Section VI
measures them.
A. Two construction quantities
Let P be a poison set built for a target question set Q, and
Cthe clean corpus it is inserted into.
Verbatim containment.Normalise a string by lower-casing,
stripping punctuation, and collapsing whitespace. A poison
document x∈P built for target q∈Q iscontainedif the
normalised q occurs as a contiguous substring of the normalised
x. Containment is the fraction ofPthat is contained.
Length overlap.Let [ℓ5, ℓ95] be the fifth and ninety-fifth
percentiles of the word counts in C; length overlap is the
fraction of P inside that interval. We print the clean spread
ℓ95/ℓ5 beside it: with no length variance in C a low overlap
describes the corpus rather than the attack, and CUAD’s fixed
150-word chunking gives it a spread of one.
Neither quantity runs a defense, reads a score, or needs
a held-out label (Table III). Containment is 1.00 wherever
poison is regenerated from a published template and 1.00 on
the one condition whose poison we did not write, because
PoisonedRAG’s released implementation prepends the target
question to every inserted passage [2]. Nor is the profile specific
to our corpora (Table IV). Every question-targeted attack that
releases poison as text inserts it at full containment by its own
construction: PoisonedRAG on three corpora, HijackRAG at

B. The control ladder
A control removes one profiled cue while holding payload
and attack family fixed.Template:the published generator,
unmodified.Prefix removed:every verbatim occurrence of
the target question deleted and query-token coverage capped,
payload untouched; containment falls to zero. On NQ that rung
is applied to the attack authors’ own passages, deleting the
prefix their code writes and nothing else, which is why it is the
control Section VI leads with.Length matched:poison padded
with benign filler from the clean corpus until its word count
lies inside [ℓ5, ℓ95]; two placebo fillers of the same length, the
clean sentences with their words shuffled and pseudo-words,
separate length from content (Section VI-B).Both.A fifth
rung,mimicry, edits a real domain passage so that only the
target fact changes [3], [89], and is length-matched in turn.
Every rung is scored under the conjunctive criterion of
Section III-C [65], [66], with retrieval survival, its first leg,
printed under every rung of Fig. 3. For adjacent rungs
r, r′ and defense f, the detection attributable to the cue r′
removes is FNRr′(f)−FNR r(f) at the deployed point, what
the deployment loses, and the same difference at matched
FPR = 5%, what the ranking loses; every table below prints
both.
Template leakage and hold-out.Domain-regime scorers
train on a disjoint half of our skeletons (Section IV) and
are evaluated on the other; the only shared structure is the
verbatim prefix and the answer slot, which the prefix-removed
and mimicry rungs delete. The general-regime scorer trains on
HotpotQA and MS MARCO poison only, so both NQ arms are
scored by a model that has seen neither their corpus nor their
generator (held-out-skeleton AUROC financial 1.000; legal
0.999; medical 1.000, on the calibration split, so it certifies
only a shared register).
What the protocol does not establish.Four corpora do not
validate a predictor, so we report the per-point scatter (Fig. 5)
rather than a correlation coefficient; the profile’s direction is
wrong at 40 of 90 (corpus, attack, defense) points there, so
it predicts which corpora saturate, not which defense does on
which cell. A clean profile is necessary for an informative
benchmark, not sufficient: unprofiled cues may remain, and
surviving all five rungs certifies nothing beyond those five
edits.

B. The control ladder
Fig. 3 runs the ladder of Section V-B, with retrieval survival,
the first leg of the conjunction, printed under every rung.
The two cues are independent, and each is load-bearing on
a different corpus. Removing the verbatim prefix moves our
scorer’s deployed miss rate by +0.009 on medical and +0.421
on financial; matching poison length moves it by +0.983 on

a study running one control reads the other cue as content.
Both readings have been published: TrustRAG’s Table 4 and
TriShieldRAG’s adaptive arm reach opposite verdicts from one
control each [11], [71].
Length matching pads and never truncates, so on medical
the padded document is mostly clean prose. Two placebo
arms hold length fixed and vary the filler (Table VII): the
same clean sentences with their words shuffled across the pool
(identical length and vocabulary, no content), and pseudo-words
of matched length. Our scorer and TrustRAG’s set-level stage
collapse at the deployed point under all three fillers; the shuffled
arm gives 0.986 at AUROC 0.953 for our scorer and 0.827
for TrustRAG, so the collapse is not content dilution. The two
negative-log-likelihood detectors do not survive the placebo:
perplexity and RAGuard miss 0.361 and 0.519 under clean filler
and 0.000 and 0.000 under shuffled filler, because a mean-token
likelihood is set by whatever it is padded with: fluent prose
lowers it, shuffled words raise it. Padding cannot give an NLL
detector a length control at all, so their collapse is withdrawn
and both arms are reported as bounds. The pseudo-word arm
is a vocabulary control rather than a length control: it pushes
the document off the clean vocabulary, our scorer’s ranking
recovers to AUROC 0.999 while its deployed miss rate stays
at 0.968, and DRS, an embedding-distance rule, recovers from
0.990 to 0.605; the shuffled arm holds vocabulary fixed and
is the placebo we read. Where the clean corpus is a constant
width, as on legal, padding overshoots and installs an inverted
cue instead of removing one (Table X, Appendix B).
Survival is the leg that fails on medical with both cues
removed: 0.18 of the poison is retrieved for its target, so the
miss rate of 1.000 drawn there is over the survivors and the
attack’s success under the conjunction is 0.18; on financial and

the two rungs and adding style mimicry gives the strongest
attack in this paper: on NQ the miss rate goes from 0.118 at
the first rung (the archive-stored passages, already without the

fixed-width chunking that makes CUAD a length constant [88]
also makes padded poison expensive to retrieve.
The query-based attacker of Section III-C, tuned to our own
scorer on NQ (the grid is Appendix B’s E4-D1 table), passes

Verdict: the load-bearing cue is corpus-specific.One

D. Mechanism and repair
Table VIII ablates the one scorer whose features we control.
On length-matched medical, deleting the document-length
feature moves the deployed miss rate from 0.986 to 0.204
under clean filler and from 0.988 to 0.026 under shuffled filler,
ECE from 0.416 to 0.111, while AUROC risesfrom 0.885
to 0.992: the feature carried the threshold and was hurting the
ordering, because a scorer that learned short documents are
poison reads padded poison as an argument for admission. The
repair is a fallback, not a recovery of content: containment is
still 1.00 on that cell, deleting the query-overlap features instead
gives 0.992 at AUROC 0.680, and with both cues removed
nothing repairs anything — every row of that column misses at
least 0.990 and the full scorer’s AUROC is 0.483. A gradient-
boosted tree on the identical features reaches FNR 0.000 at
AUROC 1.000 on the same cell, so the collapse is a property
of this MLP’s calibration, which indicts our scorer first; the
tree dies with everything else at 0.571 once both cues are gone.
Source identity is an exact label indicator here (Section IV-A),
so an earlier draft’s trust-on version of this table, like every
trust-weighted AUROC in Table VII, re-ranked on a leaked
label.
Repair follows the taxonomy. An empirical clean-quantile
floor — refuse to admit below the q-th percentile of clean
scores, cross-fitted on held-out clean queries — returns the
length-matched medical miss rate from 0.983 to 0.750 for +1.47
points of FPR; where the signal has collapsed no ordering is
left for a quantile to exploit, and on medical with both cues
removed it moves the miss rate by -0.029.
Verdict: the profiled cue that remains carries the
calibration.Remove one and the threshold moves onto the
other; remove both and the ranking goes with them.
E. Decision agreement and inertness
Table XIII (Appendix B) counts what the decision layer
changes. Across the 26 corpus-by-attack cells the harm-blind
τ=0.5 classifier, the cost-derived τd and the trust-adjusted
τd,s produce bit-identical actions on every candidate in 12 of
them. On templated medical poison the fraction of candidates
whose score falls anywhere in the decision band is 0 (0.00%):
a shortcut drives score mass to the extremes, where every
threshold rule is the same rule (397 distinct score values per
cell). 5 of those carry ρd=1, where the cost-derived ruleis
the τ=0.5 classifier; the table flags them and none counts as
evidence.
Where the score is unsaturated the cost-derived point acts: on
length-matched legal, harm per 1,000 queries falls from 4195
to 2585, and on prefix-removed medical from 1600 to 900; the
same rule wrapped over RAGuard’s and perplexity’s scores
reproduces the direction on length-matched legal (18460 →
3915 and 50100 → 3725) at a printed false-positive cost that
rule 5 of Section VII calls a closed door (Fig. 7, Appendix B).
Verdict: no.On the benchmark the literature reports against,
the harm-blind, cost-derived and trust-adjusted rules take bit-
identical actions on every candidate, so no operating-point
claim on those cells — ours first — can be falsified by an
experiment run on them.
VII. A REPORTINGPROTOCOL FORQUERY-TIMEFILTERS
Six rules, each forced by a result above.
1) Publish the construction profile beside every reported
AUROC: containment rate, length separation, and the
clean band’s own width, for the poison actually evaluated.
Reconstruct the passage the attack inserts, not the fragment
its archive stores: the difference is the result.
2) Run at least two controls, and report the inert ones.
Which cue is load-bearing is corpus-specific, and an inert
control is the evidence that the other cue carries the
detection. A control that changes length needs a placebo
filler: a filler with content moves the score for its own
reasons.
3) Report FNR and FPR at the rule you would deploy,
beside AUROC, and, for any detector that emits a
probability, ECE at the realised prevalence rather than
the training base rate. AUROC alone cannot separate
calibration collapse from signal collapse.
4) Print the number of distinct score values beside every
threshold-derived metric.Where a score takes few values
every threshold rule is the same rule, and no operating-point
claim is falsifiable there.
5) Compare at matched FPR, or on a swept harm axis
with the harm ratio stated[51], never at each system’s
own operating point. A miss rate of zero at a false-positive
rate above one half is a closed door, not a detector.

payload preservation[65]. An edit that removes a cue and
then falls out of the candidate set, or that survives retrieval

VIII. LIMITATIONS ANDCONCURRENTWORK
Scope.Three of our four corpora carry poison regenerated from
published templates, so every separability number there is an
upper bound; the exception, NQ, carries the argument, and the
prefix control on the attack authors’ own passages (RAGuard
at AUROC 0.953 against 0.758) is one of the two controls
everything rests on. The other, the placebo filler, withdrew a
headline rather than repairing one: an earlier draft led with
perplexity’s length-matched collapse, which word-shuffled filler
of the same length undoes (Table VII).
What the measurement does not cover.Seven published
defenses are our re-implementations at their published rules
(Table I prints nominal against realised false-positive rates);
two of them, TrustRAG stage 1 and RAGDefender, also ran as
released, on the poison as released (Table VI), RAGDefender
with its artifact’s MiniLM embedder rather than the 1.5B-
parameter encoder its paper reports; RAGuard, DRS, perplexity,
NLI and the duplicate filter ran only as our ports. An earlier
draft calibrated DRS on 199 clean documents, fewer than
the 384 embedding dimensions: the covariance is then rank-
deficient, its lowest-variance directions are sampling noise, the
cross-fitted threshold sat on fold directions that differ from the
deployed ones, and DRS blocked most of the benign corpus
at a nominal 1%. That number was ours, not DRS’s, and a
wider encoder would not repair it: the operative quantity is
calibration documents per dimension. Every DRS cell now uses
the calibration size its Algorithm 2 specifies, with the threshold
on a held-out half of that set, and realises 0.035 on medical and
0.103 over the four template cells (withdrawn rates: Table V’s
note). The scale is 100 target questions, 40 evaluation queries
per cell and corpora of 21k–34k documents, against a sub-area
norm of 106–107; every other fitted threshold is a quantile
of the calibration split’s ≤200 clean documents, so a 99th
percentile is that sample’s second-largest value, and because
the split is fixed across seeds no per-seed spread contains that
variance. One retriever, no reranker, no NQ length rung (the
released NQ poison lies inside the clean band, overlap 1.00),
one poison rate, one trust setting: the Contriever condition,
the synthetic corpus and the legitimate-update and poison-
rate arms are cut, no Sybil or competing-attack condition
runs [90]–[92], and poison is never separated from a legitimate
correction [26]. Every adaptive number is a lower bound: one
attacker, tuned to our scorer and transferred unchanged to
the others (Section III-C), with no LLM-guided or human
arm, far below the budgets of [81] and without the strongest
learned generator [93]. Generator-side harm is secondary (a
0.5B generator, substring matching, the template rung alone:
0.150).
Concurrent work.Section II-E measures the 2026 co-
hort’s constructions rather than re-running the systems. The
ranking/operating-point gap for thresholded detectors under
shift is known [67], [68], [94], [95]; shortcut features in security
benchmarks are old [58], [60]–[63]; and the query-inclusion ar-
tifact and the prefix-removed control are concurrently observed
on the attack side and in one defense (Section II-E). What is
new here is tying the gap to a construction cue of the poison
set, the label-free profile that predicts it, and the false-positive
side none of the above measures.
IX. CONCLUSION
Query-time RAG poisoning filters are reported on bench-
marks whose poison carries the target question verbatim,
because the field’s most-cited attack constructs it that way
in its own released code [2]. Of the 21 published evaluations
we surveyed, 8 run poison built exactly that way, and 12 report
no false-positive rate at the point they deploy. Two numbers,
computable from a poison set and a clean corpus alone, say
in advance, at corpus level, which cells will saturate. On the
cells that do, unrelated defenses, ours among them, report near-
perfect detection at operating points no cost-derived threshold
can tell apart. Delete one cue and the deployed operating point
dies while the ranking survives, degraded; delete both and the
ranking goes with it. The first failure is repairable, at a printed
false-positive cost; the second is not.
Until an AUROC arrives with its poison set’s construction
profile beside it, it is a statement about how the poison
was written, not about what the filter will stop.

ETHICALCONSIDERATIONS
This work is defensive. It introduces no new attack capability:
every attack we run is a published attack run as its own code
runs it (PoisonedRAG [2]), a published attack re-implemented
from its paper (CorruptRAG [3], HijackRAG [17], jamming [5],
HotFlip [24]), or a standard adaptive-evaluation technique [65],
[66] applied to defenses we implement ourselves. The control
ladder removes construction cues from those attacks; it gives
an attacker nothing the published generator does not. The
trust-attack scenarios instantiate the long-known taxonomy of
reputation-system attacks [56], [78], [82] in simulation only.
We follow the Menlo Report principles [100].Respect for
persons: no human subjects were involved; adjudication is by
ground-truth labels and the human-review path is simulated,
not staffed.Beneficence: the poison generators are released
only as templates bound to the public benchmark question sets
used here (NQ, HotpotQA, MS MARCO, PubMedQA, FinQA,
CUAD), and the generated passages are labelled adversarial and
are not fluent enough to pass as authoritative medical, financial
or legal guidance outside a benchmark harness. We release
the adaptive attacks because a defense evaluated without them
is not a defense [65].Justice: the deployments modelled are
those where the burden of poisoning falls on end users, and the
cost-sensitive formulation makes the trade-off between missed
poison and withheld benign content explicit rather than hidden
in a fixed threshold.Respect for law and public interest: no
live system was probed, nothing was inserted into any third-
party corpus, and all datasets are used within their licences;
the medical corpus is published abstracts and the legal corpus
contracts released for research, neither containing patient or
client data.
Dual use, and disclosure.The decision dial is dual-use in both
directions: an operator who sets ρd or κs to admit what should
be blocked moves the operating point without moving any
ranking metric, which is the dissociation this paper measures
under attack. Two properties of the implementation bound that,
neither a guarantee: every decision is written to an audit record
carrying ˆp, κs, ˜p, τd, τd,s and the action taken, so a shifted
operating point is visible after the fact, and κs is clipped below
at κmin, so no accumulated reputation moves the threshold
past a stated bound. Responsible disclosure does not arise:
no third-party or live system was probed, and every defense
whose failure we report is one we re-implemented and ran
ourselves. We release the trust-attack and threshold-probing
code too: both instantiate behaviour a Kerckhoffs adversary is
assumed to have already, and the threat model (Section III) puts
the mitigation in authenticated source identity and adjudicated
evidence, not in secrecy.
