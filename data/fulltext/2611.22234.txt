Abstract

Attribution-guided pruning scores components by their causal contribution and protects the
important ones, and it is increasingly applied to compress reasoning language models without
destroying their chain of thought. Its reliability rests on an unexamined assumption: that
reasoning-importance can be faithfully measured. Across four small reasoning models we show this
measurement is fragile in three ways, each of which changes pruning decisions.
First, scoring final-answer tokens, the natural choice, saturates in 
every

chain-of-thought model we test (answer-token negative log-likelihood, NLL, of 
–
) because the model states
its answer inside its own reasoning; guiding head pruning with this target costs 
 and

 accuracy points relative to scoring reasoning tokens on the two models we power with five
seeds, while perplexity barely moves and even reverses sign at 7B. Second, causal importance is
not localizable to single units: single-neuron effects sit at the measurement floor, their
cross-split ranking is noise, and they cancel: a group’s ablation effect disagrees in sign with
the summed effects of its members in 90% of groups. Reproducible importance emerges only at
groups of 
 neurons. Third, contrastively isolated “reasoning-specific” importance is
not separable from generic importance, and protecting it is worse than protecting raw importance.
We additionally pre-registered the hypothesis that distillation-induced concentration of
importance predicts the pruning cost; it is not supported, and since our grid confounds
concentration with scale we report it as unresolved rather than reversed. The result is a short
set of measurement rules: score reasoning tokens, attribute at coarse granularity, and evaluate
with accuracy rather than perplexity.

Introduction

Small language models with chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning 
(
Wei et al., 2022
)
 are attractive
targets for on-device deployment, and pruning is the standard route to shrink them. Classical
pruning scores parameters by their contribution to language-modeling loss

(
Han et al., 2015
; 
Frantar and Alistarh, 2023
; 
Sun et al., 2024
)
, and a recent line of work makes
pruning 
reasoning-aware
: calibrating on self-generated CoT activations

(
Lucas et al., 2025
)
 or attributing importance causally and protecting high-scoring components

(
Sheth et al., 2026
)
, echoing interpretability findings that specific heads carry outsized
functional load 
(
Michel et al., 2019
; 
Voita et al., 2019
)
.

All such methods share an unexamined dependency: they assume the reasoning-importance signal is
faithful. Attribution requires three implicit choices: 
what
 to score against (the target
token set), 
at what granularity
 to score, and 
whether
 reasoning-specific components
are separable from generically important ones. We show each choice is fragile in reasoning
models, and that the first has large, measurable consequences for pruning.

Our thesis is restatable in one sentence: 
to guide pruning without silently degrading
reasoning, importance must be scored on reasoning tokens rather than answer tokens, measured over
groups of at least 
 units rather than single components, and taken as raw importance
rather than a reasoning-specific contrast.
 We frame this
as a methodological complement to reasoning-aware pruning, not a critique of any method. We
verified from source that the two closest methods do not use the saturating target: the
reconstruction-based pruner of 
Lucas et al. (2025)
 reconstructs weights over prompt and decode
tokens, which include the chain of thought, and the causal-attribution pruner of

Sheth et al. (2026)
 scores a span that is not the model’s own CoT followed by its answer, and is
evaluated on non-distilled instruct models. The saturating target is a 
naive-but-plausible
 choice
that we characterize, not a flaw we attribute to prior work.

Related Work

Reasoning-aware pruning.

Reconstruction-based 
(
Frantar and Alistarh, 2023
)
 and
activation-magnitude 
(
Sun et al., 2024
)

(
Lucas et al., 2025
)
 or score components by causal
contribution and protect the important ones 
(
Sheth et al., 2026
)
. Structured pruning of whole

(
Michel et al., 2019
; 
Voita et al., 2019
; 
Ma et al., 2023
)
.
We do not propose a new mask; we audit the reliability of the importance signal these methods
consume, which is complementary and, we argue, prior.

Causal attribution and circuit localization.

Our estimators follow the
activation-patching tradition 
(
Conmy et al., 2023
)
 and its gradient approximation, attribution
patching 
(
Syed et al., 2023
)
, whose known failure modes at fine granularity our
granularity result quantifies in the reasoning setting. Interpretability work reports that
specific heads carry disproportionate functional load 
(
Michel et al., 2019
; 
Voita et al., 2019
)
;
we ask whether such localization transfers into a reliable pruning prior, and find specific

Scoring targets.

We measure the importance of a component by how much removing it
raises the model’s loss on a chosen set of tokens. Concretely, head importance is the increase in
negative log-likelihood (NLL) on that token set when a query head’s output is mean-ablated, that
is, replaced by its average over the calibration set (a standard way to delete a component’s
contribution while keeping activation statistics in range). The 
scoring target
 is precisely
this set of tokens: the choice of which tokens to score is the free decision that attribution
methods must make, and it is our object of study. We compare three targets: 
 = final-answer tokens with the CoT present (the naive
choice); 
 = the CoT reasoning tokens (the corrected choice); and 
 = answer tokens with
the CoT removed, a control isolating whether the pathology is answer tokens 
per se
 or
answer-after-CoT.

Pruning and evaluation.

We prune at the granularity of whole query heads for three
reasons. First, the attribution-guided methods we audit operate on heads and comparable coarse
units, so this is where a reasoning-importance ranking actually drives a pruning mask. Second, a
head is structured hardware-friendly compute (an entire attention sub-computation), unlike an
individual weight. Third, our own granularity result (Section 
7
) shows that
importance is not reliably measurable at the level of single neurons, so the head is close to the
finest unit at which the ranking is trustworthy. Concretely, we prune a query head by zeroing the
corresponding rows of the query projection 
 and columns of the output projection 
; under
grouped-query attention the key and value projections are shared across heads and we leave them
untouched (we assert a maximum 
 norm change of exactly 
 for every model). We report
accuracy (exact match, permissive extractor accepting 
\boxed{}
, “the answer is 
”, or
the final number), CoT-token NLL, and format-success rate on 
 held-out problems from
GSM8K, a grade-school math word-problem benchmark 
(
Cobbe et al., 2021
)
; calibration is drawn from the training split, so held-out evaluation is
disjoint by construction. To compare models at matched operating points we sweep the pruned-head
fraction and use each model’s

9 
Discussion and Recommendations

The three fragilities yield concrete guidance for attributing reasoning-importance in CoT
models, for pruning and equally for analysis or data selection.

•

Score reasoning tokens, not answer tokens.
 Answer-token targets saturate in every
CoT model tested and, for pruning, cost 
–
 accuracy points at 1.5B. Score the CoT span.

•

Do not attribute at single-unit granularity.
 Neuron-level causal importance cancels
and is irreproducible; use groups of 
 units, or coarser objects such as heads.

•

Do not rely on reasoning-specific localization.
 Specific and generic importance are
not separable for masking; contrastive protection does not beat raw importance.

•

Evaluate with accuracy, not perplexity.
 The two decouple under reasoning pruning
and disagree in sign at 7B.

Each is inexpensive to apply, and they complement rather than replace existing reasoning-aware
pruning methods.

Scope of the claims.

We are explicit about what each finding is powered to support.
The universal saturation of 
 (Section 
4
) is measured in 
all four

models and is the broadest claim. The pruning cost (Section 
5
) is established at
1.5B with five seeds on GSM8K, and we deliberately do not generalise it to 7B or to other tasks;
the 7B arm is reported so that a reader can see its variance rather than omitted because it is
inconvenient. The granularity and specificity results (Sections 
7

and 
8
) are measured on one model family and we present them as such. The
concentration mechanism is unresolved. We would rather state a narrow claim that holds than a
broad one that does not.

10 
Limitations

Our well-powered evidence is at 1.5B on GSM8K with five seeds; the 7B arm has three seeds, does
not resolve the concentration hypothesis, and leaves D2’s gap indeterminate rather than null.
Concentration is confounded with scale in our 
 grid, so Section 
6

cannot separate the two. We study attention-head pruning; head-level protection is in tension with
the 2:4 semi-structured sparsity formats (two nonzero weights per group of four, which GPUs accelerate) that deliver kernel speedups, and we do not report end-to-end
latency, so our claims are about the 
signal
, not about achieved compression. Our estimators
are mean-ablation for heads and attribution patching for neurons; the latter did not pass a
per-neuron validation gate (rank-stratified Spearman 
 against true ablation), which is why we
report neuron results only at the collective, group level, and which is itself consistent with the
granularity finding. Task coverage is math reasoning; extending to non-math CoT is future work.
The single-head probe in Appendix 
A
 is preliminary and uncontrolled, and we draw no
claim from it.

11 
Conclusion

Attribution-guided pruning of reasoning models rests on an importance signal that is fragile to
measure. The natural answer-token target saturates in every CoT model and, used for pruning,
collapses reasoning accuracy while barely moving perplexity; causal importance is not localizable
below 
 neurons because single-unit effects cancel; and reasoning-specific importance is
not separable from generic importance. A pre-registered concentration mechanism is tested and
unresolved. The practical consequence is a set of measurement rules that make reasoning-importance attribution
reliable, offered as a complement to the reasoning-aware pruning literature.
