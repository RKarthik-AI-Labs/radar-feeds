Abstract.

Advertising platforms subsidize advertisers through billing discounts, and 
Advertiser Subsidy Allocation
 (ASA) chooses a subsidy level for every account to maximize total discounted revenue under a platform-wide subsidy-rate constraint. In practice, operators configure these subsidies from business rules, advertiser tiers and operating goals. Such configuration draws on rich business experience, yet as the advertiser base grows, tier-level policies can neither resolve how individual accounts respond across subsidy levels nor allocate a shared budget at the granularity of single accounts. We propose CASH, a Capability-Aware Subsidy Harness for ASA. CASH pairs a knowledge-guided large language model (LLM) with a data-driven numerical response model, learns from realized outcomes where each is locally reliable across accounts and subsidy levels, and turns this reliability into explicit capability licenses that govern source selection, agent review and budget allocation. Its 
Expected Policy Coverage Gain
 (EPCG) directs a limited feedback budget toward evidence expected to expand high-value reliable coverage, and the resulting feedback further improves the numerical model and updates capability estimates. In a one-month online experiment with CASH serving 60% of a large-scale advertising platform’s traffic, CASH raises revenue per account by 15.35% and ADVV by 19.86% over Control at an essentially unchanged subsidy rate, with further gains in revenue quality, LTV and retention.

Keywords: 
Online advertising, advertiser subsidy allocation, LLM agents

1. 
Introduction

Figure 1
.

Online advertising platforms 
(
Yang et al., 2026
; 
Han et al., 2026
)
 connect vast numbers of users and advertisers, and their long-term growth depends on effective traffic matching, a thriving advertiser ecosystem and sustainable commercial revenue. Yet an advertiser’s competitiveness in impression-level auctions does not always reflect its business potential. Advertisers with high conversion potential may receive insufficient exposure due to cold start, sparse historical feedback or weak ranking competitiveness, hindering their growth and long-term retention 
(
Ye et al., 2023
)
. Platforms therefore provide differentiated account-level support by raising ranking bids through an uplift factor while discounting billing, helping promising advertisers become more competitive and obtain additional traffic. Advertisers, however, respond very differently to the same support. Excessive subsidies increase platform cost without commensurate revenue gains, whereas insufficient subsidies constrain the growth of promising advertisers. Efficiently allocating a limited subsidy budget is therefore a central challenge in advertising operations.

In industrial systems, account-level subsidies are configured mainly through manual rules and operator experience, such as adjusting discounts during promotions or helping new advertisers ramp up. These practices cannot capture each account’s marginal revenue response at different subsidy levels. For example, when a 30% discount already secures sufficient traffic, deepening it to 50% may add little revenue while substantially increasing subsidy cost. Subsidies also affect competition for traffic, motivating a platform-level allocation objective. We formulate this problem as 
Advertiser Subsidy Allocation
 (ASA), which selects a subsidy level for each account to maximize total platform revenue under a platform-wide subsidy-rate constraint. ASA operates at the account level and shapes subsequent traffic allocation through the existing auction mechanism.

Large language models (LLMs) 
(
Zhao et al., 2026
; 
Brown et al., 2020
)
 offer a natural way to scale subsidy decisions by incorporating business rules, account operating information and operational knowledge into revenue-response estimation. Their estimates, however, do not guarantee numerical accuracy for specific subsidy actions 
(
Tan et al., 2024
; 
Song et al., 2026
)
. Data-driven numerical response models provide an alternative, learning directly from realized outcomes and predicting efficiently where historical feedback is abundant. Yet subsidies concentrate on a small number of accounts and levels, leaving many account-action combinations poorly supported. Neither estimator is uniformly reliable; their strengths depend on account states, subsidy levels and available evidence, as shown in Figure 
1
.

The central challenge is to make heterogeneous estimators collaborate reliably while allowing their usable decision range to evolve with experience. In ASA, an error at a single account-action pair can alter the allocation of a shared budget and consequently change decisions for other accounts. Meanwhile, the evidence available to assess reliability is shaped by previous allocation decisions, so poorly supported actions may remain difficult to evaluate. Reliable allocation thus requires both outcome-grounded limits on where each estimator can be used and a principled way to acquire evidence that expands these limits over time.

We propose 
CASH
 (Capability-Aware Subsidy Harness), which integrates estimator collaboration, reliability control and shared-budget allocation through explicit capability boundaries, as shown in Figure 
2
. CASH pairs an LLM-based prediction agent with a numerical response model to estimate account-level revenue curves. A capability plugin evaluates both sources using realized outcomes and issues per-level licenses for individual account states. These licenses constrain routine source selection and the candidate actions available to the shared-budget allocator. A separate LLM-based orchestrating agent reviews contested accounts using the predictions, capability report and operating information, choosing among licensed sources or withholding the subsidy. As new feedback arrives, CASH updates these licenses and adjusts the division of labor between the prediction agent and the numerical model.

CASH further introduces 
Expected Policy Coverage Gain
 (EPCG) to manage how these capability boundaries evolve. Because only executed actions yield feedback, historical logs leave gaps in the evidence needed to assess alternative decisions. EPCG allocates a limited feedback quota according to each region’s expected contribution to future value-weighted reliable coverage of the numerical model, measured through changes in regional admission while holding other license conditions fixed. Acquired feedback supports model retraining and capability reassessment, and CASH updates or revokes licenses as evidence changes, while EPCG prioritizes the interventions expected to expand reliable Tool coverage. EPCG thereby connects evidence acquisition to the same capability boundaries that govern current allocation, closing the loop between decision making and capability evolution.

Our main contributions are as follows.

•

Problem formulation.
 To our knowledge, we are the first to study advertiser subsidy allocation from an advertising operations perspective, formulating it as account-level optimization under a platform-wide subsidy-rate constraint. We identify its core challenges: heterogeneous estimator reliability across accounts and subsidy levels, and cross-account error propagation through the shared budget.

•

Capability-aware harness.
 We propose CASH, which learns outcome-grounded, per-level capability licenses for a prediction agent and a numerical response model, and uses these licenses to govern source selection and shared-budget allocation. EPCG directs limited feedback toward regions with the greatest expected expansion of reliable coverage, closing the loop between evidence acquisition and capability evolution.

•

Industrial validation.
 In a one-month online experiment with CASH serving 60% of platform traffic, CASH increases revenue per account by 15.35% and ADVV by 19.86% over Control at an essentially unchanged subsidy rate, while improving revenue quality, LTV, and retention.

4. 
Experiments

We evaluate CASH through six research questions.

• 
RQ1: Online impact.
 How does CASH perform relative to the incumbent policy online?

• 
RQ2: Prediction accuracy.
 How does CASH compare with individual estimators and alternative combinations?

• 
RQ3: Capability reliability.
 Are the estimators complementary, and do capability estimates identify reliable predictions and improve review by the orchestrating agent?

• 
RQ4: Call selection and licenses.
 What are the effects of call selection and capability licenses on accuracy and inference cost?

• 
RQ5: Budget coupling.
 How do local estimation errors propagate through the shared budget?

• 
RQ6: Capability evolution.
 Does EPCG expand reliable coverage and improve subsequent predictions?

4.1. 
Setup

4.1.1. 
Online experiment

CASH, Tool and Control serve 60%, 20% and 20% of platform traffic, respectively, with group assignment based on hashed account IDs. A pre-experiment A/A test verified that the groups were comparable in revenue per account, ADVV and subsidy rate. Control retains the incumbent tier-based policy guided by operator experience. Tool combines the numerical model and budget allocator. CASH’s full harness ran in production for one month from September 14 to October 14, 2026.

4.1.2. 
Offline data and evaluation cohort

We construct a dataset of 663,312 account-days from real advertising logs. Each sample contains the account history available before the target period, the executed discount factor and the realized discounted revenue in that period. The chronological training, validation and test splits contain 554,724, 54,851 and 53,737 account-days. Validation data serve model selection, error calibration and threshold setting. The 16 candidate discount factors range from 0.50 to 0.80, in addition to the no-discount action 
. For offline method comparisons involving LLMs, we use a common subset of 20,143 account-days from 11,314 accounts across two test periods, evaluating all methods at historically executed subsidy levels.

4.1.3. 
Compared methods

Tool uses the numerical response model alone and, online, pairs it with the budget allocator. Agent uses the LLM-based prediction agent alone. Agent Routing lets an LLM select between the two predictions, following the LLM-as-a-judge paradigm 
(
Zheng et al., 2023
)
. Judge Routing uses a learned source selector without capability constraints, following RouteLLM 
(
Ong et al., 2025
)
. CASH combines selective LLM calls with capability-constrained routing and allocation, and CASH (all calls) obtains both predictions and invokes the orchestrating agent for every account. Implementation details are in Appendix 
B
.

4.1.4. 
Metrics and protocol

Revenue, ADVV and LTV are reported per account using all advertisers in each group as the denominator. Revenue is post-discount platform revenue, ADVV is the advertiser value generated by acquired traffic, and LTV is cumulative revenue over 7 and 14 days. Target-achieving share is the fraction of revenue from accounts meeting their optimization targets, ineffective share is the fraction of revenue without conversion, and retention is the proportion of accounts active on days 7 and 14. Online results use Control as the reference unless otherwise stated. Gains in Revenue, ADVV and LTV are relative changes, while changes in revenue shares and retention are reported in percentage points. Offline, MAE and RMSE measure revenue prediction error at executed levels. CASH uses the Tool prediction for scoring when it takes 
Fallback
 or the executed level is outside the selected source’s license. A severe exceedance is an absolute error above twice the account’s pre-prediction historical revenue scale. Cost counts every underlying LLM request, including prediction, audit and orchestration calls. Offline paired comparisons use a 1,000-replicate account-clustered bootstrap for 95% confidence intervals, RQ6 applies Holm correction for multiple comparisons, and Appendix 
D.1
 details the daily-aggregate analysis of online temporal uncertainty.

Related Work

5.1. 
Budget-Constrained Incentive Allocation

Incentive allocation requires estimating heterogeneous responses and coordinating decisions under a shared budget. Uplift models estimate treatment effects 
(
Zhao et al., 2017
; 
Zhong et al., 2022
)
, while budget-aware allocation methods connect response estimation to constrained optimization 
(
Zhao et al., 2019
; 
Ai et al., 2022
)
. More recent approaches tighten this connection through end-to-end optimization or decision-oriented causal learning 
(
Sun et al., 2024
; 
Zhou et al., 2023
)
. In advertising, related work studies budget-constrained bidding 
(
Wu et al., 2018
)
 and coupon mechanisms for advertiser participation and retention 
(
Shen et al., 2021
; 
Xing et al., 2025
)
. CASH addresses a complementary issue: which response estimates should be allowed to influence allocation when historical interventions provide uneven support. Its account- and level-specific licenses restrict the estimates used by the shared-budget optimizer, while targeted feedback updates these restrictions over time.

5.2. 
Capability-Aware LLM Agents

LLM agents combine reasoning with external tools 
(
Yao et al., 2023
; 
Schick et al., 2023
)
, including for bidding and advertising budget allocation 
(
Cai et al., 2025
; 
Song et al., 2026
)
. Their use as numerical estimators 
(
Gruver et al., 2023
)
 also raises questions about calibration and the reliability of confidence signals 
(

;

;

;

)
. Learning to defer and selective prediction study when to rely on a predictor or seek assistance 
(
Mozannar and Sontag, 2020
; 
Charusaie et al., 2022
; 
Geifman and El-Yaniv, 2017
)
; LLM routing and tool-use policies balance predictive quality against inference cost 
(
Chen et al., 2023
; 
Ding et al., 2024
; 
Ong et al., 2025
; 
Qian et al., 2025
)
. CASH makes a finer distinction between selecting a source and determining the actions on which that source may be used. Outcome-based licenses constrain both the orchestrating agent’s source choice and the subsequent allocation across accounts. This design also relates to uncertainty-aware decision-making and active evidence acquisition. Conformal prediction calibrates uncertainty, including for downstream decisions 
(
Lei et al., 2017
;

6. 
Conclusion

We studied Advertiser Subsidy Allocation, which turns experience-driven subsidy configuration into automated account-level decisions under a platform-wide subsidy-rate constraint. The central challenge is to combine estimators whose reliability varies across accounts and subsidy levels while controlling how estimation errors affect shared-budget allocation. CASH combines an LLM-based prediction agent with a numerical response model, learns per-level capability licenses for both sources from realized outcomes, and uses these licenses to constrain source selection, review by the orchestrating agent and shared-budget allocation. EPCG prioritizes feedback by its expected contribution to reliable Tool coverage. Offline evaluations show that licenses reduce tail prediction errors and block propagation from unlicensed estimates, while EPCG expands reliable coverage more effectively than random acquisition. In a one-month online experiment with CASH serving 60% of platform traffic, CASH raises revenue per account by 15.35% and ADVV by 19.86% over Control at an essentially unchanged subsidy rate. It further increases revenue per account by 6.31% over automated allocation with the numerical model alone. These results demonstrate the practical value of combining operational knowledge with data-driven prediction within outcome-grounded capability boundaries for fine-grained advertiser subsidy allocation, improving platform revenue and advertiser outcomes under a shared budget.

D.2.4. 
Acquisition baselines

In an independent masked replay, the average Spearman correlation between the predicted regional gain and the realized coverage gain is 0.384 for EPCG, 0.352 for value-first, 0.134 for boundary-first and 0.078 for uncertainty-first acquisition; value-first is better on some slices. On the replay of RQ6, the mean predicted coverage gain is 1.31 points against a realized 1.94 points. Removing the value weight from EPCG leaves 12 of the 15 combinations unchanged.

D.2.5. 
Routing and solver statistics

In CASH (all calls), the orchestrating agent selects Tool for 34.76% and Agent for 65.24% of account-days. In each evaluation period, the LP has one fractional account, and repair changes at most one account. Final allocations meet the predicted subsidy-rate constraint.
