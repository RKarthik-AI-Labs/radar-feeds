Abstract

GUI agents can formulate a correct immediate plan in their reasoning
yet emit an action that fails to execute it, creating an

execution gap
.
This recurring mismatch undermines reliable task completion, as a
single execution error can derail a multi-step workflow.
In outcome-supervised reinforcement learning, broadcasting a
negative response-level advantage across all generated tokens risks
penalizing valid planning together with erroneous execution.
To address this credit-assignment conflict, we introduce

PACT
 (
P
lan–
A
ction 
C
onsistency

T
raining), a training framework that combines
reference-aligned plan protection and plan-aligned action repair
without altering the original scalar action reward.
Using annotated reference actions to identify execution gaps, PACT
protects the verified plan by masking negative policy-gradient
contributions across its token span. Keeping the generated reasoning
unchanged as context, it then favors the reference action over the
emitted erroneous action through a preference surrogate with
stop-gradient pair weights.
Compared with the outcome-only baseline, PACT raises
AndroidWorld task success from 60.34% to 
68.97%

(
+8.63 pp
), while improving
rule-based plan–action consistency on AndroidControl High from
83.71% to 
89.74%
 (
+6.03 pp
).
Adapting PACT to desktop agents also improves task success from
40.92% to 
44.17%
 (
+3.25 pp
) on the full 369-task
OSWorld benchmark.
Together, these results establish PACT as an effective credit-assignment
framework for bridging execution gaps and improving task completion
across mobile and desktop GUI environments.

1 
Introduction

GUI agents must translate visual interfaces and user goals into executable interactions. Yet formulating a correct immediate plan in generated reasoning does not guarantee that the emitted action executes it. For example, an agent may state that it will tap a visible 
+
 button, yet emit an application-launch command instead (Figure 
1
). This 
execution gap
 is distinct from a planning error: the response already contains a valid immediate plan, but the final action fails to realize it. Building on prior diagnostic studies of reasoning–execution gaps in mobile agents 
[
5
]
, we investigate how training can preserve valid plans within failed responses while repairing their execution.

In reinforcement learning (RL), such execution gaps create a credit-assignment problem. Several GUI-agent methods use Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) or its variants to learn from action feedback 
[
7
, 
9
]
. In implementations that broadcast one response-level advantage across reasoning and action tokens 
[
17
]
, a negative advantage penalizes valid planning alongside erroneous execution. A scalar thought–action consistency reward 
[
9
]
 does not determine the direction of correction: higher agreement can result from adapting a plan to justify an incorrect action, and a flawed plan can be executed consistently. Effective correction therefore requires identifying which component the annotated reference action supports preserving.

Our central principle is to 
preserve a reference-supported plan and repair the action that fails to implement it
. Here, a plan denotes the intended next GUI interaction expressed in the generated reasoning, and the plan span denotes the tokens expressing it. Verifying reference support for parameterized GUI actions requires checking spatial or textual arguments beyond action type alone, enabling selective credit assignment that shields valid plans while repairing failed actions.

We present 
PACT
, which instantiates this principle within a group-relative RL pipeline while leaving the original scalar action reward intact. First, a conservative rule-based selector identifies responses featuring a reference-supported plan but an incorrect, inconsistent final action. Second, 
reference-aligned plan protection
 masks negative policy-gradient contributions within the plan span, leaving the advantages on action tokens and the remaining reasoning unchanged. Third, 
plan-aligned action repair
 pairs a reference-derived positive action suffix with the model’s emitted erroneous suffix under the same decoded reasoning trace via a stop-gradient pair-weight preference surrogate in the spirit of direct preference optimization (DPO) 
[
15
]
, outside the original GRPO rollout group. Both mechanisms operate strictly during training; inference 
retains
 the standard single-pass reasoning–action interface without extra reflection or re-planning.

PACT improves interactive task completion over the original GRPO baseline, reaching 68.97% success on AndroidWorld 
[
16
]
 (+8.63 pp) and 44.17% on the full OSWorld benchmark 
[
21
]
 (+3.25 pp). It also achieves 89.74% rule-based plan–action consistency on AndroidControl High 
[
12
]
 (+6.03 pp), alongside improved visual grounding on ScreenSpot-v2 Mobile 
[
20
]
.

Three complementary diagnostics examine why these gains arise. Fixed-plan continuation isolates action generation, showing that plan-aligned action repair improves execution of a valid plan. Aligned-state retention analyses demonstrate that reference-aligned plan protection reduces regressions on baseline-correct actions. Free-generation comparisons further show that protecting plans preserves more valid plans and enables more joint repairs, supporting the complementary roles of protection and repair beyond surface plan–action agreement.

Our main contributions are threefold:

•

Reference-aligned plan protection and plan-aligned action repair.
 PACT uses reference-supported plans to decouple plan preservation from action correction without altering the scalar action reward.

•

Task improvements across mobile and desktop environments.
 Comparisons against outcome-only and two repair baselines demonstrate consistent gains in dynamic task completion on AndroidWorld and OSWorld, supported by consistency and grounding evaluations on AndroidControl and ScreenSpot-v2 Mobile.

•

Mechanistic evidence on repair and retention.
 Through fixed-plan continuation, free-generation, and baseline-correct retention analyses, we characterize the repair–retention trade-off while disentangling plan correctness, action validity, and plan–action consistency.

Related Work

Reasoning–execution alignment in GUI agents.

Reliable GUI interaction demands both sound planning and faithful
execution, motivating fine-grained evaluation beyond aggregate task success.
VenusBench-Mobile 
[
6
]
 decomposes user-centric
agent evaluation across perception, understanding, decision, action,
and memory capabilities, while Dong et al. 
[
5
]

specifically diagnose reasoning–execution gaps by separating the
correctness of stated plans from that of emitted actions.
On the training side, Faithful-Agent 
[
9
]
 combines
evidence-grounded supervision, thought–action consistency rewards,
and a guided advantage estimator, whereas MobileIPL 
[
10
]

derives thinking-level preferences by propagating action feedback
through reasoning trees to improve planning.
In contrast, PACT uses reference-supported plans within
unsuccessful responses to determine the direction of correction—preserving
valid plans while repairing only their failed execution.

Fine-grained preference learning and action correction.

Building on the pairwise objective of DPO 
[
15
]
,
Step-DPO 
[
11
]
 targets individual reasoning steps
conditioned on a correct prefix, whereas Agentic-DPO 
[
2
]

contrasts expert actions against sampled mistakes at expert states.
From a rollout-augmentation perspective, Octopus 
[
4
]

recombines sampled trajectories and masks specific segments to
separate self-correction from direct reasoning.
PACT instead retains the model’s own decoded reasoning trace as the shared
conditioning context to contrast the reference-derived action suffix
against the emitted erroneous suffix.

Credit assignment within failed responses.

Localized supervision also raises a credit-assignment question: which
parts of an unsuccessful response should be penalized?
In standard GRPO 
[
17
]
, a single group-relative
advantage is broadcast across every token of a sampled response.
Recent methods refine this assignment based on intermediate
correctness, ranging from retrieval-calibrated negative advantages in
CalibAdv 
[
19
]
 to teacher-identified first-error
boundaries in Cliff 
[
8
]
.
In GUI agent training, UI-Venus-2 
[
18
]
 introduces
structured action-aware distillation to condition token-level
supervision on action correctness and distinguish action-type errors
from parameter errors.
Within outcome-supervised GRPO, PACT masks negative policy-gradient
contributions on reference-supported GUI plan spans and repairs
failed action suffixes under the same plan, while keeping the scalar
action reward intact.

Generation and measurement.

Mobile evaluation uses greedy decoding (temperature 
), a
4,096-token output limit, and up to ten prior interaction steps. On
AndroidControl, models observe recorded screenshots, with non-initial
states conditioned on each model’s own generated history. OSWorld uses
the same environment configuration, greedy decoding (temperature 
),
a 100-step limit, and the official native evaluator.
Distinct from the training-time selector, our AndroidControl consistency
evaluator extracts a function-style or keyword-based action intent from
the reasoning trace and compares the inferred action type and recoverable
parameters with those of the emitted action. We report

with consistent and judgeable counts provided in the supplementary material.
For action retention, we measure the proportion of O’s reference-matched
states on which a compared variant also matches the reference.

In mechanism diagnostics, fixed-plan continuation conditions each model
on the same input and the exact valid reasoning prefix generated by O to isolate action
generation, whereas free generation requires each model to produce both
reasoning and action from scratch. On these subsets,
screenshot-based semantic assessments use language-model assistance
and human verification to separately evaluate plan correctness (

action validity (

complementing benchmark-wide rule-based scores; the supplementary

7 
Discussion and Limitations

Why plan protection complements action repair.

Across multi-step benchmarks, the complete PACT framework achieves
substantially higher task success on AndroidWorld and the highest task
success on OSWorld within the matched comparisons of
Table 
3
(B),
while preserving more baseline-correct actions across aligned states
in Table 
4
, even though OAR and OA match or approach PACT on
isolated single-step grounding in Table 
3
(B)
and fixed-plan continuation in Table 
5
. These results clarify
the complementary roles of our two mechanisms: plan-aligned action repair teaches
the policy to translate a valid plan into the intended action,
whereas reference-aligned plan protection shields that plan from negative
policy-gradient updates on failed rollouts. Because each action in
closed-loop interaction alters the context for subsequent turns,
retaining mastered behaviors is as critical as recovering from errors,
enabling PACT to drive stronger end-to-end task completion across mobile
and desktop environments.

Limitations and future directions.

First, our training-time selector requires explicit plans whose
parameters can be verified against a single annotated reference.
Consequently, valid but implicit plans or semantically equivalent
alternative actions may fall outside the eligible set; incorporating
semantic plan verifiers or multi-reference supervision could
broaden the coverage of repairable failures.
Second, whereas our OSWorld experiments establish task-level gains on
desktop workflows, our fine-grained plan and fixed-plan
continuation diagnostics focus on mobile states. Extending these
mechanistic analyses to richer desktop action spaces—such as keyboard
shortcuts, composite operations, and long-horizon workflows—will
clarify how both mechanisms interact in complex environments.

8 
Conclusion

We presented 
PACT
, a training framework that couples

reference-aligned plan protection
 with 
plan-aligned action repair

to resolve GUI execution gaps without altering the original scalar
action reward. It shields reference-supported plan spans from
negative policy-gradient updates while optimizing corrected action
suffixes under the same decoded reasoning trace. Compared with outcome-only
RL, PACT substantially improves AndroidWorld task success and achieves
higher task success on OSWorld, alongside mobile plan–action consistency
and visual grounding. Aligned-state retention analyses further show
that PACT preserves a higher proportion of baseline-correct actions
than unprotected repair variants. Moreover, fixed-plan continuation and
free-generation diagnostics confirm the complementary roles of both
mechanisms while distinguishing true action correctness from
surface-level plan–action agreement. Together, these findings
establish a correctness-guided credit-assignment principle for GUI
agents: preserve valid plans and selectively repair their
execution, rather than optimizing consistency alone.
