Abstract.

Re-ranking aims to present users with an ordered list that maximizes sequence-level utility.
Industrial deployments widely adopt the Generator–Evaluator framework, where the generator proposes candidate sequences, and the evaluator scores them to select the best one for display.
The fidelity of the evaluator’s utility estimates thus directly governs recommendation quality.
However, existing evaluators predominantly follow the 
Independent Behavior Modeling (IBM)
 paradigm, which simplifies the continuous user 
browsing journey
 into independent per-item predictions (e.g., CTR).
This oversimplification ignores two physical realities:
(i) a click can occur only after the item has been reached and impressed,
and (ii) items compete for finite user attention.
As a result, IBM produces systematically biased evaluations and suboptimal sequence selection.
To address this, we propose a 
Joint Behavior Modeling (JBM)
 perspective that explicitly models the 
display
 
 
impression
 
 
click
 process to capture causal and competitive dependencies within a sequence.
Building on JBM, we develop the 
Impression-Click Evaluator (ICE)
, a plug-and-play framework that
(i) predicts a browsing-depth distribution to estimate per-position impression probabilities,
(ii) leverages predicted impressions to suppress spurious context from unexposed items during click modeling,
and (iii) introduces a click distribution matching objective to capture intra-sequence dependencies.
Extensive offline experiments across diverse backbones, together with a 14-day online A/B test in the Taobao App (+0.70% clicks, +3.67% revenue) and full-traffic deployment, validate the effectiveness and robustness of our approach.

Introduction

Modern industrial recommender systems 
(

;

;

 typically follow a multi-stage cascade architecture: retrieval

;

;

;

)
, ranking 
(

;

(

;

;

; 
Bello et al., 2018
)
.
As the final decision stage, re-ranking directly determines the ordered list displayed to users and must account for complex sequence-level effects such as inter-item interactions, rather than evaluating items in isolation.
To this end, industrial practice has converged on the Generator–Evaluator (G–E) framework 
(
Feng et al., 2021a
; 
Xi et al., 2021
; 
Yang et al., 2025
; 
Wang et al., 2025
)
, where the generator proposes multiple candidate sequences and the evaluator estimates their sequence-level utility to select the optimal one for display.

Accordingly, the performance of the G–E framework is fundamentally bounded by the evaluator’s ability to perform reliable sequence-level evaluation 
(
Zhang et al., 2025
)
.
Sequence-level utility is not a static property intrinsic to items; rather, it is dynamically realized through the user 
browsing journey
 along the displayed sequence.
However, as shown in Fig. 
1
(A), existing evaluators predominantly follow the 
I
ndependent 
B
ehavior 
M
odeling (
IBM
) paradigm, which approximates the overall sequence response as a collection of independent per-item interactions (e.g., clicks).
Such oversimplification ignores two fundamental characteristics of real-world user behavior.

Comparison of modeling paradigms for the

First, there exists a universal 
display-to-impression gap
 (
C1
) driven by browsing drop-off.
While the system displays a fixed-length sequence (e.g., 20 items), users often disengage early due to dwindling attention or unfavorable content, leaving tail items unseen.
This gap impairs the evaluator in two ways:
(i) it systematically overestimates tail-item utility, favoring sequences that look promising offline but fail to deliver online gains;
(ii) it introduces spurious context, as unexposed items are mistakenly treated as valid neighbors for those actually seen, contaminating the contextual representations and degrading evaluation reliability.

Second, user interactions exhibit complex 
intra-sequence behavioral dependencies
 (
C2
).
On one hand, a 
causal
 dependency arises as any interaction is strictly predicated on the item being successfully reached and impressed.
On the other hand, items compete for a user’s finite attention and intent satisfaction, leading to a 
competitive
 dependency:
once a user clicks an item and finds it satisfactory, the marginal utility of subsequent similar items drops significantly even if their individual quality is high.
Our empirical analysis (Fig. 
4
) confirms that IBM-style models struggle in such “multi-click” scenarios, as they treat clicks as isolated events and fail to capture how items 
shadow
 each other across the sequence.

In this work, we focus exclusively on advancing the evaluator design
, taking the generator as given.
To address the above issues, we propose the 
J
oint 
B
ehavior 
M
odeling (
JBM
) paradigm.
As illustrated in Fig. 
1
(B), JBM reformulates the sequence-level evaluation by explicitly estimating impression probabilities to bridge the display-to-impression gap and modeling the joint distribution of interactions to capture intra-sequence behavioral dependencies.
Building on JBM, we develop 
ICE
 (
I
mpression-
C
lick 
E
valuator), a plug-and-play framework with three core components:
(i) 
browsing-depth modeling
 predicts positional impression (exposure) probabilities to capture early disengagement;
(ii) 
impression-aware dynamic masking
 suppresses context noise from unexposed items during click modeling;
(iii) 
click distribution matching
 captures competitive click patterns within a sequence.
Extensive experiments on a large-scale industrial dataset, together with comprehensive ablation and rationality analyses, consistently validate ICE’s effectiveness across diverse evaluator backbones.
A 14-day online A/B test in the Taobao App further yields 
+0.70%
 clicks and 
+3.67%
 commercial revenue, leading to successful large-scale full-traffic deployment for hundreds of millions of daily users.

The main contributions of this work are summarized as follows:

•

We formally identify the systematic bias in current sequence-level evaluation and propose 
JBM
 as a principled paradigm to ensure physical consistency and capture behavioral dependencies along the browsing journey.

•

We develop 
ICE
, a backbone-agnostic, plug-and-play solution under the JBM paradigm that seamlessly enhances diverse industrial evaluator architectures.

•

We validate JBM and ICE through comprehensive offline and online evaluations, with successful full-traffic deployment further demonstrating their real-world impact.

Evaluator Design.

Within G–E, prior work has predominantly focused on the generator 
(

;

Existing evaluators, despite diverse architectures (e.g., RNNs

Impression Modeling.

Exposure bias has been widely studied at the ranking stage

Mansoury et al., 2024
)
, but remain under-explored at the sequence level.
A recent work 
(
Zhang et al., 2025
)
 models personalized consumption boundaries to mitigate estimation biases, yet still relies on factorized per-item modeling and ignores the competitive nature of user interactions.
In contrast, our JBM paradigm jointly models the entire 
display
 
 
impression
 
 
click

5.5. 
Online Experiment (Q4)

We deploy ICE in the 
Guess You Like
 feed of the Taobao App, a flagship recommendation scenario serving hundreds of millions of daily users.
The production system follows a G–E re-ranking framework with a Transformer-based baseline evaluator.
We integrate ICE into this evaluator while keeping the generator fixed.

We conduct an online A/B test with 5% traffic allocation over 14 days, tracking core metrics alongside 14-day Lifetime Value (LT-14d) as a guardrail metric to monitor user retention.
Results show that ICE improves impressions by 
+0.58%
, clicks by 
+0.70%
, and commercial revenue by 
+3.67%
.
Notably, LT-14d also increases by 
+0.23%
, indicating that the gains are sustained without compromising the long-term user experience.
Regarding efficiency, the average response time (RT) increases by less than 0.5ms, which is negligible compared to the total system latency.
Given these consistent improvements, ICE has been fully deployed to the online system, validating the JBM paradigm at industrial scale.

6. 
Conclusion

This paper introduces the JBM paradigm for evaluator learning in G–E re-ranking.
Unlike the prevailing IBM that performs independent item-level predictions, JBM explicitly models how sequence utility is realized through interdependent user behaviors.
We instantiate JBM with ICE, a plug-and-play framework combining browsing-depth impression modeling, impression-aware masking (C1), and sequence-level click distribution matching (C2).
Across six evaluator backbones, ICE consistently improves impression, click, and sequence-level evaluation.
A 14-day Taobao A/B test further yields +0.70% clicks and +3.67% revenue with negligible latency overhead, followed by full-traffic deployment.
Future work will extend JBM to richer behavioral signals (e.g., dwell time) and leverage ICE as a reward signal for generative re-rankers.
